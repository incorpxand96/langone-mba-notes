# Delivery and Presence

The course treats delivery as a combination of:

- visual elements;
- vocal elements;
- verbal elements.

The goal is not perfect performance.

Engaging speakers need:

- physical and vocal presence;
- authenticity;
- flexibility;
- focus;
- ability to handle the unexpected.

---

# Visual Elements

## Eye Connection

Use eye contact to connect with real people rather than scanning mechanically.

For large groups, focus on specific sections or individuals.

## Facial Expression

Expression should fit the message.

## Stance / Posture

Aim for:

- stability;
- comfort;
- alignment.

Movement should be intentional rather than nervous.

## Gestures

Use hands strategically for:

- illustration;
- emphasis.

Avoid repetitive self-soothing movements that distract.

## Movement

Move when it supports a transition or interaction.

Do not pace without purpose.

---

# Vocal Elements

- volume;
- pace;
- tone;
- enunciation;
- emphasis;
- inflection.

Pauses matter.

Silence allows the message to land and can replace filler words.

---

# Verbal Elements

Reduce distracting fillers such as:

- um;
- like;
- you know;
- ah.

A useful tactic:

> **Pause where the filler would normally appear.**

---

# Camera Grammar

The course distinguishes several camera frames.

## Head Only

Emphasize:

- direct camera connection;
- facial expression.

## Waist Up

Add modest gestures close to the body.

## Full Body

Use:

- stable stance;
- prescribed gesture zone;
- full visual presence.

---

# Congruence

Words, tone, and body should reinforce rather than contradict one another.

A confident recommendation delivered with:

- low volume;
- collapsing posture;
- uncertain inflection

can weaken the message.

---

# My Take

Presence is not theatricality.

It is the degree to which the speaker's physical and vocal behavior makes the message:

- easier to follow;
- more credible;
- more human.

The best delivery often feels conversational even when thoroughly prepared.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Be stable before trying to be dynamic.
- Use eye connection, not scanning.
- Pause instead of filling silence.
- Emphasize important words.
- Move intentionally.
- Keep non-speakers visibly attentive.
- Rehearse enough to become flexible, not robotic.
- Make delivery congruent with the message.
