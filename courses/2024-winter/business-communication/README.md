# Business Communication

This folder is my working knowledge base for **MCOM-GB 2105.33: Business Communication**, taught by **Professor Jenn Wynn** at NYU Stern.

The course focuses on communication strategy, audience analysis, credibility, message structure, delivery, writing, visuals, Q&A, impromptu speaking, and feedback.

The most useful umbrella idea is:

> **Good business communication is designed backward from the result I want from a specific audience.**

The course's recurring framework is **AIM**:

- **Audience** - attention;
- **Intent** - influence;
- **Message** - memory.

Everything else - structure, delivery, visuals, writing style, Q&A - should support that strategic core.

# My Current Communication Principles

1. **Start with the audience, not the content.**
2. **Define the result before drafting.**
3. **Reduce the message to one Big Idea.**
4. **Put the main point early.**
5. **Use the audience's logic, not the writer's logic.**
6. **Make structure visible.**
7. **Use both logical and emotional appeals when influence is the goal.**
8. **Chunk information so attention can reset.**
9. **End with a clear action or next step.**
10. **Use Plain English wherever possible.**
11. **Design written communication for skimming.**
12. **Treat subject lines and slide titles as messages, not labels.**
13. **A slide should have one primary "so what?"**
14. **Visuals should reinforce the verbal message, not compete with it.**
15. **Delivery should feel conversational and credible rather than over-rehearsed.**
16. **Pause instead of filling silence.**
17. **Prepare for questions as seriously as the scripted presentation.**
18. **Paraphrase and reframe questions when useful.**
19. **If I do not know, do not bluff.**
20. **Impromptu speaking improves through preparation, not stream of consciousness.**
21. **Teams should divide responsibility without fragmenting the message.**
22. **Feedback should describe observable behavior rather than attack identity.**
23. **Review strengths before weaknesses.**
24. **Choose two or three concrete behaviors to improve at a time.**
25. **Clarity is a form of respect for the audience.**

# Communication Quality in One Page

The course rubrics consistently evaluate communication across eight dimensions:

## Strategy

- Is the objective clear?
- Is the communication designed for this audience?
- Is there a clear desired result?

## Structure

- Is the main point early?
- Is there a preview?
- Does the logic follow the preview?
- Do supporting points support the Big Idea?

## Content

- Is information relevant?
- Are recommendations specific?
- Is evidence sufficient?
- Does the communication persuade or inform as intended?

## Style

- Is the language concise?
- Is Plain English used?
- Is tone appropriate?
- Is active voice used where useful?

## Format / Visuals

- Is the subject line or title informative?
- Is the document easy to skim?
- Are visuals readable?
- Does design reinforce the message?

## Delivery

- Does the speaker engage the audience?
- Do visual, vocal, and verbal signals align?
- Does the speaker sound credible and conversational?

## Teamwork

- Is the team prepared?
- Are transitions smooth?
- Is responsibility shared?
- Do non-speakers remain attentive?

## Interaction

- Is Q&A controlled without becoming defensive?
- Are questions clarified or reframed when needed?
- Does the response bridge back to the message?
- Does the speaker admit uncertainty appropriately?

That consolidated framework is developed further in [Communication Success Criteria](communication-success-criteria.md).
