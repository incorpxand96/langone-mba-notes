# Audience Questions and Q&A

The course treats Q&A as part of the communication strategy, not as an afterthought.

Questions create an opportunity to:

- address concerns;
- demonstrate credibility;
- reinforce the message.

---

# General Q&A Practices

- Open the session and wait.
- Keep the full audience involved.
- Make sure responsibility is shared across team members.
- Paraphrase when useful.
- Respond briefly.
- Bridge back to the message.
- Control emotion.
- Do not bluff.

Do not address only the asker.

The answer is still part of the presentation to the room.

---

# Seven Question Types

## 1. Global

Very broad question.

### Response

- narrow scope;
- seek clarification;
- return to the core message.

## 2. Non-Question

A comment disguised as participation.

### Response

- pick up a useful keyword or phrase;
- connect it to the topic.

Avoid:

> "So what's your question?"

## 3. Hypothetical

Asks what might happen.

### Response

- decline to speculate; or
- speculate while stating assumptions and limits.

## 4. Forced Choice

Presents an artificial either/or.

### Response

- listen for the "or";
- reframe;
- answer one, both, or neither.

## 5. Empty Chair

Asks the speaker to represent someone who is absent.

### Response

- do not speak for another person unless authorized;
- state the perspective you can legitimately offer.

## 6. Loaded / Leading

Embeds emotion or an accusation.

### Response

- reframe;
- avoid repeating inflammatory wording;
- do not become defensive;
- answer in neutral language.

## 7. "Don't Know"

Asks for information I do not have.

### Response

- do not bluff;
- offer to find out;
- qualify any estimate.

---

# Bridging

A good response can answer the question and still return to the central message.

This is not evasion.

It is message discipline.

---

# Team Q&A

Teams should:

- choose a moderator where useful;
- distribute responses;
- stay attentive while others answer;
- avoid one person dominating;
- maintain shared credibility.

---

# My Take

Q&A is where scripted communication becomes adaptive.

The best preparation is not memorizing answers.

It is:

- understanding the audience;
- anticipating concerns;
- knowing the message;
- practicing ways to reframe and bridge.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Prepare likely questions.
- Listen fully before answering.
- Paraphrase complex questions.
- Reframe false choices.
- State assumptions when speculating.
- Neutralize loaded language.
- Admit when I do not know.
- Bridge back to the Big Idea.
- Keep answers concise.
- Share team responsibility.
