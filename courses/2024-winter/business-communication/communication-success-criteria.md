# Communication Success Criteria

This page consolidates the course rubrics into one reusable quality standard for business communication.

Use it before:

- sending an important email;
- presenting;
- pitching;
- circulating a deck;
- leading a Q&A.

---

# 1. Strategy

## Success Behaviors

- Objective is clear.
- Audience is explicitly considered.
- Desired result is known.
- Big Idea is easy to state.
- Communication adheres to the actual assignment or business need.

## Questions

- Who is the audience?
- What should they know, believe, or do afterward?
- Why should they care?
- What is the one sentence I want remembered?

---

# 2. Structure

## Success Behaviors

- Main information appears early.
- Opening contains a useful preview when needed.
- Organization follows a logical sequence.
- Supporting details support the Big Idea.
- Ending summarizes and gives next steps.

## Questions

- Is the lede buried?
- Does the preview match the body?
- Can the audience predict where I am going?
- Does the ending land on action?

---

# 3. Content

## Success Behaviors

- Information is relevant.
- Recommendations are clear and specific.
- Claims are supported with observations, research, facts, or reasoning.
- Content is organized to preserve attention.
- The communication is persuasive or informative in the way intended.

## Questions

- What can I delete?
- What evidence is actually needed?
- Have I answered the audience's likely questions?

---

# 4. Style

## Success Behaviors

- Plain English.
- Short, understandable sentences.
- Appropriate tone.
- Predominantly active voice where useful.
- Correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation.

## Questions

- Can a simpler word replace this one?
- Am I using jargon?
- Does the tone fit the relationship and situation?
- Is responsibility clear?

---

# 5. Format and Visual Design

## Success Behaviors

- Subject line or title is informative.
- Layout supports skimming.
- Slides are readable.
- Visuals reinforce verbal messages.
- Message titles tell the story.
- Design creates clarity rather than decoration.

## Questions

- Can the reader scan this quickly?
- Does each slide have one primary point?
- Are headings meaningful?
- Is anything too small to read?

---

# 6. Delivery

## Success Behaviors

- Speaker engages the audience.
- Speaker sounds credible and conversational.
- Eye connection supports the message.
- Volume, pace, tone, emphasis, and inflection are controlled.
- Posture and gestures are intentional.
- Fillers do not dominate.
- Speaker handles the unexpected.

## Questions

- Do I look and sound like I believe the message?
- Am I rushing?
- Can I pause?
- Are my gestures helping or distracting?

---

# 7. Teamwork

## Success Behaviors

- Team is prepared and professional.
- Message feels integrated.
- Transitions are planned.
- Visuals are consistent.
- Non-speakers remain attentive.
- Responsibility for Q&A is shared.

## Questions

- Does this feel like one presentation?
- Does everyone understand the whole argument?
- Who should answer which questions?

---

# 8. Audience Interaction

## Success Behaviors

- Questions are heard fully.
- Speaker paraphrases when useful.
- Responses are brief and relevant.
- Loaded language is reframed.
- Uncertainty is acknowledged honestly.
- Answers bridge back to the main message.

## Questions

- Am I answering the real concern?
- Am I becoming defensive?
- Do I know this, or am I guessing?
- What message should the audience retain after the answer?

---

# Pre-Send / Pre-Present Checklist

Before communicating, confirm:

- [ ] I know the audience.
- [ ] I know the desired result.
- [ ] I can state the Big Idea in one sentence.
- [ ] The main point is early.
- [ ] The logic is visible.
- [ ] Evidence is sufficient but not excessive.
- [ ] Language is plain and concise.
- [ ] Format is easy to skim.
- [ ] Visuals reinforce the message.
- [ ] The ending has a next step.
- [ ] I have anticipated likely questions.
- [ ] I know what I will say if I do not know.
- [ ] I have rehearsed the highest-risk parts.

---

# My Take

The rubrics reveal that communication quality is not mainly about polish.

It is the intersection of:

> **Strategy + clarity + evidence + usability + credibility + interaction**

A beautifully delivered presentation with no clear intent is weak.

A logically perfect email that no one can skim is weak.

A persuasive pitch that collapses in Q&A is incomplete.

The communication succeeds when the audience can:

- understand;
- remember;
- trust;
- act.
