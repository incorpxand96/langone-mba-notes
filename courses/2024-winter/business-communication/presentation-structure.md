# Presentation Structure

A clear presentation has a visible beginning, middle, and ending.

The course's structure is simple enough to reuse across almost any business presentation.

---

# Beginning

## Attention Grabber

Get attention.

The grabber should connect to the topic rather than exist as entertainment alone.

## Big Idea

State the core point.

Why are we here?

Why should the audience care?

## Preview

Tell the audience how the argument will unfold.

This creates a roadmap.

---

# Middle

Use a small number of supporting points.

For each:

- make the point;
- provide evidence;
- explain relevance;
- transition.

The course emphasizes **flow structure** so the audience always knows where it is.

---

# Ending

## Summary

Return to the Big Idea.

Reinforce the major supporting reasons.

## Call to Action

State:

- next step;
- decision;
- request;
- behavior.

A strong ending should **deposit the audience at the action**.

---

# Persuasive Structure

A reusable persuasive sequence:

1. Recommendation / Big Idea
2. Preview
3. Reason 1 + evidence
4. Reason 2 + evidence
5. Reason 3 + evidence
6. Close
7. Q&A
8. Final close

This puts the recommendation early instead of forcing the audience to infer it after a long buildup.

---

# Logical and Emotional Appeals

Influence can use both.

### Logical

- data;
- evidence;
- comparisons;
- reasoning.

### Emotional

- story;
- consequences;
- aspiration;
- human relevance.

The choice depends on the audience and purpose.

---

# My Take

Structure is not decoration.

It is a form of cognitive assistance.

The speaker knows the material already.

The audience does not.

A visible structure reduces the effort required to understand the argument.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- State the main point early.
- Preview the structure.
- Limit supporting points.
- Use transitions.
- Chunk information.
- Repeat the Big Idea at the end.
- End with action, not drift.
