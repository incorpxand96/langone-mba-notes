# Business Writing

The course applies the same strategic logic to writing as to speaking.

Strong business writing is:

- strategic;
- structured;
- readable;
- plain.

---

# AIM for Writing

Before drafting:

- **Audience** - who will read this?
- **Intent** - what result do I want?
- **Message** - what is the main point?

---

# Reader's Logic

Do not organize around the order in which I discovered the information.

Organize around the order in which the reader needs it.

The course emphasizes:

- state the main point up front;
- provide supporting evidence;
- anticipate reader questions;
- make logic easy to follow.

---

# Persuasive Writing

For recommendations:

1. Recommendation
2. Preview
3. Reason 1 + evidence
4. Reason 2 + evidence
5. Reason 3 + evidence
6. Close / next step

Do not bury the lede.

---

# Four-Step Writing Plan

## 1. Strategic Issues

Audience, Intent, Message.

## 2. Macro Issues

Document design and organization.

## 3. Micro Issues

Plain English and sentence-level style.

## 4. Correctness

Grammar and punctuation.

This sequence is useful because it prevents polishing sentences inside a document whose strategy is still wrong.

---

# High Skim Value

Increase readability with:

- white space;
- lists;
- parallel form;
- headings;
- topic sentences;
- selective typography.

The preview should accurately predict what follows.

Headings should capture the essence of the section, not merely label a topic.

---

# Plain English

The course emphasizes:

- everyday words;
- short sentences;
- active voice;
- personal pronouns;
- confident, courteous tone.

Examples:

- use, not utilize;
- because, not due to the fact that;
- analyze, not perform an analysis of;
- conclude, not reach a conclusion about.

---

# Active Voice

Active voice can:

- reduce wordiness;
- save time;
- clarify responsibility.

Passive voice still has legitimate uses when:

- the actor is unknown;
- the actor is unimportant;
- the writer intentionally de-emphasizes the actor.

---

# My Take

The strongest business writing is not "professional-sounding."

It is **easy for the intended reader to understand and act on**.

Complexity in the topic does not require complexity in the sentence.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Decide before drafting.
- Put the main point early.
- Use reader's logic.
- Make recommendations specific.
- Support claims with evidence.
- Use short sentences and paragraphs.
- Prefer active voice.
- Remove jargon and inflated phrasing.
- Design for skimming.
- Proofread after structure is settled.
