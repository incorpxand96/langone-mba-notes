# Team Presentations and Pitches

A team presentation should feel like **one communication delivered by several people**, not several mini-presentations placed next to each other.

---

# Preparation Sequence

1. Plan AIM together.
2. Organize the content.
3. Assign parts only after the structure is clear.
4. Rehearse as a group.
5. Plan transitions.
6. Design visuals consistently.
7. Prepare Q&A.
8. Stay attentive while teammates speak.

---

# Team Pitch Rubric

The course evaluates five dimensions.

## Structure

- logical order;
- supporting details reinforce main ideas.

## Content

- relevant information;
- organization maintains attention.

## Delivery

- presenters engage;
- presenters speak with conviction.

## Visuals

- readable;
- reinforce verbal messages.

## Teamwork

- prepared;
- professional;
- shared Q&A responsibility.

---

# Transitions

Use backward and forward references.

Examples:

- summarize what the prior speaker established;
- explain why the next section follows;
- hand off intentionally.

Avoid:

> "Now Xander is going to talk about..."

without explaining the logic.

---

# Non-Speakers

Team credibility continues when a person is not speaking.

Non-speakers should:

- stay attentive;
- look at the presenter;
- avoid visible distraction;
- prepare for questions.

---

# Q&A

A team should not allow one member to answer everything.

Shared response demonstrates:

- preparation;
- ownership;
- collective credibility.

---

# My Take

The hardest team-presentation problem is usually integration.

Dividing slides is easy.

Building:

- one Big Idea;
- one visual language;
- one logical flow;
- shared ownership

requires more coordination.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Build the story before assigning slides.
- Rehearse transitions, not only individual sections.
- Use one consistent visual system.
- Keep all speakers visibly engaged.
- Share Q&A.
- Preserve the team's Big Idea across speakers.
