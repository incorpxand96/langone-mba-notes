# Business Email

Business email should make the reader's task easy.

The course emphasizes:

- clear strategy;
- succinct language;
- high skim value;
- specific next steps.

---

# Subject Line

Be specific.

Weak:

> Proposal

Better:

> Strategic planning project proposal

When action is needed, use a verb.

Examples:

- Vote on new policy
- Review June 6 meeting agenda
- Submit applications for flex-time program

The subject line should tell the reader why the email matters.

---

# Opening

Get to the point quickly.

Put the most important information early.

If asking for something, move the request up front.

Use a preview when the email has multiple points.

Examples:

> Here are four arguments in favor of the proposal.

> This email explains a five-step installation process.

---

# Body

Organize around:

- issues;
- recommendations;
- alternatives;
- aspects of the problem.

Use:

- headings;
- short chunks;
- lists;
- white space;
- parallel form.

---

# Content

Recommendations should be:

- clear;
- specific;
- supported by observations, research, or facts.

The email should make the case for why the reader should accept or act.

---

# Style

Use:

- Plain English;
- active voice where useful;
- appropriate tone;
- correct grammar;
- concise sentences.

---

# Common Email Mistakes

1. Unclear subject line
2. Poor or missing greeting
3. Unnecessary copies
4. Rambling, unformatted message
5. Sloppy grammar/spelling/punctuation
6. Unfamiliar abbreviations
7. All caps
8. Unfriendly tone
9. Missing closing
10. No clear request for action

---

# My Take

Email is an asynchronous interface.

The reader cannot interrupt to ask:

- "What's the point?"
- "What do you need from me?"
- "How is this organized?"

The email should answer those questions preemptively.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Make the subject line informative.
- Put the request or recommendation early.
- Preview multi-part emails.
- Use high skim value.
- Make next steps explicit.
- Keep tone direct and courteous.
- Proofread before sending.
