# AIM Communication Strategy

AIM is the strategic core of the course:

- **Audience** - attention;
- **Intent** - influence;
- **Message** - memory.

The point is to think strategically before choosing words, slides, or delivery technique.

---

# Audience

Ask:

- Who are they?
- What are their expectations?
- What are their attitudes and interests?
- What do they already know?
- What do they know about me?
- How will they benefit from what I am saying?
- What might cause resistance?

Audience analysis should shape both content and form.

The same idea should be communicated differently to:

- executives;
- technical specialists;
- peers;
- customers;
- investors;
- employees.

---

# Intent

Intent answers:

> **What specific result do I want from the audience?**

Possible results:

- know something;
- believe something;
- approve something;
- take an action;
- change a behavior;
- support a proposal.

The course repeatedly emphasizes **starting with the end in mind**.

The desired result should be specific enough to drive the rest of the communication.

---

# Message

Message answers:

> **What is the one Big Idea I want the audience to remember?**

The course pushes toward being able to articulate the idea in a single sentence.

The Big Idea should combine:

- a point of view;
- relevance to the audience;
- a reason to care.

---

# Attention, Influence, Memory

AIM also maps to three communication problems.

## Attention

The audience must notice and stay engaged.

Tools include:

- grabbers;
- chunking;
- transitions;
- stories;
- visuals.

## Influence

The audience must see why the idea matters.

Use:

- logic;
- evidence;
- emotional relevance;
- credibility.

## Memory

The audience should retain the core idea.

Use:

- simple message;
- repetition;
- structure;
- ending;
- call to action.

---

# Chunking

Breaking a presentation into sections helps attention reset.

Important information often benefits from placement at:

- the beginning of a chunk;
- the end of a chunk.

Transitions should make the movement visible.

---

# How I Think About It

AIM is useful because it moves communication planning away from:

> "What do I want to say?"

toward:

> **"What does this audience need to hear in order to produce the result I want?"**

That sounds like a subtle difference, but it changes the entire drafting process.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Define the audience before drafting.
- Write the intent as an observable result.
- State the Big Idea in one sentence.
- Remove content that does not support the Big Idea.
- Use structure to protect attention.
- Use both logic and emotion where persuasion requires both.
- End by depositing the audience at the next step.
