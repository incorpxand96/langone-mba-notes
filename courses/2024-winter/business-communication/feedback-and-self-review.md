# Feedback and Self-Review

The course treats communication as a trainable skill.

Video review and feedback are used to turn vague impressions into observable behaviors.

---

# Three-Step Video Review

## Step 1 - Identify Strengths

Begin by forcing yourself to identify what worked.

Look at:

- audience response;
- timing;
- humor;
- eye contact;
- stance;
- gestures;
- vocal emphasis;
- pauses;
- clarity.

This creates a balanced baseline and identifies behaviors worth repeating.

## Step 2 - Describe Weaknesses Objectively

Do not say:

> "I looked terrible."

Describe:

- what happened;
- when;
- how often;
- what effect it had.

Examples:

- relied too much on script;
- eye contact stayed below camera;
- gestures were limited;
- stood in one place;
- pace accelerated;
- last sentences were too quiet.

Objective description makes improvement possible.

## Step 3 - Target Two or Three Behaviors

Do not try to fix everything at once.

Choose a small number of delivery behaviors and define a strategy for each.

---

# Feedback Language

Good feedback focuses on:

- observable behavior;
- impact;
- alternative behavior.

It should avoid identity judgments.

Instead of:

> "You're not confident."

say:

> "Your volume dropped and your eyes stayed on the slide during the recommendation."

---

# My Take

The most useful idea here is that **self-awareness should be behavioral, not self-punitive**.

Communication development becomes much easier when the unit of analysis is:

- stance;
- pace;
- filler;
- eye contact;
- opening;
- transition;

rather than:

- "good presenter";
- "bad presenter."

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Start with strengths.
- Describe, do not insult.
- Separate behavior from identity.
- Use video evidence.
- Choose two or three goals.
- Define a replacement behavior.
- Rehearse the replacement.
- Review again.
