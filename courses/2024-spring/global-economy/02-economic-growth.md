# Economic Growth

## Production Function

`Y = F(A, K, L, H)`

where `A` is TFP, `K` physical capital, `L` labor, and `H` human capital.

Course Cobb-Douglas form:

`Y = A × K^α × (HL)^(1−α)`

Properties:
- positive marginal products;
- diminishing marginal products of individual inputs;
- constant returns to scale when all inputs rise together.

## Sources of Growth

Long-run growth can come from:
- physical-capital accumulation;
- labor/employment growth;
- human-capital improvement;
- TFP growth.

Growth accounting decomposes observed growth across these sources.

## Solow Model

Core assumptions in the course version:
- no government;
- no international trade/capital flows;
- Cobb-Douglas production;
- constant saving fraction;
- initially constant population/labor;
- initially constant TFP.

Capital rises when investment exceeds depreciation.

At the steady state:

`Investment per worker = Depreciation per worker`

## Predictions

Under comparable fundamentals:
- poorer economies grow faster;
- growth slows as capital accumulates;
- poorer economies can converge toward richer economies.

## Saving

A higher saving/investment rate raises the steady-state level of capital and output per worker, but does not by itself create permanently faster per-worker growth.

## Population Growth

Holding other factors fixed, faster labor/population growth can lower steady-state capital per worker.

## TFP

TFP measures how efficiently the economy converts inputs into output.

It can improve through:
- technology;
- better allocation;
- policy reform;
- contract enforcement;
- property rights;
- competition;
- lower corruption/misallocation.

The course's key long-run conclusion:

> **Sustained growth in output per worker ultimately requires sustained TFP growth.**

## Taiwan Homework

My homework decomposed Taiwan's 1960–2014 growth in GDP per worker. The largest contribution came from physical-capital accumulation, followed by human capital, then TFP.

## Japan Application

The slides show early postwar Japanese growth relying heavily on capital accumulation and TFP; the later slowdown coincided with weak TFP growth.
