# Selected Homework Applications

This file preserves useful reasoning patterns from my submitted homework rather than reproducing every question.

## 1. GDP Accounting

If a government buys an imported good:
- `G` rises;
- `M` rises by the same amount;
- domestic GDP does not rise from the imported production itself.

## 2. CPI vs. GDP Deflator

A price change for an imported consumer good can affect CPI more than the GDP deflator because CPI is based on consumer purchases while the GDP deflator covers domestically produced output.

## 3. Growth Accounting — Taiwan

My growth homework:
1. calculated GDP per worker;
2. solved for TFP;
3. decomposed growth into capital per worker, human capital, and TFP.

The largest contribution in the exercise came from physical-capital accumulation, followed by human capital and then TFP.

## 4. Minimum Wage

A minimum wage above the equilibrium wage creates unemployment, but a decline in labor demand only makes the wage floor binding if the new market-clearing wage falls below it.

## 5. Negative Supply Shock

My business-cycle homework modeled a temporary negative supply shock:
- SRAS shifts left;
- output falls;
- prices rise;
- employment falls.

If the shock is temporary, wage adjustment can gradually return the economy toward potential output.

## 6. Laffer Curve

A tax cut raises revenue only if the initial tax rate lies above the revenue-maximizing point. The conceptual curve does not establish that the actual economy is on that side.

## 7. Fiscal Expansion Near Full Employment

With little output gap, a tax cut or spending increase has less room to raise real output and more potential to raise prices, rates, and crowding out.

## 8. Fed Asset Purchases

When the Fed buys an asset from a non-bank:
1. Fed assets rise;
2. the seller's bank receives reserve credit;
3. the seller receives a deposit;
4. aggregate reserves rise.

## 9. Excess Reserves

Individual banks can transfer reserves, but system-wide Fed-created reserves do not disappear simply because banks make loans to non-banks.

## 10. Macro Problem-Solving Template

For most policy questions:
1. identify initial equilibrium;
2. identify the first curve/market shift;
3. explain the behavioral mechanism;
4. state short-run changes in output, prices, employment, and rates;
5. explain wage/price adjustment;
6. state the medium-run equilibrium;
7. separate aggregate from distributional effects.
