This folder contains my notes for **The Global Economy (COR1-GB 2303)** at NYU Stern, taught by **Julen Esteban-Pretel** in Spring 2024.

The course focuses on understanding the determinants of countries' economic performance and using economic theory and data to evaluate macroeconomic questions.

Because this course is relatively self-contained within the MBA curriculum, these notes live entirely in the course folder rather than feeding a broader concepts tree.

## Unifying Course Logic

1. Measure the economic outcome.
2. Build a model explaining the outcome.
3. Compare the model with data.
4. Use the model to analyze a policy or shock.
5. Distinguish short-run from medium-/long-run effects.

## High-Level Takeaways

- Measurement definitions matter.
- Real and nominal variables must be separated.
- Long-run growth depends heavily on productivity.
- Labor outcomes depend on markets plus frictions/institutions.
- Trade raises aggregate consumption possibilities but creates winners and losers.
- Business cycles are fluctuations around a longer-run trend.
- Supply and demand shocks have different signatures.
- Fiscal policy can support demand but crowd out private activity.
- Monetary policy operates through rates, demand, output, and prices.
- Exchange-rate interpretation depends on the quote convention and policy regime.
