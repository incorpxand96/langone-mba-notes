# Business Cycles

Business cycles are fluctuations around the long-run growth trend.

## Describing Macro Variables

### Cyclicality
- procyclical;
- countercyclical;
- acyclical.

### Volatility
More or less volatile than GDP.

### Synchronicity
Leads, coincides with, or lags GDP.

## Patterns from Class

- consumption: procyclical, less volatile;
- investment: procyclical, much more volatile;
- imports: procyclical, more volatile;
- exports: procyclical;
- employment: procyclical;
- unemployment: countercyclical.

## Leading Indicators

Useful indicators should correlate with activity, lead GDP, arrive quickly, and be measured well.

Examples:
- industrial production;
- yield curve;
- housing starts;
- durable-goods orders;
- share prices;
- consumer confidence;
- hours worked;
- interest-rate spreads.

An inverted yield curve has short rates above long rates and can reflect expectations of weaker future activity / lower future rates.

## Aggregate Demand

AD slopes downward. Higher prices reduce real purchasing power and can raise interest rates, reducing consumption and investment.

## Aggregate Supply

Short-run AS slopes upward because wages do not instantly adjust. Medium-/long-run supply is tied to potential output.

## Temporary Negative Supply Shock

Short run:
- output ↓;
- employment ↓;
- unemployment ↑;
- prices/inflation ↑;
- interest rates ↑.

## Temporary Negative Demand Shock

Short run:
- consumption ↓;
- investment ↓;
- output ↓;
- employment ↓;
- unemployment ↑;
- inflation ↓;
- interest rates ↓.

## Diagnostic Rule

- output ↓ + prices ↑ → adverse supply shock;
- output ↓ + prices ↓ → adverse demand shock.
