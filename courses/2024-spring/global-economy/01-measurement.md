# Measurement in Macroeconomics

## GDP

GDP is the market value of all final goods and services produced within a country over a period of time.

Three approaches should produce the same total.

### Production Approach
Sum value added.

`Value Added = Sales Revenue − Intermediate Inputs Purchased`

### Expenditure Approach
`GDP = C + I + G + X − M`

Only newly produced goods count.

### Income Approach
Sum income received by labor, owners of physical/financial capital, and relevant taxes.

## GDP vs. GNP

`GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad`

GDP is organized around location of production; GNP around national ownership/income.

## Limits of GDP

National accounts can miss or mismeasure:
- household production;
- underground activity;
- intangible investment;
- environmental damage;
- some government-service value.

GDP is an activity measure, not a complete welfare measure.

## Nominal vs. Real GDP

**Nominal GDP:** current prices.

**Real GDP:** base-year prices.

The gap between nominal and real GDP growth reflects price changes.

## Inflation Measures

### GDP Deflator
`GDP Deflator = Nominal GDP / Real GDP`

### CPI
Measures the cost of a representative consumer basket.

CPI focuses on consumer purchases; the GDP deflator spans domestically produced output.

## Purchasing Power Parity

PPP uses a common set of prices across countries. It is preferable to simple market-exchange-rate conversion when comparing real living standards because exchange rates fluctuate and local price levels differ.

## Homework Applications

- Imported production does not add to domestic GDP.
- A government purchase of an imported good can raise `G` and `M` by offsetting amounts.
- CPI can respond more strongly than the GDP deflator to a price change in an imported consumer good.

## Checklist

1. Nominal or real?
2. Aggregate or per capita?
3. What prices are used?
4. Is production domestic?
5. Activity measure or welfare measure?
