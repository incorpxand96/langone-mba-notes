# Rachel Weldon at KFC

## Context

Weldon inherited KFC's weakest U.S. region:

- declining sales and transactions;
- weak profitability;
- high management turnover;
- low morale;
- new leadership team.

She was also:

- younger;
- less experienced;
- less credentialed

than peer division leaders.

---

# Early Leadership Approach

Weldon responded by going to the field.

She personally visited a vast number of restaurants and built a reputation for:

- listening;
- working relentlessly;
- understanding local problems;
- publicly backing concerns she believed were valid.

This built trust with franchisees and operators.

---

# Results

The division began improving:

- sales;
- transactions;
- local programs;
- morale.

At the same time, her willingness to deviate from corporate preferences created friction with headquarters.

---

# Leadership Tension

Weldon illustrates a recurring leadership problem:

> **local truth vs. corporate alignment**

A leader close to the work may discover that central policy is wrong for the operating context.

But continual deviation can also fragment the organization.

---

# Power of Listening

Listening generated:

- information;
- trust;
- legitimacy.

This is a useful reminder that leaders sometimes create influence by increasing the quality of their information before trying to persuade.

---

# My Take

Weldon's early success was not simply charisma.

It was a system of:

- field presence;
- inquiry;
- visible effort;
- local experimentation;
- trust.

That is a more reproducible model of leadership.
