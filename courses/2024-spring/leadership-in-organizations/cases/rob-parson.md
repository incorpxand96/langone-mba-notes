# Rob Parson

The case creates a classic tension:

- exceptional business performance;
- weak fit with desired culture and interpersonal norms.

---

# Business Performance

Parson contributed materially to the division's improved standing.

Strengths included:

- selling;
- initiative;
- resource mobilization;
- commercial impact.

---

# Cultural Misalignment

Morgan Stanley was trying to create a more collaborative "one firm" culture.

Parson's working style created friction.

My memo argues that this is not merely "form over substance."

In a collaborative firm:

> **form can become substance**

because interpersonal dysfunction can damage the ability to coordinate resources and sustain performance.

---

# Promotion Decision

My recommendation was:

- retain Parson;
- do not promote him yet;
- make the development path explicit.

Feedback should:

1. acknowledge genuine strengths;
2. give concrete behavioral examples;
3. explain the gap to Managing Director expectations;
4. frame development as a route to future promotion.

---

# Feedback Failure

Manager Paul Nasr's weak communication contributed to the problem.

A high performer should not discover at the promotion decision that his behavior has been viewed as disqualifying.

---

# Congruence

The case links:

- strategy;
- culture;
- people;
- incentives;
- promotion criteria.

If the firm claims collaboration matters but promotes purely on revenue, the enacted culture will contradict the espoused culture.

---

# My Take

The case is a useful reminder that culture must enter real resource decisions.

If culture never affects:

- hiring;
- promotion;
- compensation;
- retention;

then it is branding, not culture.
