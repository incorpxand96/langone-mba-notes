# Leading Change

Organizations are built for stability, yet environments require adaptation.

Leading change therefore requires changing both:

- the system;
- people's willingness to move.

---

# Change Process

The course uses a sequence consistent with Kotter:

## Unfreeze

- establish urgency;
- form a guiding coalition;
- create a vision.

## Change

- communicate the vision;
- empower others to act;
- create short-term wins.

## Refreeze / Institutionalize

- consolidate improvements;
- produce additional change;
- institutionalize new approaches.

---

# Pull and Push

The 12 Angry Men exercise highlights two kinds of influence.

## Pull

- ask questions;
- frame;
- appeal to emotion.

## Push

- assert;
- involve;
- appeal to reason.

Effective change leadership can require both.

---

# Credibility and Relational Capital

Influence becomes easier when the change agent has:

- individual credibility;
- relationships;
- trust.

This makes change partly a pre-work problem.

You cannot build every relationship after resistance begins.

---

# 20 / 60 / 20 Logic

A useful coalition idea:

- some people are already supportive;
- some are opposed;
- a large middle may be movable.

Instead of spending all energy on the hardest opponents:

1. establish common ground with the movable middle;
2. build those relationships into a coalition;
3. let coalition pressure alter the broader environment.

---

# Directive vs. Persuasive Pace

A more directive approach fits conditions such as:

- urgency/crisis;
- high dissatisfaction;
- low resistance;
- clear change;
- strong information advantage.

A more persuasive/involving approach fits when:

- commitment is essential;
- the change is complex or unclear;
- support from important constituencies is needed.

---

# My Take

Change fails when leaders treat resistance as a communications nuisance rather than information about:

- incentives;
- trust;
- identity;
- uncertainty;
- political position.
