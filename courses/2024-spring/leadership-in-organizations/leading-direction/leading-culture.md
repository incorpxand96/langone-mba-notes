# Leading Culture

Organizational culture is the shared set of:

- values;
- attitudes;
- beliefs;
- expected behaviors.

It is the organization's internal personality.

Culture is not simply what management says is important.

It is what people learn is actually important.

---

# Three Levels of Culture

## 1. Artifacts

Visible or audible elements:

- offices;
- rituals;
- events;
- symbols;
- stories;
- routines.

## 2. Espoused Values

Stated:

- principles;
- standards;
- goals;
- beliefs.

## 3. Underlying Assumptions

Taken-for-granted beliefs about:

- people;
- work;
- reality;
- what is rewarded;
- what is safe.

These are often implicit.

---

# Espoused vs. Enacted Values

## Espoused

The desired culture.

What the organization says.

## Enacted

The perceived or real culture.

What people actually experience.

Leadership credibility depends heavily on alignment between them.

---

# Culture Formation and Reinforcement

Culture is reinforced through:

- who is attracted to the organization;
- who is selected;
- who stays or leaves;
- formal training;
- informal socialization;
- mentors;
- rewards;
- role models;
- leader behavior.

---

# Attraction–Selection–Attrition

Organizations can become more culturally homogeneous because:

1. similar people are attracted;
2. similar people are selected;
3. people who do not fit leave.

This can create cohesion.

It can also reduce difference and adaptability.

---

# My Take

A values statement is weak evidence of culture.

A better culture audit asks:

- Who gets promoted?
- Who gets punished?
- What behavior is tolerated?
- What happens after a mistake?
- Who speaks in meetings?
- What do people warn new hires about?

Culture is learned through consequences.
