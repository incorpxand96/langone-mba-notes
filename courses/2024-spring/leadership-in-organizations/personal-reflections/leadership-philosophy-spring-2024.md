# My Leadership Philosophy — Spring 2024 Synthesis

This is a synthesis of my Spring 2024 assignments and should be read as a historical statement.

---

# 1. Leadership Is Earned Through Behavior

I was reluctant to use "leader" as a self-description.

That discomfort is consistent with the course premise:

leadership should be judged by what a person does.

---

# 2. Alignment Matters More Than Slogans

Across my LCP and case work, I repeatedly returned to congruence.

Examples:

- values vs. time;
- culture vs. promotion criteria;
- talent-development goals vs. information systems;
- vision vs. execution mechanisms.

A system loses credibility when its mechanisms contradict its message.

---

# 3. Trust Is an Operating Asset

The Roller Coaster memo treats broken trust as a reason not to make an otherwise tempting retention move.

Trust changes:

- information;
- delegation;
- risk;
- coordination.

---

# 4. Performance and Culture Cannot Be Fully Separated

The Rob Parson memo argues that interpersonal behavior affects the ability of the organization to perform.

"Form" can become substance because organizations rely on cooperation.

---

# 5. Development Is a Leadership Obligation

My own professional goals included mentoring others.

The *Beating the Odds* reading reinforced the importance of:

- sponsors;
- feedback;
- opportunity;
- affirming contexts.

Leadership includes helping other people build capacity.

---

# 6. Ambition Needs Constraints

The LCP's central personal risk was not lack of ambition.

It was trying to maximize too many things at once.

A mature leadership model needs:

- recovery;
- tradeoffs;
- resource limits;
- contingency planning.

---

# 7. Systems Matter

Everest, the Congruence Model, Parson, and Roller Coaster all pushed me away from purely individual explanations.

The leader's job is not only to manage people.

It is to design conditions in which good behavior becomes more likely.

---

# Spring 2024 Leadership Statement

> **Lead by creating alignment: define a direction people can understand, understand the people and system well enough to diagnose real constraints, build trust and support, and design incentives, structures, feedback, and culture that make the desired behavior sustainable.**
