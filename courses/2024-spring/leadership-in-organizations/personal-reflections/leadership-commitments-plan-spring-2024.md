# Leadership Commitments Plan — Spring 2024

## Vision

My stated twelve-month vision was:

> **Do It All and Do It Well**

The deeper goal was balance among:

1. professional success;
2. personal growth;
3. social connection.

---

# Strategy

The plan attempted to convert the vision into measurable commitments.

Examples of the logic:

- professional advancement needed deliberate performance and stakeholder support;
- personal growth needed protected time;
- relationships needed explicit commitments rather than residual time.

---

# Measures

The assignment reinforced a useful principle:

> **If success matters, define how success will be observed.**

The plan therefore used concrete behaviors and milestones rather than relying only on aspiration.

---

# Alignment

The core analytical question was:

> Are my values, priorities, time, motivations, and goals pointing in the same direction?

This mirrors the Congruence Model at an individual scale.

---

# Tradeoffs

The plan acknowledged that full alignment was impossible.

Time and energy are scarce.

Success therefore required conscious tradeoffs rather than pretending every goal could be maximized simultaneously.

---

# Resilience

The plan also considered what to do if outcomes outside my control did not occur.

This is important because a commitment plan should not make psychological well-being depend entirely on a single external result.

---

# Organizational Analogy

The final paper connects personal alignment to organizational alignment.

A leader who communicates one vision but creates:

- conflicting incentives;
- incompatible goals;
- contradictory systems

creates frustration and distrust.

The same is true personally when declared priorities and actual resource allocation diverge.

---

# My Spring 2024 Insight

The most durable idea from the LCP is:

> **Alignment is not agreement among words. It is consistency among values, priorities, resources, behavior, and consequences.**
