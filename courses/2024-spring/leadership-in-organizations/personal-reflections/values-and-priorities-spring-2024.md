# Values and Priorities — Spring 2024

This note synthesizes LCP Exercise A and the final Leadership Commitments Plan.

---

# Values I Identified

My Exercise A list included:

- curiosity;
- hard work;
- growth;
- self-reliance;
- timeliness;
- truth-seeking;
- uniqueness;
- achievement;
- courtesy;
- community.

An important part of the exercise was comparing:

> how I see myself

with:

> how people close to me see me.

There was substantial overlap, but other people also saw qualities I was less inclined to name directly.

---

# Three Priority Pillars

My final LCP organized the year around three categories.

## Professional Growth

Themes:

- advancement;
- learning;
- expanded responsibility;
- mentoring;
- community contribution.

## Personal Growth

Themes:

- intellectual curiosity;
- physical activity;
- creative/hobby development;
- recovery.

## Social Connection

Themes:

- maintain friendships;
- spend time with others;
- deliberately allocate resources to relationships.

---

# Balance

The most important addition was **balance**.

Rather than maximize one pillar, I wanted meaningful progress across all three.

This reflects a change from a pure achievement model toward a portfolio model.

---

# Values as Constraints

Values matter only if they change resource allocation.

The real test is:

> When two valuable opportunities conflict, which one wins?

That is where values become operational.

---

# Historical Tension

The strongest tension I identified was between:

- self-directed growth/achievement;
- relationships/community.

The LCP explicitly recognized that time allocated to one could crowd out the other.

---

# My Spring 2024 Insight

A value is not something I admire.

It is something I am willing to trade for.
