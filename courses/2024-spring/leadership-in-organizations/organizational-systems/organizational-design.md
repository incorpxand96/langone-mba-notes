# Organizational Design

Organizational design should enable the strategy and work.

It includes more than boxes on an org chart.

Relevant elements include:

- roles;
- decision rights;
- information;
- incentives;
- reporting;
- coordination;
- talent;
- informal norms.

---

# Design as Fit

The Congruence Model implies there is no universally best structure.

The right design depends on:

- work;
- people;
- strategy;
- culture;
- environment.

A redesign that changes formal structure without addressing informal behavior may fail.

---

# Resilience

A good organization should handle foreseeable contingencies.

Questions:

- What happens when a key person leaves?
- Where is knowledge concentrated?
- Who can make decisions?
- Can another person step in?
- Are succession and development systems real or rhetorical?

---

# Roller Coaster Ride

My case analysis highlights a design weakness:

the group depended heavily on a star senior analyst and lacked sufficient organizational resilience if that person left.

The case also suggests a potential mismatch between a stated desire to develop internal talent and a performance-information system that concentrated information with direct managers.

---

# Design and Culture

Formal design can say:

> collaborate.

Incentives can say:

> compete.

Culture can say:

> protect yourself.

Performance follows the system people actually experience.

---

# My Take

Reorganization is not organizational design.

A real design diagnosis asks how:

- work;
- information;
- incentives;
- relationships;
- talent

fit together.
