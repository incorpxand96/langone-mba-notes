# Collaboration and Conflict

Group work contains a basic tension:

- people want inclusion and acceptance;
- people also want distinctiveness and status.

Organizations add a second tension:

- work is often team-based;
- rewards are often individual.

These tensions produce social dilemmas.

---

# I vs. We

A social dilemma exists when:

> individual/private interests conflict with collective interests.

Examples include:

- status dilemmas;
- reward dilemmas;
- temporal-mobility dilemmas;
- effort/free-rider dilemmas;
- speaking-up dilemmas.

---

# Intergroup Conflict

People tend to classify themselves into groups and prefer to see their own group favorably.

This can create:

- comparison;
- defensiveness;
- competition;
- distorted interpretation.

---

# Sources of Conflict

Conflict can arise from:

### Surface

- miscommunication;
- misinformation.

### Deeper

- different needs;
- different priorities.

### Deepest

- values;
- beliefs;
- perceptions;
- culture.

---

# Conflict Promoters

Examples:

- heightened evaluation;
- power abuse;
- intransigence;
- indirect communication;
- avoidance;
- assuming intentions;
- talking to third parties rather than the source.

---

# Superordinate Goals

Intergroup conflict can improve when leaders:

- focus groups on a common objective;
- redefine the relevant "we";
- create shared identity;
- avoid defining the other side as the enemy.

---

# Relationship vs. Task Conflict

## Relationship Conflict

Personal/social tension unrelated to the work.

Usually harmful.

## Task Conflict

Disagreement about ideas and the work.

Can be useful.

The course emphasizes:

> **The absence of conflict is not harmony. It can be apathy.**

Moderate task conflict can improve performance, particularly for non-routine work.

Too much conflict is damaging.

---

# Managing Team Conflict

## 1. Diagnose

Ask:

- Who are the parties?
- How do they define the conflict?
- Is it task, relationship, or process?
- Is there spillover?
- Are parties interdependent?

## 2. Decide Whether to Act

Avoidance can occasionally be useful when:

- people need time to cool down;
- more information is needed.

## 3. Set the Stage

Before asserting my position:

- understand the other side;
- make common ground explicit;
- acknowledge emotion;
- reduce unnecessary tension.

## 4. Discuss

- thank people for taking the risk;
- remain open to other perspectives;
- avoid dismissing feelings;
- keep the discussion on the relevant task.

---

# Conflict Styles

Five familiar approaches:

- competing;
- collaborating;
- compromising;
- avoiding;
- accommodating.

The problem is not having a style.

It is using the wrong style automatically.

---

# My Take

Conflict quality matters more than conflict quantity.

Healthy organizations should make it possible to disagree about the work without turning disagreement into identity threat.
