# Power and Influence

Formal authority is only one source of power.

People can influence organizational outcomes through:

- expertise;
- relationships;
- scarce information;
- reputation;
- alternatives;
- control of important work;
- coalition support.

---

# Formal vs. Informal Power

The Roller Coaster Ride case illustrates how formal hierarchy can coexist with strong informal dependence.

A subordinate who controls:

- client relationships;
- revenue;
- scarce expertise;
- reputation

may have considerable power over a formal manager.

---

# Dependence

Power grows when another person depends on something I control and cannot easily replace.

This means power is relational.

---

# Influence Without Authority

Useful influence tools include:

- credibility;
- framing;
- questions;
- coalition building;
- common ground;
- emotion;
- reason.

Leading change often depends on these because formal directives cannot manufacture commitment.

---

# Political Skill

Politics need not mean deception.

Organizational politics can include:

- understanding interests;
- mapping stakeholders;
- sequencing conversations;
- building support;
- recognizing veto players.

Ignoring politics does not make it disappear.

---

# My Take

Formal power determines what I am allowed to do.

Informal power often determines what I can actually get done.
