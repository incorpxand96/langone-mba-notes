# Listening, Trust, and Followership

Leadership is not simply transmitting direction.

It also requires gathering information from people who:

- see the work differently;
- know things the leader does not;
- may distrust the organization;
- may have experienced prior failures.

---

# Rachel Weldon as an Example

Weldon inherited a severely underperforming KFC region.

Early in her tenure she:

- visited the field extensively;
- listened directly to restaurant operators and franchisees;
- supported concerns she believed were legitimate;
- built credibility through visible effort.

This improved trust with people close to the operation.

It also created friction with some corporate functions.

---

# Listening as Diagnosis

Listening can reveal:

- local information;
- implementation barriers;
- misaligned policy;
- morale;
- customer reality;
- hidden resistance.

The leader's job is not to accept every local claim.

It is to improve the quality of the system's information.

---

# Trust

Trust affects:

- willingness to speak;
- willingness to follow;
- willingness to share bad news;
- interpretation of motives;
- acceptance of change.

Trust is therefore an operating variable.

---

# Followership

Leaders need to understand:

- how people see the situation;
- what they value;
- what they fear;
- why they might resist.

The course repeatedly emphasizes understanding followers rather than assuming a good idea will sell itself.

---

# My Take

Listening is not the opposite of decisive leadership.

Good listening improves the information on which decisive action is based.
