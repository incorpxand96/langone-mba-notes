# Leadership in Organizations

This folder is my working knowledge base for **Leadership in Organizations**, taught by **Nate Pettit** at NYU Stern.

The course starts from a premise that is easy to say and difficult to live:

> **Leadership is behavior, not position.**

Leadership opportunities exist throughout an organization. Authority can help, but authority is not the same thing as leadership.

The course is also fundamentally a course about **systems**. Effective leadership requires understanding:

- myself;
- other people;
- groups;
- incentives;
- culture;
- structure;
- information;
- the external environment;
- the interactions among all of them.

A useful umbrella question is:

> **What behavior, design, or intervention will improve alignment among the people, work, structure, culture, incentives, and direction of the organization?**

---

# Three Levels of Analysis

A recurring lesson from the course is to avoid explaining organizational outcomes too narrowly.

## Individual

Examples:

- overconfidence;
- motivation;
- bias;
- skill;
- emotion;
- escalation of commitment.

## Group

Examples:

- trust;
- hierarchy;
- communication;
- speaking up;
- groupthink;
- conflict;
- coordination.

## System

Examples:

- structure;
- culture;
- incentives;
- resources;
- environment;
- logistics;
- interdependence.

The Mount Everest case makes the point vividly: major outcomes rarely have a single-level explanation.

# My Current LiO Mental Model

## 1. Diagnose Before Acting

Ask:

- What outcome is weak?
- Is the problem individual, group, system, or some combination?
- Where is the misalignment?
- What evidence supports the diagnosis?

## 2. Set Direction

Create a vision people can actually picture.

A good vision should:

- evoke emotion;
- make success concrete;
- connect future direction to daily work;
- create a shared reference point.

## 3. Align the Organization

Check fit among:

- strategy;
- work;
- people;
- formal organization;
- informal organization.

## 4. Mobilize People

Leadership requires:

- credibility;
- relationships;
- listening;
- influence;
- motivation;
- communication.

## 5. Design the System

Leaders shape:

- incentives;
- goals;
- information flows;
- decision processes;
- culture;
- feedback;
- structure.

## 6. Preserve Learning

Make it possible to:

- surface mistakes;
- receive feedback;
- update beliefs;
- challenge assumptions;
- experiment without creating unacceptable risk.

# My Current Leadership Principles

1. **Leadership is behavior, not title.**
2. **Do not confuse formal authority with actual influence.**
3. **Diagnose outcomes at the individual, group, and system levels.**
4. **Beware the romance of leadership: context and systems matter.**
5. **Management and leadership are complementary.**
6. **Leadership creates direction, alignment, and motivation.**
7. **A vision should be concrete enough that people can picture success.**
8. **Goals should follow from vision rather than substitute for vision.**
9. **Change requires both pull and push.**
10. **Build credibility and relational capital before demanding change.**
11. **When influencing a group, the movable middle often matters more than the loudest opponents.**
12. **Culture is what people experience, not what the values poster says.**
13. **Misalignment between espoused and enacted values destroys credibility.**
14. **Culture is reinforced through selection, socialization, rewards, and role modeling.**
15. **Listening is an operating mechanism, not simply interpersonal courtesy.**
16. **Trust creates access to information and makes change easier.**
17. **Fairness is a powerful motivator.**
18. **Money is only one source of motivation.**
19. **Incentives communicate what the organization truly values.**
20. **Never reward A while hoping for B.**
21. **Specific, challenging, attainable goals generally outperform "do your best."**
22. **Feedback should reduce the gap between how I see myself and how others see me.**
23. **Employees should not be surprised by formal performance feedback.**
24. **Developmental feedback should describe behavior, not personality.**
25. **Group potential is higher than average individual potential—but groups often fail to realize it.**
26. **Design the decision process before debating the decision.**
27. **Overconfidence, framing, sunk costs, and anchoring distort judgment.**
28. **Groupthink, pluralistic ignorance, and polarization distort groups.**
29. **Healthy task conflict can improve decisions; relationship conflict usually damages them.**
30. **The absence of conflict is not necessarily harmony—it may be apathy.**
31. **A leader should create conditions where dissent can surface before a decision.**
32. **Alignment is dynamic; organizations must continually re-fit as conditions change.**
33. **Personal leadership also requires alignment among values, priorities, resources, behavior, and measures of success.**
