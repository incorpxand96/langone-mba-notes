# Strategic Alignment and the Congruence Model

The Congruence Model treats the organization as an open system.

## Inputs

- environment;
- resources;
- history.

These influence strategy.

## Transformation System

Four major organizational components:

1. **Work**
2. **People**
3. **Formal organization**
4. **Informal organization**

## Outputs

Performance occurs at:

- individual;
- group/unit;
- organizational/system

levels.

---

# The Central Idea: Fit

There is no universally ideal:

- structure;
- culture;
- person;
- incentive system.

What matters is **fit**.

Key pairings include:

- people ↔ work;
- people ↔ formal organization;
- people ↔ informal organization;
- work ↔ formal organization;
- work ↔ informal organization;
- formal ↔ informal organization.

Poor fit creates performance problems.

---

# Formal vs. Informal Organization

## Formal

- structure;
- roles;
- reporting lines;
- processes;
- policies;
- rewards.

## Informal

- norms;
- relationships;
- culture;
- informal power;
- actual patterns of behavior.

A formal redesign can fail if the informal system remains incompatible with the new work.

---

# Diagnostic Sequence

A useful Congruence Model sequence:

1. Identify symptoms.
2. Specify inputs.
3. Define required and actual outputs.
4. Identify performance gaps.
5. Describe the organizational components.
6. Assess congruence.
7. Generate hypotheses about root causes.
8. Identify actions and evaluate consequences.

---

# Dynamic Alignment

Alignment is not a one-time design exercise.

External change can alter:

- technology;
- competition;
- policy;
- customer behavior.

That can require:

- new strategy;
- new work;
- new structure;
- different people;
- culture change.

---

# My Take

This framework is valuable because it resists single-variable explanations.

When performance is weak, "reorg" is not automatically the solution.

The better question is:

> **Where is fit poor, and which component is the best leverage point?**
