# Leadership vs. Management

Kotter distinguishes management from leadership without treating one as superior.

They solve different organizational problems.

## Management

Management helps organizations cope with **complexity**.

Core activities:

- planning and budgeting;
- organizing and staffing;
- controlling and problem solving.

Management creates:

- order;
- reliability;
- predictability.

## Leadership

Leadership helps organizations cope with **change**.

Core activities:

- setting direction;
- aligning people;
- motivating and inspiring.

Leadership creates:

- movement;
- adaptation;
- energy.

---

# Complementarity

A well-led but poorly managed organization may have:

- inspiration;
- ambition;
- movement;

but little operating discipline.

A well-managed but under-led organization may have:

- budgets;
- controls;
- routines;

but weak adaptation or direction.

Strong organizations need both.

---

# Direction vs. Planning

Planning creates steps.

Direction creates a destination.

Direction-setting is often more inductive:

- identify patterns;
- understand forces;
- decide where the organization must go;
- create a vision and broad strategy.

Planning should then make the direction operational.

---

# Alignment vs. Organizing

Management creates formal roles and systems.

Leadership creates commitment to direction.

Alignment can empower action because people understand:

- where the organization is going;
- why;
- which behaviors fit.

---

# Motivation vs. Control

Control detects deviations and corrects them.

Leadership motivation provides energy to overcome obstacles.

Motivation can draw on:

- achievement;
- belonging;
- recognition;
- self-esteem;
- control;
- ideals.

---

# My Take

The useful question is not:

> "Should I lead or manage?"

It is:

> **"Does this situation need more direction, more alignment, more motivation, more structure, or more control?"**
