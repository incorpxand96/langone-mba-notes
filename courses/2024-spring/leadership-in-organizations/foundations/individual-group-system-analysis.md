# Individual, Group, and System Analysis

Organizational outcomes often have causes at multiple levels.

## Individual

Potential causes:

- ability;
- motivation;
- overconfidence;
- emotion;
- escalation of commitment;
- fatigue;
- bias.

## Group

Potential causes:

- low trust;
- hierarchy;
- poor communication;
- groupthink;
- coordination failure;
- lack of cohesion;
- silence.

## System

Potential causes:

- structure;
- incentives;
- logistics;
- resources;
- external environment;
- tight interdependence;
- policy.

---

# Why This Matters

The wrong level of diagnosis produces the wrong intervention.

Examples:

### Individual Diagnosis

"That employee needs coaching."

### Group Diagnosis

"The team has no process for challenging the leader."

### System Diagnosis

"The incentive system punishes people for raising bad news."

All three may be relevant.

---

# Everest as a Model

The Everest debrief separates causes such as:

### Individual

- overconfidence;
- escalation;
- leader fatigue;
- weak contingency planning.

### Group

- no speaking up;
- hierarchy;
- low trust;
- poor communication;
- weak cohesion.

### System

- weather;
- supply/logistics;
- tight interconnectedness.

The tragedy cannot be reduced to one bad decision by one leader.

---

# My Take

This is an antidote to managerial storytelling.

Humans like simple explanations.

Organizations rarely offer them.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Diagnose at all three levels.
- Do not personalize structural problems.
- Do not structuralize every interpersonal problem.
- Look for interaction effects.
- Ask what made the behavior rational or predictable.
- Intervene at the level where the cause actually lives.
