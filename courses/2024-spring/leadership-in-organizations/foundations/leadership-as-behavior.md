# Leadership as Behavior

The course rejects the idea that leadership is synonymous with seniority, title, compensation, or formal authority.

> **Leadership = behavior, not position.**

A person can occupy a senior role and fail to lead.

A person without formal authority can:

- identify a problem;
- create direction;
- influence others;
- organize action;
- change a system.

---

# Romance of Leadership

People tend to over-attribute organizational outcomes to visible leaders and underweight situational or systemic factors.

This creates the **romance of leadership**.

A useful diagnostic correction is:

> Before asking "Who caused this?", ask "What combination of person, group, structure, incentives, and environment made this outcome likely?"

---

# Leadership Opportunities

Leadership can include:

- speaking up;
- asking the question others avoid;
- clarifying the goal;
- creating a coalition;
- surfacing a misalignment;
- giving candid feedback;
- redesigning a process;
- protecting dissent;
- helping others act.

Leadership is therefore widely distributed.

---

# "Dead Right"

A leader can be factually correct and still ineffective.

Being "dead right" can mean:

- having the correct analysis;
- lacking support;
- failing to communicate;
- ignoring incentives;
- underestimating politics;
- moving before the system is ready.

Correctness is not sufficient for leadership effectiveness.

---

# My Take

This is one of the course's most durable lessons because it shifts leadership from identity to action.

Instead of asking:

> "Am I a leader?"

ask:

> **"What leadership behavior does this situation require?"**

That question is much more useful.

---

# Rules of Thumb

- Do not wait for permission to notice.
- Distinguish authority from influence.
- Diagnose context before assigning blame.
- Correct analysis is not enough; mobilization matters.
- Leadership can come from below or beside.
- Evaluate behavior, not title.
