# Vertical Integration and Firm Boundaries

Vertical integration moves the firm's boundary into another stage of the value chain.

The core choice is **market vs. quasi-integration vs. common ownership**.

## Evaluate

### Setup Costs
Capital, systems, training.

### Transaction Costs
Information, legal contracting, purchasing/sales.

### Transaction Risks
Hold-up, unreasonable price changes, foreclosure, dependence.

### Coordination Effectiveness
Inventory, utilization, delivery, quality, run lengths.

## Integrate When

Common ownership materially improves coordination or protects value because:

- the market is thin/unreliable;
- investments are highly specific;
- transactions are frequent;
- an adjacent stage has strong bargaining power;
- the market is missing or emerging;
- double marginalization or similar problems are material.

## Do Not Integrate Merely To

- smooth earnings;
- "guarantee" supply;
- capture an attractive supplier margin;
- mimic competitors.

## Quasi-Integration

Alternatives include long-term contracts, JVs, alliances, licensing, franchising, and partial ownership.

## Principle

> **Own the adjacent stage only when ownership solves a contracting or coordination problem more efficiently than the alternatives.**
