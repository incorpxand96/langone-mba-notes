# Disney and Pixar

Disney/Pixar is the cleanest illustration of the difference between **relationship value** and **ownership necessity**.

## Disney Corporate Advantage

Disney historically linked:

- films and characters;
- parks;
- television;
- merchandising;
- music;
- retail;
- distribution.

A successful creative asset could create value across many businesses.

## Pixar's Distinctive Resources

Pixar brought:

- proprietary CG technology;
- creative talent;
- production systems;
- collaborative culture;
- superior recent animation economics.

Disney increasingly depended on Pixar as its own animation capability weakened.

## Existing Alliance

Disney and Pixar already created substantial value together.

Disney contributed financing, marketing, distribution, and exploitation across channels. Pixar contributed creative and production capability.

Therefore the first question is:

> Was the relationship worth more than independence?

The second is harder:

> **Could contracts continue to capture that value, or was acquisition necessary?**

## Ownership Test

Possible reasons ownership could matter:

- renegotiation risk;
- sequel rights;
- bargaining power;
- strategic dependence;
- long-term control of intellectual property;
- risk that Pixar chose another partner.

## Organization Test

Acquisition could destroy value if Disney damaged Pixar's:

- culture;
- autonomy;
- creative processes;
- key talent.

## Principle

> **A valuable partnership does not by itself justify acquisition. Acquisition is justified only when ownership solves a problem that contracts cannot solve at lower cost.**
