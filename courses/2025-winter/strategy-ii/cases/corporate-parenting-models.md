# Corporate Parenting Models

The course compares three successful but very different ways to own businesses.

## Danaher

Closest to **strategic planning / active operating improvement**.

Value comes from:

- disciplined target selection;
- Danaher Business System;
- process improvement;
- margin expansion;
- management practices;
- active portfolio shaping.

## Berkshire Hathaway

Closest to **financial control / capital allocation**.

Value comes from:

- operating autonomy;
- management selection;
- reputation;
- permanent capital;
- disciplined capital allocation.

The parent generally does not impose a single operating system.

## Berkshire Partners

Closest to **strategic control**.

As a PE owner it combines:

- disciplined underwriting;
- sector expertise;
- leverage;
- governance;
- management support;
- explicit value-creation plans;
- eventual exit.

## Comparison

| Question | Danaher | Berkshire Hathaway | Berkshire Partners |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate intervention | High | Low operationally | Moderate/selective |
| Primary edge | Operating system | Capital allocation / reputation | Investment process + governance |
| Holding period | Long / portfolio managed | Often permanent | Finite |
| Best target | Business improvable by DBS | Strong business/manager at good economics | Business with identifiable value-creation plan |

## Principle

> **There is no single superior parenting style. The parent must have a repeatable advantage that fits the needs of the businesses it owns.**
