# Core Definition

Corporate strategy is the set of choices around **scope, ownership, and organizational design** configured with corporate resources to create a sustainable **corporate advantage**.

A corporation has corporate advantage when the businesses it owns are worth more under that parent than they would be under feasible alternative owners or as standalone companies.

# The Three Dimensions

```text
Corporate Strategy
│
├── Scope
│   ├── Which businesses?
│   ├── Which geographies?
│   ├── Which value-chain stages?
│   ├── Enter / grow / exit?
│   └── Which mode of entry or exit?
│
├── Ownership
│   ├── Full ownership?
│   ├── Partial ownership / JV?
│   ├── Alliance / contract?
│   ├── Standalone?
│   └── Who is the best owner?
│
└── Organizational Design
    ├── What should HQ do?
    ├── What should stay decentralized?
    ├── Which activities/resources should be shared?
    └── How should capital, talent, and capabilities move?
```

# Three Tests of Corporate Strategy

## Better-Off Test
Are the businesses more valuable together than apart?

## Ownership Test
Does realizing that value actually require common ownership?

## Organization Test
Can the parent design the organization so that the potential value is actually realized?

# My High-Level Takeaways

1. Corporate strategy is not merely diversification strategy.
2. Corporate advantage is distinct from business-level competitive advantage.
3. The right question is not whether businesses are related, but whether this parent can add value to them.
4. Being the best owner is a demanding standard.
5. Synergy must exceed entry, integration, coordination, and corporate-center costs.
6. A better-off relationship may not require common ownership.
7. Vertical integration is justified when market contracting is sufficiently costly, risky, or ineffective.
8. Quasi-integration can preserve coordination benefits without full ownership.
9. Investor-level risk reduction alone is not a sufficient diversification rationale.
10. The conglomerate discount is not mechanical proof that diversification destroys value.
11. Corporate parents can add value through selection, restructuring, skill transfer, activity sharing, capital allocation, talent, and reputation.
12. Corporate parents can also destroy value through bureaucracy, bad targets, misguided synergies, poor capital allocation, and one-size-fits-all policies.
13. M&A should be evaluated as one mode among build, buy, and ally.
14. Different M&A motives require different integration approaches.
15. Acquisitions of people/culture-heavy capabilities can destroy the resource being purchased.
16. Cost synergies are often more controllable than revenue synergies, but technical incompatibility can make even cost synergies illusory.
17. Acquisition price determines who captures synergy.
18. Divestiture is an active strategic tool, not an admission of failure.
19. Corporate headquarters should do only what it can do better than business units or outside markets.
20. Financial control, strategic control, and strategic planning are different parenting models.
21. Capital allocation is a real corporate capability only when the parent allocates better than the alternatives.
22. Owners themselves can create or destroy strategic value.
23. Ownership advantage asks whether a particular ultimate owner improves competitive or corporate advantage.
24. Shared value is strongest when solving a social problem also improves the firm's competitive economics.
25. Disney/Pixar shows that relationship value and ownership necessity are separate questions.
26. Ayala shows why sum-of-parts analysis alone cannot determine corporate advantage.
27. Cadbury/Adams shows that strategic fit does not excuse overpaying or weak integration logic.
28. Humana shows that failure under common ownership does not prove integration is inherently wrong.
29. Danaher, Berkshire Hathaway, and Berkshire Partners show that successful parents can add value in radically different ways.
30. The best corporate strategy is a coherent parenting system, not simply a collection of attractive businesses.
