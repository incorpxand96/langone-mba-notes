# Portfolio and Capital Allocation

A parent can create value if it allocates scarce capital better than standalone boards or external markets.

## Common Failure

Capital is often allocated through:

- prior-year budgets;
- internal politics;
- business-unit cash generation;
- equal treatment.

This can trap capital in mature businesses.

## Strong Internal Capital Market

1. collect excess cash;
2. compare opportunities across businesses;
3. estimate future ROIC/growth;
4. account for entry cost and goodwill;
5. fund the best uses;
6. harvest or exit weak uses.

## Economic vs. Accounting Return

Existing businesses can look artificially attractive when book capital is low, while new deals include goodwill. Compare economic returns, not raw accounting returns.

## Principle

> **Internal capital markets are an advantage only when the parent allocates better than external markets and standalone boards would.**
