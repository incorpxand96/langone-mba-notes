# Personal Notes and Synthesis

## Three Questions

I found it useful to break corporate strategy into:

1. Where to compete?
2. What to own?
3. How to organize?

This prevents "good market" from becoming "must acquire."

## Synergy Skepticism

Decompose synergy into:

- revenue;
- cost;
- resource transfer;
- activity sharing;
- market-power effects.

Then ask:

- is it feasible?
- does ownership matter?
- who captures it?
- what coordination is required?

## Corporate Tradeoffs

More coordination can create scale, sharing, and learning.

It can also create bureaucracy, slow decisions, divisional conflict, and weak accountability.

## Vertical Integration

Full ownership should solve a real contracting/coordination problem, not serve as a prestige move.

## Diversification

Risk reduction alone is not corporate advantage.

The stronger question is:

> **Can this parent allocate capital, improve operations, transfer resources, or govern these businesses better than alternative owners?**

## M&A

My notes repeatedly flag:

- integration difficulty;
- culture;
- leadership;
- price;
- deal-team bias;
- optimistic revenue assumptions.

I also liked the staged question:

> Could an alliance that later becomes an acquisition be better than immediate full ownership?

## Strategy I → Strategy II

```text
Strategy I
    ↓
How does this business create competitive advantage?

Strategy II
    ↓
Why should this business be owned with these other businesses,
by this parent, under this governance structure?
```
