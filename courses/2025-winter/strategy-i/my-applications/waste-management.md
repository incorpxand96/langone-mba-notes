# My Waste Management Group Project

Our group analyzed Waste Management through:

- industry structure;
- competitive positioning;
- incumbent advantage;
- ongoing innovation.

---

# Industry Structure

Our Five Forces assessment characterized:

## Substitutes — Low

Waste management behaves much like a utility.

Alternatives such as:

- local composting;
- community recycling;
- zero-waste lifestyles

exist but have limited adoption.

## Buyer Power — Moderate

Municipal contracts can be long-lived, but:

- bidding is competitive;
- buyers can be price sensitive.

## New Entry — Low Threat

Barriers include:

- major capital requirements;
- scale;
- regulation;
- distribution/control of disposal infrastructure.

## Supplier Power — Low

Many inputs are standardized and Waste Management's size strengthens purchasing leverage.

## Rivalry — High

Core services can be commoditized.

Switching costs can be low and regional firms compete for contracts.

---

# Strategic Positioning

Our project argued that Waste Management differentiates through **value differentiation**, not merely scale.

Drivers include:

- advanced recycling;
- vertical integration;
- diverse ancillary services;
- waste-to-energy.

---

# Vertical Integration

Waste Management controls assets such as:

- large landfill network;
- transfer/deposit stations;
- recycling processing;
- measurement/reporting systems.

These assets support both:

- cost/control advantages;
- differentiated service.

---

# Sticky Relationships

Ancillary services can deepen customer relationships through:

- sustainability consulting;
- reporting;
- sophisticated billing;
- streamlined servicing;
- renewable-energy solutions.

This raises switching costs and WTP even in a relatively commoditized core business.

---

# Scale as a Resource

Scale matters because it supports:

- infrastructure ownership;
- bargaining power;
- capital-intensive innovation;
- geographic coverage.

That connects Five Forces to RBV.

The same barrier that limits entrants can become a valuable incumbent resource.

---

# Acquisitions

Our wrap-up emphasized that incumbents can acquire not only for cost synergies but also to:

- add channels/capabilities;
- remove emerging competitors;
- reinforce barriers.

---

# Our Key Takeaways

1. Commoditized industries can still support differentiated positions.
2. Capital intensity and scale can create durable barriers.
3. Incumbents must keep innovating even when structure favors them.
4. Acquisitions can be both offensive and defensive strategic tools.

---

# My Take

This project is a useful example of framework integration.

Five Forces explains:

> why the industry favors large incumbents.

Positioning/RBV explains:

> **why Waste Management may outperform other incumbents inside that structure.**
