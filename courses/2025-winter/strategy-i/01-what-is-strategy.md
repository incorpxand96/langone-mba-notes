# What Is Strategy?

Strategy is not the same thing as operational effectiveness.

## Operational Effectiveness

Operational effectiveness means:

> performing similar activities better than rivals.

Examples:

- faster production;
- higher quality;
- better forecasting;
- lower waste;
- better technology.

Operational effectiveness moves a firm toward the productivity frontier.

It is essential.

But best practices diffuse.

Competitors imitate.

The result can be convergence rather than durable advantage.

---

# Strategic Positioning

Strategic positioning means:

> **performing different activities from rivals, or performing similar activities in meaningfully different ways.**

A strategy is therefore a system of choices.

---

# Three Sources of Position

## Variety-Based

Serve a subset of products or services.

## Needs-Based

Serve most or all needs of a particular customer group.

## Access-Based

Serve customers who are best reached through a distinct configuration based on factors such as:

- geography;
- scale;
- channel;
- accessibility.

---

# Tradeoffs

A sustainable position requires saying **no**.

Tradeoffs arise because positions can demand different:

- product configurations;
- equipment;
- employee behavior;
- capabilities;
- management systems;
- brand images.

A competitor cannot always graft the attractive parts of another position onto its current model without creating inconsistency.

---

# Fit

Strategy is strengthened when activities reinforce one another.

Three forms:

1. consistency between activities and overall position;
2. activities reinforcing one another;
3. optimization of effort across activities.

Fit makes imitation harder because the rival must reproduce a system rather than a single practice.

---

# Growth Risk

Growth can weaken strategy when management responds by:

- broadening customer scope indiscriminately;
- adding incompatible services;
- eliminating tradeoffs;
- compromising the activity system.

A better question is:

> **How can we deepen the position?**

Possible answers:

- strengthen distinctive activities;
- deepen fit;
- communicate the existing value proposition more clearly.

---

# Leadership

Strategy requires choices.

Leadership matters because managers must:

- decide what not to do;
- protect tradeoffs;
- resist attractive-looking moves that weaken the system.

---

# My Take

Operational effectiveness answers:

> How well do we perform an activity?

Strategy answers:

> **Why are these the activities we have chosen to perform at all, and why do they fit together better for our chosen position?**
