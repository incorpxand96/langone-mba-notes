# Integrated Strategy Diagnosis

The most important meta-lesson from Strategy I is knowing **which level of strategy problem I am solving**.

---

# Step 1 — Define the Performance Problem

Examples:

- margins are falling;
- growth is weak;
- returns lag peers;
- a new entrant is taking share;
- a new market looks attractive;
- a platform is tipping away from us.

Do not begin with a framework.

Begin with the economic symptom.

---

# Step 2 — Industry Structure

Ask:

- Is rivalry intensifying?
- Is buyer power rising?
- Is supplier power rising?
- Has entry become easier?
- Are substitutes improving?

Use Five Forces when the problem is mainly about the environment.

---

# Step 3 — Competitive Position

Ask:

- Who are we serving?
- Which needs?
- Which products?
- Which geographies?
- What raises WTP?
- What lowers cost?
- Is our WTP–C wedge better?
- Are the activities consistent?

Use positioning when performance differences are firm-specific.

---

# Step 4 — Resources and Capabilities

Ask:

- What assets/capabilities make the position possible?
- Are they valuable in this environment?
- Are they scarce?
- Can rivals copy or substitute?
- Are we organized to exploit them?
- Who captures the value?

---

# Step 5 — Platform Dynamics

If the business mediates groups, ask:

- who are the sides?
- what transaction cost is reduced?
- what network effects exist?
- who should be subsidized?
- how costly is multi-homing?
- could the market tip?
- could an adjacent platform envelop us?

---

# Step 6 — Rival Response

Ask:

- who will react?
- what actions are available?
- what does retaliation cost them?
- which threats are credible?
- can our advantage survive imitation or response?

---

# Step 7 — Financial Consistency

Test the strategic story against:

- margins;
- asset turns;
- ROIC/ROA;
- working capital;
- capital requirements.

A strategy story should eventually show up in economics.

---

# Strategy as a System

A strong strategy should exhibit consistency among:

```text
Industry environment
        ↓
Chosen position
        ↓
Activities
        ↓
Resources / capabilities
        ↓
Rival constraints
        ↓
Financial outcomes
```

---

# My Take

The frameworks are best thought of as a **diagnostic stack**.

A good strategy analysis does not say:

> "Five Forces is high, RBV is strong, network effects are medium."

It tells a causal story:

> **this environment rewards these choices; this firm can make those choices because of these capabilities; rivals cannot profitably copy the system; therefore the firm can create and capture superior value.**
