# Rivalry, Game Theory, and Strategic Interaction

The central question:

> **How do competitive interactions affect profitability?**

Strategy differs from many business analyses because the environment can react to the decision.

---

# Interdependence

A pricing decision changes:

- customer behavior;
- competitor incentives;
- competitor actions.

The correct analysis is therefore not:

> What happens if we do X?

but:

> **If we do X, what will rivals rationally do, and what happens after that?**

---

# Game-Theory Workflow

From my reading notes:

## 1. Formulate the Problem

Be explicit about the decision.

## 2. Build the Model

Identify:

- players;
- actions;
- timing;
- information;
- strategies;
- payoffs.

## 3. Return to the Business Context

Test the model result against:

- actual incentives;
- operational constraints;
- organizational goals;
- uncertainty.

A simplified model disciplines intuition but does not replace judgment.

---

# Credible Threats

A threat matters only if carrying it out is rational or feasible.

Questions:

- Does the incumbent have the cost structure to retaliate?
- Would retaliation hurt the incumbent more than accommodation?
- Does the incumbent have non-profit motives?
- Is reputation across markets important?

---

# Ryanair Launch

Early Ryanair entered Dublin–London with:

- low fares;
- but a service/activity configuration still resembling full-service incumbents.

That is strategically dangerous.

If a new entrant competes on the incumbent's activity system while charging much less, the economics may not sustain the price.

---

# Ryanair Turnaround

The later strategy became distinctive:

- drop loss-making routes;
- redeploy aircraft;
- eliminate frills;
- cut costs aggressively;
- lower fares;
- add ancillary revenue;
- grow the market.

The difference was not merely "cheaper price."

It was:

> **a different operating system supporting the price position.**

---

# Rival Response

BA and Aer Lingus had difficulty matching the low fare economically and shifted toward full-fare business travelers.

This illustrates a powerful strategic outcome:

> a tradeoff can make a rival's rational response something other than imitation.

---

# Quantitative Rivalry

The course also emphasizes that strategic interaction can be modeled numerically.

But quantitative conclusions depend on assumptions about:

- elasticity;
- cost structure;
- capacity;
- rival objectives.

Numbers discipline the debate; they do not eliminate strategic uncertainty.

---

# My Take

Rivalry analysis forces strategy to become dynamic.

Competitive advantage is not:

> a position that works if nobody reacts.

It is:

> **a position that remains attractive after reasonable rival responses are considered.**
