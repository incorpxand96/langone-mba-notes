# Cross-Course Connections

Strategy I connects strongly to several concept trees already in the repository.

---

# Financial Accounting — Strategy in the Numbers

Competitive positioning can show up through:

- gross margin;
- SG&A;
- asset turnover;
- ROA/ROIC;
- working capital.

The course explicitly uses supermarket ratios to distinguish strategic paths.

A low-cost firm may create strong returns through:

- low margin;
- high asset turns.

A differentiated firm may carry:

- higher operating expense;
- but higher WTP and margin.

---

# Finance — Value Creation and Capture

The WTP–C framework asks how much economic value the business creates.

Finance asks what future cash flows investors value.

The bridge is:

- value creation;
- value capture;
- capital requirements;
- durability;
- risk.

---

# Operations — Activity Systems

Porter's "fit" concept connects directly to Operations.

Strategy says:

> choose a distinctive system of activities.

Operations asks:

> how does that system actually work?

Cases such as Trader Joe's and Ryanair show how:

- process;
- variety;
- capacity;
- sourcing;
- service

support the position.

---

# Leadership / Organizations — Capabilities

RBV treats some organizational capabilities as strategic resources.

Examples:

- culture;
- routines;
- coordination;
- innovation systems.

These can be hard to imitate because of causal ambiguity and path dependence.

---

# Entrepreneurship — Market Selection

Five Forces helps evaluate:

- market attractiveness;
- structural profit pools.

RBV warns against entering an attractive market without a defensible advantage.

For search/ETA, the useful combined question is:

> **Is this a structurally attractive business, and does the target possess a position/resource set that can actually capture the attractiveness?**
