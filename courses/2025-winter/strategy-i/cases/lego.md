# LEGO

LEGO links:

- industry change;
- positioning;
- resources;
- scope;
- operating complexity.

---

# Environmental Change

The toy market shifted through:

- shorter product life cycles;
- fad products;
- technology;
- retailer power;
- faster imitation;
- changing child behavior.

LEGO had to adapt.

---

# Overexpansion

LEGO expanded into:

- theme parks;
- video games;
- media;
- apparel;
- watches;
- licensed properties;
- more complex brick designs.

The problem was not "innovation is bad."

The problem was that expansion created:

- manufacturing complexity;
- inventory problems;
- design complexity;
- forecasting difficulty;
- cost growth;
- cannibalization.

---

# Core Resources

The turnaround refocused on resources such as:

- LEGO brand;
- brick;
- system of play;
- loyal community;
- manufacturing/materials expertise;
- creative culture;
- retailer relationships.

---

# Contextual Value

The case emphasizes that resources are valuable only in context.

A strong historic brand does not automatically create advantage in every adjacent market.

---

# Turnaround Sequence

The lecture summarizes:

1. manage for cash;
2. manage for value;
3. manage for growth.

That ordering matters.

Growth is dangerous before the business understands:

- what is core;
- where value comes from;
- which capabilities matter.

---

# Scope

The strategic question is not:

> What markets can our brand enter?

It is:

> **Where do our distinctive resources allow us to create and capture superior value?**

---

# Durable Lesson

> **Growth that stretches beyond the economic domain of a firm's distinctive resources can destroy rather than leverage advantage.**
