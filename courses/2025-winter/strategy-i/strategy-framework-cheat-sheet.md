# Strategy Framework Cheat Sheet

## Fundamental Question

`Why are some firms more successful than others?`

---

# Five Forces

Use for:

> industry-average profitability

Forces:

- rivalry;
- buyers;
- suppliers;
- entry;
- substitutes.

---

# WTP–C

`Value Created = WTP − Cost`

Differentiation:

`WTP ↑`

Low cost:

`C ↓`

Question:

> did the wedge expand?

---

# Positioning

Three classic position sources:

- variety-based;
- needs-based;
- access-based.

Sustainability requires:

- tradeoffs;
- fit.

---

# Resource Analysis

Ask:

- valuable?
- rare?
- difficult to imitate?
- organized?

Also:

- durable?
- appropriable?
- substitutable?
- superior to rivals?

---

# Platform Diagnosis

Ask:

- who are the sides?
- what exchange is facilitated?
- what transaction cost is reduced?
- what cross-side effects exist?
- what same-side effects exist?
- who should be subsidized?
- how costly is multi-homing?
- could it tip?
- is envelopment a threat?

---

# Rivalry / Game Theory

Specify:

- players;
- actions;
- timing;
- information;
- strategies;
- payoffs.

Test:

- credible response?
- sustainable economics?
- dynamic equilibrium?

---

# Integrated Strategy Sentence

A strong strategic argument should be expressible as:

> **Because the industry rewards ___, this firm chooses ___, which creates a superior WTP–C wedge through ___; it can sustain that wedge because of ___ resources/activity fit, while rivals are constrained by ___.**
