# Resources and Capabilities

The Resource-Based View asks:

> **What firm-specific assets and capabilities explain performance differences?**

Resources can include:

- physical assets;
- intangible assets;
- organizational capabilities.

---

# Resource Value Is Contextual

A resource is not strategically valuable in isolation.

Its value depends on:

- the market;
- industry structure;
- customers;
- rivals;
- technology;
- complementary resources.

Therefore:

> **Resource value is contextual rather than inherent.**

---

# VRIO Logic

The course uses a progression similar to:

1. **Valuable**
2. **Rare**
3. **Difficult to imitate**
4. **Organized to execute**

Implications:

- not valuable → disadvantage;
- valuable but common → parity;
- valuable + rare but easy to imitate → temporary advantage;
- valuable + rare + hard to imitate but poorly organized → unused advantage;
- all four → potential sustained advantage.

---

# Why Resources Are Hard to Copy

Sources include:

## Physical Uniqueness

- location;
- patents;
- exclusive rights.

## Path Dependence

Capabilities developed over time.

## Causal Ambiguity

Rivals cannot easily identify what produces the advantage.

This is common with:

- culture;
- routines;
- organizational capabilities.

## Economic Deterrence

Replication requires a large, market-specific investment that a rival may not rationally make.

---

# Additional Resource Tests

The personal reading notes add several useful questions:

## Durability

How quickly does the resource depreciate?

## Appropriability

Who captures the value created by the resource?

## Substitutability

Can another resource produce the same strategic outcome?

## Competitive Superiority

Is our resource actually better than rivals' alternatives?

---

# LEGO

LEGO's strategically important resources included:

- brand;
- customer loyalty;
- nostalgia;
- the LEGO brick/system of play;
- manufacturing and materials-science capabilities;
- creative culture;
- retailer relationships.

Its crisis came partly from expanding into activities that did not adequately leverage the distinctive core while adding complexity and cost.

---

# Resource Leverage Trap

Do not assume a resource is transferable simply because it worked in one market.

Common mistakes:

1. overestimate transferability;
2. enter attractive industries without a real advantage;
3. treat generic capabilities as unique sources of advantage.

---

# Resource Maintenance

Managers must:

- invest;
- upgrade;
- prevent depreciation;
- monitor imitation;
- reassess scope.

A resource advantage can disappear even when the underlying asset remains physically present.

---

# My Take

RBV complements Five Forces.

Five Forces asks:

> What does the environment reward?

RBV asks:

> **What do we possess or know how to do that lets us capture that opportunity better than rivals?**
