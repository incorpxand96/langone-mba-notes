# Industry Analysis and Five Forces

The central question:

> **Why are firms in some industries more profitable on average than firms in others?**

Porter's Five Forces analyzes the structural distribution of bargaining power and competitive pressure.

---

# The Five Forces

1. **Industry rivalry**
2. **Buyer power**
3. **Supplier power**
4. **Threat of entry**
5. **Threat of substitutes**

Industry profitability is shaped by the balance among them.

A key course warning:

> **Even one "bad" force can be enough to drain substantial profit from an industry.**

Do not simply add up favorable and unfavorable forces.

---

# Rivalry

Rivalry tends to be stronger when:

- many capable competitors exist;
- growth is slow;
- differentiation is weak;
- switching costs are low;
- fixed costs are high;
- exit barriers are high.

Price competition is especially destructive because it transfers value directly to customers.

---

# Buyer Power

Buyers gain power when:

- they are concentrated;
- purchases are large;
- offerings are undifferentiated;
- switching costs are low;
- backward integration is credible;
- buyers are price sensitive.

Important:

> consider the whole chain of customers.

An intermediary with high bargaining power may capture value even if the end consumer is fragmented.

---

# Supplier Power

Suppliers gain power when:

- they are concentrated;
- inputs are differentiated;
- switching suppliers is costly;
- substitutes for the input are weak;
- forward integration is credible;
- the buyer is not an important customer.

---

# Threat of Entry

Entry pressure depends on barriers such as:

- economies of scale;
- capital requirements;
- network effects;
- switching costs;
- proprietary technology;
- access to distribution;
- regulation;
- incumbent response.

Entry need not occur for the threat to affect current profitability.

---

# Substitutes

A substitute solves the same underlying customer problem through a different product or service.

Threat rises when the substitute offers an attractive price/performance tradeoff and switching is easy.

---

# What Five Forces Is For

Use it to:

- explain industry-average profitability;
- identify the most economically important structural changes;
- understand why an existing position is good or bad;
- identify opportunities to reshape industry structure;
- inform positioning.

---

# What Five Forces Is Not

It is not:

- a checklist;
- a 1-to-5 scoring exercise;
- a prediction that every firm in the industry earns the same return;
- a substitute for firm-level analysis.

Industry structure and competitive advantage answer different questions.

---

# Apple Lesson

Apple's early Mac product advantage existed inside a difficult PC industry.

Its economics were affected by:

- falling hardware prices;
- large Windows installed base;
- complement/software availability;
- operating-system development economics.

The lesson:

> **The value system around the product can matter more than the product's isolated attributes.**
