# Capital, Debt, and Time Value of Money

This portion of the book overlaps most directly with Foundations of Finance.

---

# Capital Sources

Companies choose among:

- private/public capital;
- debt/equity;
- short-term/long-term funding.

---

# Debt

Debt can differ by:

- maturity;
- seniority;
- security/collateral;
- covenant structure;
- public vs. private placement;
- credit quality.

Examples:

- commercial paper;
- revolving credit;
- term loans;
- bonds.

---

# Equity

Important concepts include:

- issued shares;
- treasury shares;
- outstanding shares;
- paid-in capital;
- share classes;
- voting rights;
- preferred shares.

Book value / par value should not be confused with market value.

---

# Dividends vs. Buybacks

Both return capital to shareholders.

They have different:

- accounting treatment;
- tax consequences;
- signaling implications;
- share-count effects.

---

# Time Value of Money

The book uses the same core principle as Foundations of Finance:

> **money today is worth more than an equal nominal amount received later.**

`FV = PV × (1+r)^n`

`PV = FV / (1+r)^n`

---

# Interest Rate Drivers

The book identifies drivers including:

- inflation;
- demand for capital;
- supply of capital / time preference;
- default probability;
- loss given default;
- administrative costs;
- lender profit margin.

This reinforces that interest rate is an economic price, not merely an accounting input.

---

# APR vs. EAR

APR is an annualized simple rate.

EAR includes compounding.

With multiple compounding periods:

`EAR = (1 + APR/m)^m − 1`

---

# Bonds

A bond's:

- coupon rate determines promised periodic cash payments;
- discount rate / required yield determines present value.

These are not the same.

## Price vs. Face Value

- coupon rate > required yield → price above face value;
- coupon rate < required yield → price below face value.

---

# Accounting for Debt

A bond issued away from face value accretes/amortizes toward face value over time.

Interest expense is based on the effective required rate applied to the carrying amount, not simply the coupon cash payment.

---

# Finance Connection

Accounting tracks:

- issue price;
- cash interest;
- interest expense;
- carrying value.

Finance explains why those values exist:

- discounting;
- required return;
- market price.

This is a strong example of accounting measurement sitting on top of valuation logic.
