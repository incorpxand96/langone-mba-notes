# Costs, Inventories, and PP&E

The central question is:

> **When should a cost become an expense?**

The answer depends on whether the resource still has measurable future economic benefit.

---

# Cost vs. Expense vs. Payment

A payment is a cash event.

A cost is the amount sacrificed to obtain a resource.

An expense is the portion of cost recognized when the resource is consumed.

These can occur in different periods.

---

# Capitalization vs. Expensing

## Expense Immediately

When the resource has no remaining measurable future benefit.

## Capitalize

When the resource is expected to provide future benefit and can be measured reliably.

Capitalization does not eliminate the expense.

It defers recognition.

---

# Inventory

Inventory is a deferred cost.

In manufacturing, inventoriable costs include:

- direct materials;
- direct labor;
- manufacturing overhead.

The cost becomes COGS when the inventory is sold.

---

# FIFO vs. LIFO

Cost-flow assumptions determine which costs flow into:

- COGS;
- ending inventory.

They need not match physical flow.

When inventory quantities and costs are rising:

## FIFO

- ending inventory reflects more recent costs;
- COGS contains relatively older costs;
- reported gross margin tends to be higher.

## LIFO

- COGS reflects more current costs;
- ending inventory contains older costs;
- taxable income may be deferred where LIFO is permitted.

---

# PP&E

PP&E is another deferred cost.

Initial purchase:

- cash decreases;
- PP&E increases;
- no immediate operating expense.

Over time:

- depreciation recognizes the cost of using the asset.

---

# Depreciation

Depreciation is an allocation estimate, not a market-value measurement.

It depends on assumptions about:

- useful life;
- salvage value;
- depreciation method.

Net book value is therefore an accounting measure, not necessarily market value.

---

# Manufacturing Depreciation

Depreciation of manufacturing equipment can initially enter inventory and later become COGS when the inventory is sold.

This explains why:

- depreciation expense on the income statement;
- D&A adjustments on the cash flow statement

can differ.

---

# Disposal

When PP&E is sold:

- cash proceeds are investing cash flow;
- the asset and accumulated depreciation are removed;
- gain/loss compares sale proceeds with net book value.

A gain is not the same thing as sale proceeds.

---

# My Take

Capitalization is fundamentally a timing judgment:

> **Does this cost create a measurable future benefit that should be recognized as expense later?**
