# Cross-Course Connections

Accounting and Foundations of Finance answer different pieces of the same business problem.

---

# Accounting

Accounting tells me:

- what happened economically;
- when income was recognized;
- when cash moved;
- what assets and liabilities remain;
- how much capital is tied up;
- how operating performance differs from financing effects.

---

# Finance

Finance asks:

- what future cash flows are expected;
- when they occur;
- how risky they are;
- what opportunity cost of capital applies;
- what those cash flows are worth today.

---

# Accrual Accounting → Cash Flow

Accounting converts operating events into:

- earnings;
- balance-sheet timing differences;
- cash flows.

This creates the starting point for valuation.

---

# Working Capital → Free Cash Flow

Revenue growth can increase:

- receivables;
- inventory.

Those uses of capital reduce near-term cash flow.

Therefore revenue and EBITDA growth do not automatically translate one-for-one into free cash flow.

---

# Capital Expenditure → Depreciation

Accounting separates:

- upfront investing cash flow;
- later depreciation expense.

Finance cares about both:

- capex determines cash investment;
- depreciation affects accounting earnings and taxes.

---

# ROIC → Value Creation

ROIC measures the operating return on invested capital.

Finance supplies the comparison benchmark:

> **the opportunity cost of capital**

The most important valuation question is therefore not simply:

> Is ROIC high?

but:

> **Is ROIC greater than the required return on comparable-risk capital?**

---

# Leverage → Equity Risk and Return

Accounting decomposition shows how debt can increase ROE when ROIC exceeds borrowing cost.

Finance adds:

- leverage also increases equity risk;
- a higher expected return is not automatically higher value.

---

# Multiples

Accounting creates denominators such as:

- earnings;
- book value;
- sales;
- EBITDA.

Finance explains why market multiples differ through:

- growth;
- risk;
- required return;
- capital intensity;
- payout.

A multiple is therefore a compressed valuation model.

---

# Business Drivers Connection

The book's final framework maps naturally to business-driver analysis:

- volume / turnover;
- margin;
- capital intensity;
- working capital;
- leverage;
- financing cost;
- tax;
- growth.

Those drivers ultimately determine:

- cash generation;
- returns;
- valuation.
