# Unlevered vs. Levered Analysis

Gode separates:

- enterprise / operating activity;
- financial activity.

This prevents financing choices from being confused with operating performance.

---

# Enterprise vs. Financial Activity

## Enterprise

Value-creating activity such as:

- producing;
- selling;
- serving customers.

## Financial

Activities that fund or hold capital:

- debt;
- interest;
- excess cash;
- investments.

In frictionless markets, financing itself is not the core source of enterprise value.

---

# Invested Capital

`Invested Capital = Equity + Financial Liabilities − Financial Assets`

Equivalent to net operating assets.

---

# ROIC

Unlevered return:

`ROIC = Operating Profit After Tax / Invested Capital`

ROIC asks:

> **How well is the enterprise using the capital invested in operations?**

---

# ROE

Levered return:

`ROE = Net Income / Equity`

ROE includes the consequences of:

- operations;
- debt;
- cash holdings;
- interest rates.

---

# Leverage

Debt provides more capital than shareholders contributed.

If operating return exceeds the cost of debt:

- leverage raises ROE.

If operating return falls below the cost of debt:

- leverage reduces ROE.

A simplified relationship:

`ROE = ROIC + (ROIC − rd) × Debt/Equity`

With both financial assets and liabilities, the book generalizes the equation further.

---

# Risk

Higher ROE from leverage is not free performance.

Debt creates fixed interest expense.

That makes equity earnings more sensitive to changes in operating income.

Therefore:

> **Leverage amplifies variability.**

Good times become better for equity.

Bad times become worse.

---

# Liquidity

Financial assets provide fixed/low-volatility interest income.

Cash behaves economically like **negative debt**:

- lowers volatility;
- lowers risk;
- usually lowers expected return.

---

# Operating Leverage

The same principle applies to fixed operating costs.

Fixed costs make operating income more volatile than sales.

Financial leverage then makes equity income still more volatile.

---

# My Take

Comparing ROE across companies without considering leverage can be misleading.

The first question should be:

> **What return is the operating business generating before financial structure changes the distribution to equity?**
