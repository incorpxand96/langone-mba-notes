# Funding Needs and Working Capital

This is one of the most practically useful sections of the book.

An entity needs funding whenever:

> **cash payments occur before the associated receipts arrive.**

Funding need can arise from:

- operating losses;
- profitable investments with upfront cash needs;
- timing differences.

Investors generally prefer financing productive timing differences to financing persistent economic losses.

---

# Operating Assets Increase Funding Needs

Examples:

- receivables;
- inventory;
- prepayments;
- PP&E.

Why?

They represent:

- delayed receipts; or
- payments made in advance of future benefit.

---

# Operating Liabilities Reduce Funding Needs

Examples:

- accounts payable;
- accrued expenses;
- deferred revenue.

Why?

They represent:

- delayed payments; or
- advance receipts.

---

# Important Tradeoff

Funding needs should be **optimized**, not blindly minimized.

Examples:

## Inventory

Less inventory:

- lowers funding need;
- may increase ordering cost;
- may create stockouts.

## Receivables

Tighter credit:

- accelerates cash receipt;
- may reduce sales.

## Payables

Longer payment terms:

- reduce funding needs;
- suppliers may charge more.

## PP&E

Renting can reduce upfront funding need.

Owning may be cheaper economically over time.

---

# Operating Working Capital

Gode distinguishes:

`Operating Working Capital = Current Operating Assets − Current Operating Liabilities`

from generic working capital:

`Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities`

The latter mixes:

- operating;
- financing

items.

---

# Net Operating Assets

`Net Operating Assets = Operating Assets − Operating Liabilities`

These assets are the net amount that must be funded by invested capital.

---

# Invested Capital

Sources of invested capital:

`Invested Capital = Equity + Financial Liabilities − Financial Assets`

Financial assets are treated as uninvested capital / negative debt because they can be used to repay debt and generally earn lower returns than operating assets.

Thus:

`Net Operating Assets = Invested Capital`

conceptually ties operating uses of funding to capital-provider sources.

---

# Growth and Funding

Growth often requires additional:

- receivables;
- inventory;
- PP&E.

Therefore profitable growth can consume cash.

A company can report:

- rising revenue;
- positive earnings;

while still needing external capital.

---

# My Take

Working capital is not an accounting nuisance.

It is the operating system's **short-duration capital requirement**.

That makes it central to:

- cash flow;
- valuation;
- credit;
- acquisition underwriting;
- operational improvement.
