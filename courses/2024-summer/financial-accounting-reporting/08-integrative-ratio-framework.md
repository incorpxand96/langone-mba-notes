# The Integrative Ratio Framework

The final chapter tries to connect operating ratios, leverage, payout, growth, and valuation rather than treating ratios as independent statistics.

That is the main value of the framework.

---

# Operating Drivers

## Invested Capital Turnover

`Sales / Invested Capital`

Measures volume generated per dollar of invested capital.

## Operating Margin

`EBIT / Sales`

Measures operating profit generated per dollar of sales.

Together:

`Pre-Tax ROIC = Operating Margin × Invested Capital Turnover`

This is a powerful decomposition:

> **Return = margin × asset efficiency**

---

# More Detailed Operating Drivers

Examples:

- gross margin;
- expense ratio;
- receivables turnover;
- inventory turnover;
- payables turnover;
- PP&E turnover.

These help explain **why** overall capital turnover and margins changed.

---

# Benefit of Leverage

`Invested Capital / Equity`

Debt lets shareholders control a larger operating capital base.

But this is only the benefit side.

---

# Cost of Leverage

The framework compares EBT with EBIT.

Interest expense reduces operating profit available to equity holders.

Interest coverage is therefore both:

- a risk measure;
- a measure of leverage burden.

Leverage is beneficial to ROE when operating return exceeds the effective debt cost.

---

# Taxes

After-tax ROE reflects:

- operating performance;
- leverage;
- financing cost;
- tax rate.

---

# Payout and Growth

For equity:

`Beginning Book Equity + Net Income − Dividends = Ending Book Equity`

If book equity grows at rate `g`:

`Dividend Payout Ratio = 1 − g/ROE`

Interpretation:

- higher growth consumes more retained earnings;
- high growth can require additional capital if growth exceeds internally generated return.

---

# Valuation Multiples

The framework connects accounting returns with market multiples.

A particularly important result:

> **Growth creates value only when return on equity exceeds the opportunity cost of equity.**

Similarly:

- high ROE can support a higher price-to-book ratio;
- high growth is not automatically valuable;
- risk and required return remain part of valuation.

---

# Why This Matters

A ratio should not be interpreted alone.

Example:

High ROE could reflect:

- stronger margins;
- better asset turnover;
- more leverage;
- lower financing cost;
- lower taxes.

Those are economically different stories.

---

# My Take

The point of ratio analysis is not to compute more ratios.

It is to create a **causal bridge** from:

```text
Operations
   ↓
Capital efficiency
   ↓
Operating return
   ↓
Financing structure
   ↓
Equity return
   ↓
Growth / payout
   ↓
Market value
```
