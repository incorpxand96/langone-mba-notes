# Accruals, Deferrals, and Timing

Accrual accounting separates **economic activity** from **cash timing**.

This is the conceptual center of the book.

---

# Revenue vs. Receipt

A receipt is cash coming in.

Revenue is income recognized from primary business activity.

They can occur at different times.

## Revenue Before Receipt

Example:

- perform work now;
- collect cash later.

Accounting consequence:

- recognize revenue now;
- create a receivable.

Receivables are future receipts created by past revenue.

## Receipt Before Revenue

Example:

- customer pays in advance;
- company performs later.

Accounting consequence:

- receive cash now;
- create deferred revenue;
- recognize revenue later.

Deferred revenue is an obligation to perform or refund.

---

# Expense vs. Payment

A payment is cash going out.

An expense is recognition that resources have been consumed.

## Expense Before Payment

Example:

- employees work now;
- salaries are paid later.

Accounting consequence:

- recognize expense now;
- create a payable/accrued expense.

## Payment Before Expense

Example:

- prepaid insurance;
- prepaid services;
- inventory;
- PP&E.

Accounting consequence:

- pay cash now;
- record an asset;
- recognize expense later as the resource is consumed.

---

# Balance Sheet as Timing Storage

Many operating balance-sheet accounts are simply accumulated timing differences.

## Assets

- receivables → revenue recognized before receipt;
- prepayments → payment before expense;
- inventory → payment/cost before expense;
- PP&E → payment/cost before depreciation expense.

## Liabilities

- payables → expense before payment;
- deferred revenue → receipt before revenue.

---

# Financing Is Different

Borrowing and repaying principal are not accrual timing differences between revenue/expense and cash.

They are financing events.

Similarly:

- shareholder contributions are not revenue;
- dividends/buybacks are not expenses.

---

# Why Cash Flow Still Matters

If accrual income measures economic performance, why examine cash?

Because cash flows help assess:

- liquidity;
- solvency;
- financing activity;
- earnings quality;
- whether accruals/deferrals are becoming excessive.

---

# My Take

Accrual accounting does not "ignore cash."

It answers a different question.

- income statement → when was value economically created/consumed?
- cash flow statement → when did cash actually move?
- balance sheet → what timing differences remain unresolved?
