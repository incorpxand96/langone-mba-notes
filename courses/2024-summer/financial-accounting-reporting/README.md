# Core Accounting Logic

The course repeatedly separates concepts that are easy to blur together:

```text
Economic activity
      ↓
Income recognition
      ↓
Balance-sheet changes
      ↓
Cash receipts / payments
      ↓
Funding consequences
      ↓
Returns to capital providers
```

The most important discipline is to distinguish:

- revenue from cash receipt;
- expense from cash payment;
- operating activity from financing activity;
- accounting profit from cash generation;
- operating return from leverage-driven equity return.

# The Four Statements

## Balance Sheet

A stock statement at a point in time.

`Assets = Liabilities + Equity`

Equity is the residual:

`Equity = Assets − Liabilities`

## Income Statement

A flow statement measuring changes in equity generated by income-producing activity.

At a basic level:

`Net Income = Revenues − Expenses + Gains − Losses`

## Cash Flow Statement

A flow statement explaining changes in cash through:

- operating;
- investing;
- financing

activities.

## Statement of Changes in Equity

Explains changes in equity through:

- net income;
- other comprehensive income;
- shareholder contributions;
- shareholder distributions.

# My High-Level Takeaways

1. **Cash receipts are not automatically revenues.**
2. **Cash payments are not automatically expenses.**
3. **Accrual accounting separates economic activity from cash timing.**
4. **The balance sheet stores timing differences between income and cash flows.**
5. **The financial statements form one articulated system rather than four independent reports.**
6. **Double-entry bookkeeping records both the cause and effect of a transaction.**
7. **Capitalization is deferred expense recognition, not disappearance of cost.**
8. **Depreciation is not a source of cash.**
9. **Inventory and PP&E consume funding before they generate future receipts.**
10. **Operating working capital is an economic funding need, not merely a balance-sheet subtotal.**
11. **Working-capital minimization is not always optimal; timing improvements can carry economic costs.**
12. **Operating assets and liabilities should be separated from financial assets and liabilities.**
13. **ROIC measures operating return before financing choices; ROE reflects both operations and financing.**
14. **Leverage amplifies both good and bad operating outcomes.**
15. **Cash/liquidity acts economically like negative debt: lower risk, but usually lower expected return.**
16. **Higher ROE is not automatically better performance if it came from more leverage.**
17. **Ratio analysis becomes more useful when ratios are connected causally rather than viewed in isolation.**
18. **Growth creates value only when returns on incremental capital exceed the relevant opportunity cost.**
19. **Accounting gives the operating and cash-flow inputs; finance converts them into market value.**

# Course-to-Finance Bridge

The strongest overlap with Foundations of Finance is:

```text
Accounting
    ↓
Measures operating activity
    ↓
Explains cash-flow timing and capital needs
    ↓
Separates operating from financing effects
    ↓
Produces cash-flow / return inputs

Finance
    ↓
Adds timing + risk + opportunity cost of capital
    ↓
Converts expected cash flows into value
```
