# Cross-Course Finance Connections

This file captures the parts of Foundations of Finance most useful outside the course.

---

# 1. Accounting Gives Cash-Flow Inputs; Finance Converts Them to Value

Accounting helps describe:

- earnings;
- assets;
- liabilities;
- cash flows;
- working capital;
- capital expenditure.

Finance asks:

> What are those future cash flows worth today?

The bridge is discounting.

---

# 2. Opportunity Cost of Capital

The discount rate should reflect what investors could earn in comparable-risk alternatives.

Therefore value depends on:

- cash-flow magnitude;
- cash-flow timing;
- cash-flow risk;
- market opportunity set.

---

# 3. NPV as a Decision Rule

The corporate-finance logic is:

`NPV = PV of future cash flows − Initial Investment`

If:

`NPV > 0`

the project creates value relative to the required return.

This is a natural bridge to Business Drivers because operating decisions ultimately matter through their effect on future cash flow and risk.

---

# 4. CAPM and Cost of Capital

CAPM provides one theoretical way to estimate a risk-adjusted required equity return.

This can be used as:

- a hurdle rate;
- an input to project valuation;
- an input to broader cost-of-capital calculations.

The important conceptual distinction is that the relevant risk is systematic, not simply standalone volatility.

---

# 5. Working Capital and Timing

Two businesses with identical reported profit can have different values if:

- one converts profit to cash sooner;
- one requires heavier inventory;
- one collects receivables more slowly;
- one requires more reinvestment.

TVM turns operating timing into valuation consequences.

---

# 6. Balance-Sheet Risk

Fixed-income duration provides a way to think about the economic sensitivity of:

- assets;
- liabilities;
- equity

to interest-rate changes.

This matters even when accounting values do not immediately show the full economic effect.

---

# 7. Market Multiples vs. Intrinsic Value

Even when using market multiples, finance logic remains relevant:

- the multiple embeds expectations about future cash flows;
- risk;
- growth;
- discount rates.

A multiple is a compressed valuation model, not an alternative to valuation logic.

---

# 8. Arbitrage as a Consistency Check

Law of One Price is useful far beyond trading.

Whenever two structures produce economically identical future cash flows, large persistent price differences require explanation.

That mindset is useful in:

- capital structure;
- financing alternatives;
- leases vs. purchases;
- derivative overlays;
- acquisition structures.
