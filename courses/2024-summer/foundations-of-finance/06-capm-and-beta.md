# CAPM and Beta

The Capital Asset Pricing Model links expected return to **systematic risk**.

---

# Market Portfolio

Under CAPM assumptions, all investors hold combinations of:

- a risk-free asset;
- the same tangency portfolio.

In equilibrium, the common tangency portfolio must be the market portfolio.

---

# Beta

`βi = Cov(Ri, RM) / Var(RM)`

Beta measures how much a security contributes to market risk.

Interpretation:

- `β = 1` → moves with market risk;
- `β > 1` → more market-sensitive;
- `β < 1` → less market-sensitive;
- `β = 0` → no systematic market exposure.

---

# CAPM Equation

`E(Ri) = Rf + βi [E(RM) − Rf]`

Expected return equals:

- risk-free return;
- plus compensation for systematic risk.

---

# Systematic vs. Idiosyncratic Risk

Total risk can be separated into:

## Systematic

Common market risk.

Cannot be diversified away.

Priced in CAPM.

## Idiosyncratic

Security-specific risk.

Can be diversified away.

Not rewarded with additional expected return under CAPM.

---

# Key Insight

CAPM does **not** say higher total volatility automatically produces higher expected return.

The relevant risk is beta.

A highly volatile company can have substantial diversifiable risk.

---

# Security Market Line

CAPM implies a linear relation between:

- beta;
- expected return.

An asset above or below that line appears to offer abnormal expected return relative to CAPM.

---

# Alpha

In a return regression:

`Ri − Rf = αi + βi(RM − Rf) + error`

CAPM implies:

`α = 0`

A positive estimated alpha is interpreted as return not explained by market beta, though inference depends on the model and data.

---

# Corporate Finance Application

CAPM can provide a risk-adjusted required return for a project.

That required return becomes a discount rate in NPV analysis.

Managerial logic:

> **Undertake positive-NPV projects because they increase firm value.**

This is one of the strongest links between asset pricing and corporate valuation.
