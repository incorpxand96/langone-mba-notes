# Financial Markets and Securities

Financial markets perform three broad economic functions.

## 1. Consumption Smoothing

Move purchasing power through time through:

- saving;
- borrowing;
- liquidity provision.

## 2. Capital Allocation

Move funds from providers of capital toward productive projects.

## 3. Risk Allocation

Move or redistribute risk through:

- diversification;
- hedging;
- contract design.

---

# Basic Market Framework

A market includes:

- securities;
- issuers;
- investors;
- pricing mechanisms;
- intermediaries;
- market structure;
- regulation.

Issuers are generally:

- users of capital;
- originators of risk.

Investors are generally:

- providers of capital;
- bearers of risk.

---

# Market Functions

Intermediaries and market structure help participants:

- search;
- transact;
- enforce.

Prices connect risk and return across participants with different information and motives.

---

# Primary vs. Secondary Markets

## Primary

New securities are issued and capital flows to issuers.

## Secondary

Existing securities trade among investors.

Secondary-market liquidity can affect primary-market financing because investors care about their ability to sell later.

---

# Major Security Categories

## Equity

Residual ownership claim.

## Debt

Contractual borrowing claim.

Examples:

- Treasury bonds;
- municipal bonds;
- corporate bonds;
- asset-backed securities.

## Derivatives

Payoffs depend on another underlying security or variable.

Examples:

- options;
- futures;
- swaps.

---

# Market Structure Matters

Pricing is not purely a mathematical issue.

Real markets also depend on:

- liquidity;
- transaction costs;
- information asymmetry;
- regulations;
- trading mechanisms;
- collateral;
- credit quality.

These frictions become especially important when comparing textbook no-arbitrage with real-world trading.
