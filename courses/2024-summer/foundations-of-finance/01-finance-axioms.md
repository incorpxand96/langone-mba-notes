# Finance Axioms and Core Logic

The course begins with simple assumptions about investors and markets.

These are important because most later formulas are elaborations of them.

---

# Investor Axiom 1 — More Is Better

All else equal, investors prefer larger payoffs.

This establishes the basic direction of utility.

---

# Investor Axiom 2 — Investors Are Risk Averse

All else equal, investors prefer certain payoffs to uncertain payoffs with the same expected value.

That creates a required compensation for bearing risk.

---

# Investor Axiom 3 — Time Value of Money

All else equal, investors prefer money today to the same amount in the future.

Why?

Because money today can be invested productively.

This creates discounting and compounding.

---

# Market Axiom — No Free Lunch

Highly competitive financial markets make pure arbitrage opportunities rare.

If two assets deliver the same payoff, competitive trading should push their prices toward equality.

This becomes the **Law of One Price**.

---

# Real vs. Financial Assets

## Real Assets

Productive assets such as:

- land;
- buildings;
- machines;
- intellectual property.

## Financial Assets

Claims on future cash flows generated directly or indirectly by real economic activity.

Examples:

- stocks;
- bonds;
- derivatives.

Finance studies how those claims are:

- created;
- traded;
- priced;
- allocated.

---

# Price as Information

A recurring course theme is that market price combines information about:

- expected payoff;
- risk;
- timing;
- investor alternatives.

Therefore a security's price can move even when its promised cash flow does not.

A change in outside investment opportunities changes the required return, which changes present value.

---

# My Take

The course is easier to remember when formulas are traced back to the axioms.

Examples:

- PV comes from time preference.
- risk premiums come from risk aversion.
- diversification comes from how uncertainty combines.
- CAPM asks which uncertainty matters in equilibrium.
- replication comes from no-arbitrage.
