# Cross-Course Connections

Operations has stronger crossover with the rest of the MBA than its formulas initially suggest.

---

# Accounting — Working Capital

Financial Accounting records:

- inventory;
- receivables;
- payables.

Operations explains the process that creates those balances.

Little's Law converts balances into time:

- inventory → DIO;
- receivables → DSO;
- payables → DPO.

This makes the cash conversion cycle an operating-flow metric as much as an accounting ratio.

---

# Finance — Value of Constraints

Optimization provides a finance-like marginal value concept:

> **shadow price**

A bottleneck resource is worth expanding only when the incremental economic benefit exceeds the cost.

This is operational capital allocation.

---

# Business Drivers — Margin and Capital Efficiency

Operational improvements can affect:

- revenue;
- COGS;
- labor cost;
- inventory;
- PP&E;
- working capital;
- service;
- markdowns.

Those effects ultimately change:

- margin;
- turnover;
- ROIC;
- cash flow.

---

# Strategy — Capability as Differentiation

Benihana, Zara, and Apple show that operational capability can itself become strategic differentiation.

Examples:

- Benihana — process is part of entertainment product.
- Zara — speed reduces forecast risk and creates assortment freshness.
- Apple — supply-chain power and working-capital efficiency support premium economics.

---

# Analytics — Optimization

Linear programming formalizes a recurring business problem:

> allocate scarce resources among competing uses.

Sensitivity analysis then asks how valuable it would be to relax a constraint.

---

# Digital Strategy — AI Operations

The OpenAI teaching material demonstrates that digital products still depend on operational choices around:

- infrastructure;
- capacity;
- queueing;
- process design;
- variable demand.
