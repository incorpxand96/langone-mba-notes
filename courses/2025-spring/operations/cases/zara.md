# Zara

## Core Operating Advantage

Zara competes through speed, flexibility, frequent assortment change, and information flow.

Its operating model supports:

- many styles;
- small initial batches;
- rapid feedback;
- fast replenishment;
- limited markdown exposure.

---

# Vertical Integration

Zara retains significant control over:

- design;
- production;
- distribution;
- retail information.

Fashion-sensitive products are often sourced closer to headquarters.

Basic products can be sourced from lower-cost distant suppliers.

This is a segmented supply-chain strategy rather than a uniform "make everything locally" rule.

---

# Information

Store managers feed customer and sales information back to headquarters frequently.

This reduces the gap between:

- market demand;
- design decisions.

---

# Small Batches

Small initial runs reduce the cost of forecast errors.

If the item works:

- replenish quickly.

If it fails:

- losses are limited.

This trades some unit-cost efficiency for much greater demand responsiveness.

---

# Scarcity

Limited quantities also become part of the customer proposition.

Customers learn:

> buy now because it may not be there later.

Thus inventory policy supports merchandising strategy.

---

# Zara vs. Traditional Fast Fashion

The teaching material contrasts Zara with more outsourced, long-lead-time models.

Longer commitments can achieve lower sourcing cost but create:

- greater forecast exposure;
- slower reaction;
- more markdown risk.

---

# Financial Translation

Faster inventory turns can:

- reduce working capital;
- reduce markdowns;
- improve full-price sell-through;
- shorten the cash cycle.

---

# Durable Lesson

> **The cheapest supply chain is not necessarily the most profitable supply chain.**

Speed can be worth paying for when uncertainty is high and product life is short.
