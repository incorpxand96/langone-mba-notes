# Apple — From Product Flow to Cash Flow

## Product Strategy

The teaching material frames Apple through:

- limited variety;
- disciplined product timing;
- high quality;
- premium pricing.

The focused assortment concentrates volume and operational attention.

---

# Operating Model

Apple combines:

- deep supplier relationships;
- outsourced manufacturing;
- tight process control;
- massive purchasing scale;
- just-in-time logistics;
- rapid product-launch ramp-up;
- selective dual sourcing;
- design-for-manufacturing discipline.

---

# Asset-Light Production

Contract manufacturers bear much of the manufacturing asset burden.

This gives Apple:

- lower owned PP&E than a vertically integrated manufacturer;
- flexibility;
- strong return on capital.

Apple still exerts substantial control over supplier:

- quality;
- capacity;
- tooling;
- timing.

---

# Working Capital

The case is a direct application of the cash conversion cycle.

Apple combines:

- very low inventory days;
- relatively quick customer collection;
- unusually long supplier payment terms.

Result:

> **negative cash conversion cycle**

Apple can receive customer cash before paying many suppliers.

---

# Economic Implication

Strong operations contribute to:

- cash generation;
- lower external funding need;
- fewer markdowns;
- lower inventory obsolescence;
- high capital efficiency.

---

# Strategy–Operations Alignment

Apple's low variety and high volumes make it easier to:

- negotiate with suppliers;
- forecast demand;
- scale launches;
- minimize inventory.

Those operating advantages reinforce the premium product strategy.

---

# Durable Lesson

> **Product architecture, supply-chain design, and financial performance are one connected system.**
