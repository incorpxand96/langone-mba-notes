# Strategic Operations and Product–Process Fit

Operations is responsible for creating and delivering the product or service.

The strategic question is not:

> How can we make the process as cheap as possible?

It is:

> **What operating capabilities does the business strategy require, and how should we design the process to create them?**

---

# Product Strategy

A useful product framework from the course:

1. **Cost**
2. **Variety**
3. **Response time**
4. **Quality**

A company chooses where it wants to compete across these dimensions.

Tradeoffs are common.

Examples:

- high variety often increases complexity;
- faster response can require excess capacity or local sourcing;
- premium quality can increase process-control requirements;
- lowest cost can reduce flexibility.

---

# Operational Objectives

Once the product strategy is clear, translate it into operational objectives.

Examples:

- low cost;
- rapid response;
- high availability;
- low inventory;
- reliable quality;
- customization;
- flexible capacity.

---

# Operational Capabilities

Capabilities come from:

- resources;
- processes;
- technology;
- information;
- people;
- operations culture;
- supplier network.

A capability must be designed into the system.

---

# Strategic Operational Audit

A reusable sequence:

1. Identify the product/business strategy.
2. Identify target customers.
3. Translate strategy into operational objectives.
4. Examine resources and process design.
5. Test whether capabilities fit the objectives.
6. Ask whether the fit creates differentiation or advantage.
7. Trace the operating design into financial consequences.

---

# Effectiveness vs. Efficiency

An operation can be efficient at doing the wrong thing.

The course therefore distinguishes:

- **efficiency:** resource use;
- **effectiveness:** whether the operating system supports the strategic promise.

---

# Market-Driven and Process-Driven Strategy

Operations need not merely react to product strategy.

A distinctive operational capability can also change what the business is able to offer.

Examples:

- Zara's speed enables more fashion variety and scarcity;
- Benihana's hibachi process becomes part of the product experience.

---

# My Take

Operations strategy is the bridge from:

> **what customers value**

to:

> **what the organization must be unusually good at doing.**
