# Allocentric Strategy and Ecosystem Thinking

The syllabus explicitly asks students to think **allocentrically rather than egocentrically**.

## Egocentric Analysis

Ask only:

> What is the direct effect on our company?

## Allocentric Analysis

Ask how the action changes the incentives and outside options of every important player.

## Customers

- What need are they solving?
- How does WTP change?
- What alternatives improve or worsen?

## Competitors

- How will they respond?
- Can they imitate profitably?
- Will we trigger price/feature competition?

## Entrants, Substitutes, Complementors

- Does our action invite entry?
- Strengthen a substitute?
- Make complementors more valuable?

## Suppliers and Employees

- What surplus do they receive from dealing with us?
- Do our actions change their outside options?
- Can they appropriate more value?

## Acquisition Counterparties

- Are our resources worth more to another owner?
- Does our action change what a buyer or target can capture?

## License to Operate

Power should be used with restraint when aggressive appropriation would damage:

- supplier quality;
- employee commitment;
- customer trust;
- regulation;
- social legitimacy.

## Principle

> **The most important effect of a strategic action is often the reaction it causes elsewhere in the ecosystem.**
