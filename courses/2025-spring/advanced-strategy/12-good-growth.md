# Good Growth

Growth is not inherently strategic.

Good growth is **NPV-positive growth that makes the whole worth more than the sum of the parts**.

## Three Dimensions of Scope

### Horizontal Growth
Increase scale or consolidate within a stage.

Potential benefits:

- economies of scale;
- network effects;
- reduced rivalry;
- better fixed-cost amortization.

### Vertical Integration
Own/control additional stages.

Potential benefits:

- lower coordination cost;
- better control of WTP drivers;
- economies of scope;
- protection from bargaining problems.

### Diversification
Apply assets/capabilities to new products or geographies.

Potential benefits:

- economies of scope;
- complementarity;
- monetization of existing assets.

## Four Tests for Good Growth

Growth should ideally accomplish multiple items:

1. **Deepen or defend competitive advantage** — increase the WTP-C wedge.
2. **Monetize the core advantage more effectively** — extract more revenue from existing assets/IP/capabilities.
3. **Move into a more attractive market** — improve industry economics.
4. **Improve asset utilization / fixed-cost amortization** — lower unit cost.

The course rule of thumb is to seek growth opportunities with at least two of these benefits at a price that still raises net enterprise value.

## Managerial Kiss Warning

Do not assume the firm's management system is universally transferable.

A successful organization in one business may fail in another because:

- customer needs differ;
- capabilities do not transfer;
- integration costs rise;
- incentives become harder to design.

## Scarcity, Scalability, Scopability

A useful final diagnostic:

### Scarcity
Is the underlying advantage valuable and hard to copy?

### Scalability
Can volume increase without losing quality or margins?

### Scopability
Can the advantage be reused across new markets?

## Principle

> **The objective is not growth. It is growth that compounds the economics of the advantage.**
