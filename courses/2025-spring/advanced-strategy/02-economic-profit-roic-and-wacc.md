# Economic Profit, ROIC, and WACC

Advanced Strategy uses financial returns as **evidence** that a strategic advantage exists.

## ROIC

A useful conceptual form is:

`ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital`

For nonfinancial companies, sustained ROIC above WACC is evidence that returns have not been fully competed down.

## WACC

WACC is the opportunity cost of the capital supplied to the business.

Therefore:

- `ROIC > WACC` → positive economic profit;
- `ROIC = WACC` → competitive return;
- `ROIC < WACC` → economic value destruction.

## Economic Profit

A useful conceptual expression:

`Economic Profit = (ROIC - WACC) × Invested Capital`

Economic profit is a stronger strategic yardstick than accounting profit because capital itself has a cost.

## Why ROIC Is Useful

ROIC:

- is less distorted by financing decisions than ROE;
- supports cross-firm and time-series comparison;
- can be decomposed into operating drivers;
- links strategy to valuation;
- creates a hurdle against WACC.

## ROIC Is Not Strategy

High ROIC can result from many sources.

The strategist must explain **why** it exists.

Potential sources:

- pricing power;
- gross-margin advantage;
- low SG&A intensity;
- asset utilization;
- low working-capital intensity;
- superior infrastructure;
- scalable economics.

## Beware Ratio Gaming

ROIC can rise because the denominator falls or because investment is deferred.

Managers can destroy future value while mechanically improving current ROIC.

Therefore the metric should support the strategic narrative, not replace it.

## Principle

> **Financial performance is the footprint of strategy; the analytical job is to trace the footprint back to the economic source.**
