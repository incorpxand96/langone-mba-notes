# Industry Attractiveness and Market Structure

Five Forces remains a starting point, but the course adds two important lenses:

1. **value-chain position**;
2. **market structure**.

## Industry vs. Firm

Five Forces uses the **industry** as the unit of analysis.

The firm-level question is different:

> Is this company unusually added value relative to other firms in the same industry?

## Value Chain

Every industry occupies a stage in a broader value chain.

The attractiveness of one stage depends partly on the concentration and bargaining power of adjacent stages.

The strategically attractive position is often:

- concentrated at the focal stage;
- competitive upstream;
- competitive downstream.

## Six Market Structures

The course distinguishes:

1. perfect competition;
2. monopolistic competition;
3. homogeneous oligopoly;
4. differentiated oligopoly;
5. winner-take-all;
6. natural monopoly.

These are matters of degree rather than perfectly discrete boxes.

## Two Structural Drivers

### Returns to Scale

Supply-side scale lowers cost.

Demand-side scale/network effects increase WTP.

Large minimum efficient scale can support concentration.

### Nature of Customer Need

Some necessities offer limited room for sustainable differentiation within a segment.

Higher-order wants can provide more opportunities for differentiated WTP.

## Concentration: Good or Bad?

Concentration can reflect:

- genuine efficiency and scale;
- superior intangible assets;
- entry barriers and rent seeking.

Therefore concentration itself is not proof of either healthy competition or market abuse.

## Principle

> **First understand where profit can persist in the value chain; then explain why this firm is unusually well positioned within that stage.**
