# Strategic Calibration: Weight, Variance, and Diminishing Returns

The course reframes differentiation as a problem of **calibration**.

## Weight

How important is an attribute to customer WTP?

High-weight attributes are must-haves.

Failing to provide them can reduce WTP by more than the associated cost savings.

## Variance

How much do firms differ on that attribute?

An attribute becomes strategically interesting when it is:

- high weight;
- high variance.

That is where a firm can create meaningful differentiation.

## Diminishing Returns

WTP for most attributes eventually plateaus.

If the firm keeps adding the attribute after customers are satiated:

`ΔCost > ΔWTP`

and the `WTP - C` wedge contracts.

## Rivalry Through Overinvestment

Feature competition can push an industry into diminishing-returns territory.

Competitors keep adding costly features that customers barely value.

## Strategic Underinvestment

The course's playful term "sock puppeting" captures a serious idea:

> deliberately do less on attributes that are low-weight or already beyond the point of useful differentiation.

## Principle

> **Do not maximize every attribute. Invest where customer importance and competitive variance overlap.**
