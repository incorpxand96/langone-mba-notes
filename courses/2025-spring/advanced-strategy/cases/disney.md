# Disney: Eisner, Wells, Katzenberg, and Iger

Disney integrates nearly every major course theme:

- IP scarcity;
- real options;
- creative talent;
- organizational culture;
- growth/scopability;
- acquisition;
- governance.

## Walt's Strategic Insight

After losing Oswald, Disney learned the importance of **owning characters/IP**.

A successful character becomes an asset with option value across many future businesses.

## Eisner, Wells, and Katzenberg (EWK)

The team rejuvenated Disney through:

- better creative output;
- higher park pricing;
- home video;
- merchandising;
- disciplined movie budgets;
- talent recruitment;
- cross-divisional coordination.

## Katzenberg's Studio Logic

Key principles included:

- ideas/story first;
- ownable IP;
- avoid bidding wars for talent;
- favor many disciplined bets over a few enormous blockbusters;
- keep decision-making close to creative work;
- create a place where talented people can take risks.

## Culture as an Economic Asset

Under EWK, Disney deliberately created interactions among leaders through programs, shared experiences, and cross-divisional coordination.

Trust and relationships became connective tissue enabling the synergy machine.

## Post-Wells Breakdown

After Frank Wells died:

- Eisner became less balanced;
- Katzenberg left;
- promotion became perceived as less predictable;
- strategic planning gained veto power;
- senior talent departed;
- acquisitions increased complexity.

The strategic issue was not merely a bad org chart; Disney impaired relationship capital and innovative capacity.

## Iger

Iger restored relationships and placed greater emphasis on acquiring scarce creative capabilities/IP such as Pixar, Marvel, and Lucasfilm.

## Principle

> **At Disney, culture is not soft context around the strategy; it is part of the production technology for valuable creative IP.**
