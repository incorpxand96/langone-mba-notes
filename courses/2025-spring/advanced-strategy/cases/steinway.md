# Steinway

Steinway is a useful case for diagnosing **scarcity value** in a mature physical product.

## Strategic Question

Why can a piano manufacturer sustain premium economics when the basic product category is old and competitors can observe much of the product?

## Potential Sources of Advantage

### Relationship Capital

- relationships with artists;
- institutions;
- dealers;
- teachers;
- performance venues.

Prestigious users can create signaling value for other buyers.

### Infrastructure

- specialized manufacturing;
- skilled craft processes;
- distribution/dealer system;
- accumulated production know-how.

### Innovative / Tacit Capacity

Much of the value can be embedded in difficult-to-codify process knowledge rather than headline product innovation.

## Buyer Uncertainty

High-end pianos are:

- expensive;
- infrequently purchased;
- difficult for many buyers to evaluate perfectly;
- risky to experiment with.

These conditions can preserve differentiation.

## Brand as Evidence

Steinway's brand matters, but the analytical task is to identify the capabilities, relationships, and buyer conditions that make the brand credible.

## Principle

> **In a mature category, scarcity can come from accumulated trust, tacit process knowledge, and buyer risk—not only technological novelty.**
