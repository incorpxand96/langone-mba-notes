# Intel

Intel connects dominant design, path dependency, complementors, incumbent incentives, and the eventual limits of an architecture.

## DRAMs

Intel's early DRAM advantage proved unsustainable as competition increased and the economics changed.

The strategic lesson is that technical leadership alone does not guarantee durable scarcity.

## CPU Advantage

The CPU market developed differently.

Intel benefited from:

- x86 architecture;
- IBM PC adoption;
- Microsoft complementarity;
- installed base;
- software compatibility;
- manufacturing/R&D scale.

This created strong path dependency.

## Wintel

Microsoft and Intel were complements, but their incentives were imperfectly aligned.

Intel benefited when users bought new PCs.

Microsoft could often monetize through software independent of a full hardware replacement cycle.

## Diminishing Returns to Innovation

Over time, additional CPU performance generated less incremental customer WTP in traditional PCs.

Meanwhile mobile computing changed the attributes customers valued, including energy efficiency.

## Diseconomies of Scope / Architectural Lock-In

Moving from x86/CISC to a different architecture could devalue:

- fabs;
- engineering knowledge;
- software ecosystem;
- organizational capabilities;
- installed relationships.

The old advantage can therefore become a constraint.

## Principle

> **Path dependency is powerful until the attributes that made the path valuable stop being the attributes the market cares about.**
