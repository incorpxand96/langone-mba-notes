# Cola Wars

Cola Wars is a case about the economics of **where power sits in the value chain** and how duopolists can preserve or destroy industry profit.

## Concentrate vs. Bottling

The concentrate stage historically captured unusually attractive economics.

Why?

- high brand/mindshare investment created concentration;
- concentrate production itself was not especially capital intensive;
- bottling/distribution required far more physical assets;
- exclusive territories limited bottler rivalry locally;
- Coke and Pepsi could encourage marketing by bottlers while retaining key power upstream.

## Best of Both Worlds

The concentrate makers benefited from bottlers behaving as if they had local market power while the concentrate makers themselves remained the scarce upstream brands.

## Coke vs. Pepsi

The course uses financial statements to compare where each company's spending and capital intensity sit.

The point is not simply that Coke has a stronger brand.

The strategy story should explain:

- gross margin;
- SG&A;
- distribution intensity;
- capital employed;
- ROIC.

## Duopoly

Coke and Pepsi illustrate strategic awareness.

A duopoly can be highly profitable if firms avoid unconstrained price/feature escalation.

## Trees Do Not Grow to the Sky

Even advantaged firms face saturation and competitive response.

Growth eventually becomes harder when:

- market share is high;
- the category matures;
- substitutes emerge;
- incremental investment yields less WTP.

## Principle

> **The most valuable stage of a value chain can be the one that owns scarce demand rather than the one that owns the most physical assets.**
