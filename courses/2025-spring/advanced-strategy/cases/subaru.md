# Subaru

Subaru introduces the course's distinction between **strategy** and **execution**.

## Strategic Position

Subaru created a WTP-C advantage for a customer segment through a distinctive bundle of attributes rather than broad market dominance.

Important investments included:

- all-wheel-drive architecture;
- boxer engine / platform choices;
- safety and ruggedness;
- motorsport-derived learning;
- targeted brand positioning.

## Source of Advantage

The case frames Subaru's advantage mainly through **innovative capacity**, reinforced by infrastructure and customer insight.

Its racing history created a learning environment for continuously improving attributes valued by target customers.

## Strategy vs. Execution

By the early 2000s Subaru's U.S. share remained low.

A marketing study found that many potential customers simply did not know the brand.

That is not necessarily a strategy failure.

If the value proposition is attractive but customers are unaware of it, the problem can be **execution/marketing**.

## Love Campaign

Subaru's later campaign amplified characteristics of its actual customer base and helped communicate the value proposition more effectively.

## Principle

> **Do not redesign a strategy before determining whether the real problem is that the market has not heard or understood it.**
