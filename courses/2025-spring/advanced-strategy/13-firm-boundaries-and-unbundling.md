# Firm Boundaries, Unbundling, and Interaction Costs

The boundary of a company depends partly on the relative cost of coordinating activities:

- inside the firm;
- through markets and partners.

## Interaction Costs

Interaction costs include the time and money required to exchange:

- goods;
- services;
- information;
- ideas.

They include meetings, contracts, sales processes, information transfer, and coordination.

## Why Firms Bundle Activities

When internal coordination is cheaper or safer than external contracting, activities tend to remain inside the firm.

## Why Industries Unbundle

Technology, standardization, deregulation, and globalization can reduce external interaction costs.

Specialists can then outperform integrated firms in activities requiring different economics.

## Three Business Types

The unbundling framework distinguishes broadly:

- customer-relationship businesses;
- product-innovation businesses;
- infrastructure businesses.

These often have different:

- cultures;
- economics;
- scale requirements;
- speed;
- management systems.

Housing all three under one roof can create organizational conflict.

## Strategic Implication

A firm's historic vertical integration may become obsolete even if it was once efficient.

## Principle

> **Firm boundaries should change when the economics of coordination change.**
