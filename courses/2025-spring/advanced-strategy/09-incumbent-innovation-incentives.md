# Incumbent Innovation Incentives

Entrants and incumbents often evaluate the same innovation differently.

## Sunk-Cost Effect

An incumbent has already invested in:

- plants;
- equipment;
- distribution;
- employees;
- routines;
- customer relationships.

A new technology can accelerate the depreciation of those assets and capabilities.

Therefore an incumbent may rationally continue using an older process while it still produces strong contribution economics.

## Replacement Effect

An entrant may gain:

`0 → profitable market position`

while a monopolist may gain only:

`old monopoly → new monopoly`

The incremental gain to the entrant can therefore exceed the incumbent's gain from replacing itself.

## Efficiency Effect

The logic changes if credible entry is likely.

A monopolist may lose more from becoming one of two competitors than an entrant gains by becoming the second competitor.

If innovation can preempt entry, the incumbent's incentive to innovate rises sharply.

## Combined Interpretation

### Low Entry Threat
Sunk-cost + replacement effects can make inertia rational.

### High Entry Threat
Efficiency effect can dominate and justify self-disruption.

## Principle

> **Do not explain incumbent inertia as stupidity before comparing the incumbent's actual NPV with the entrant's.**
