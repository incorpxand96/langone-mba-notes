# Value Creation, Added Value, and Value Appropriation

The course uses **value-based business strategy** as a foundation.

## Value Creation

For a complete chain of buyers, firms, and suppliers:

`Value Created = Willingness to Pay - Opportunity Cost`

Marciano often simplifies the firm-level discussion to:

`WTP - C`

where `C` should be understood economically, including the opportunity costs required to keep suppliers, employees, investors, and other trading partners willing to participate.

## Value Appropriation

Creating value does not determine who gets it.

Bargaining determines how value is divided among:

- customers;
- the focal firm;
- employees;
- suppliers;
- complementors;
- other participants.

## Added Value

A player has added value when the total value available in the system would be lower without that player.

This creates bargaining leverage.

A firm with many identical competitors has little or no added value because trading partners can substitute away from it.

## Favorable Asymmetry

A durable strategy requires the firm to be meaningfully different from its alternatives in a way that matters economically.

That asymmetry can arise through:

- higher WTP;
- lower cost/opportunity cost;
- superior relationships;
- scarce capacity;
- distinctive infrastructure;
- innovative capability.

## Buyers and Suppliers Are Symmetric

A major course emphasis is not to focus only on customers.

A firm must remain attractive to **upstream** trading partners too.

Examples:

- employees choose among employers;
- suppliers choose among customers;
- creators choose among studios;
- franchisees choose among franchisors.

## Win-Win Exchange

The strongest strategies do not rely on one-off exploitation.

They create a transaction in which the focal firm remains the best available option for key partners while still appropriating enough value to earn economic profit.

## Principle

> **Create more total value than alternatives, then preserve enough bargaining leverage to capture a durable share of it.**
