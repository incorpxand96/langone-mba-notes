# Advanced Strategy

This folder contains my working knowledge base for **Advanced Strategy (MGMT-GB 2375)**, taught by **Professor Sonia Marciano** at NYU Stern in Spring 2025.

The course's distinctive goal is to **think like an economist about firm strategy**.

Rather than stop at labels such as differentiation, competitive advantage, disruption, or synergy, the course repeatedly asks:

> **What exactly creates the firm's WTP-C advantage, why can it appropriate some of that value, why can competitors not profitably copy it, and does the financial evidence support the story?**

# Core Logic

```text
Industry / value-chain context
        ↓
Who are the trading partners?
        ↓
What do they value?
        ↓
WTP - C advantage
        ↓
What investment creates it?
(Relationship / Infrastructure / Innovative Capacity)
        ↓
Why can't rivals profitably copy it?
        ↓
Scarcity / bargaining power
        ↓
Can it scale?
        ↓
Can it travel to new markets?
        ↓
ROIC > WACC / Economic Profit
```

# Three Components of Enterprise Value

## Scarcity
Does the firm possess a sustainable WTP and/or cost advantage that gives it bargaining leverage over customers or suppliers?

## Scalability
Can output grow without destroying margins, cost advantage, or product quality?

## Scopability
Can the firm's advantage be monetized across additional products, geographies, customer groups, or value-chain stages?

# High-Level Takeaways

1. Strategy should explain economic profit, not merely revenue growth or market share.
2. `WTP - C` is the core value-creation wedge.
3. Value creation and value appropriation are different questions.
4. Buyers and suppliers should be treated symmetrically.
5. A firm is added value when the trading system creates less value without it.
6. Sustainable added value requires a favorable asymmetry relative to alternatives.
7. High ROIC is evidence of advantage; it is not itself the source of advantage.
8. The relevant financial test is generally `ROIC > WACC` for nonfinancial businesses.
9. Economic profit explicitly charges the firm for capital.
10. Industry attractiveness matters, but firm position and company-specific implementation can matter more.
11. Market structure is a useful complement to Five Forces.
12. Strategy lives in attributes with both high weight and high variance.
13. Adding features past customer satiation can destroy value because WTP plateaus while cost continues rising.
14. A company should deliberately underinvest in low-value dimensions rather than compete everywhere.
15. Sources of advantage can often be organized into relationship capital, infrastructure, and innovative capacity.
16. One of these is often the linchpin; the other two reinforce it.
17. Brand, market share, and financial performance are usually evidence of advantage, not the root cause.
18. Defensibility depends on whether the investments required to imitate are economically attractive for a rival.
19. Scarcity is sustained when the residual market is too small relative to the investment needed to copy the position.
20. Information can be the enemy of differentiation when customers can easily compare competing offerings.
21. Customer inertia can be rational when search and experimentation are costly or risky.
22. Network effects can reinforce defensibility.
23. Oligopolists must reason about competitor reactions, not just direct effects.
24. High scalability can intensify rivalry if multiple rivals believe they are advantaged and can expand cheaply.
25. Dominant designs can convert early product-market fit into path-dependent entry barriers.
26. Complementors can strengthen path dependency.
27. Incumbent inertia can be economically rational because incumbents and entrants face different investment economics.
28. The sunk-cost effect discourages replacing still-productive installed assets.
29. The replacement effect means a monopolist may gain less from replacing itself than an entrant gains from entering.
30. The efficiency effect reverses that logic when credible entry threatens monopoly profits.
31. Disruption should be treated as a contingent theory, not a universal prediction.
32. Historical cases should be tested against a theory's actual conditions rather than retrofitted to the label.
33. Real options complement DCF when management has meaningful flexibility to expand, defer, abandon, or redeploy.
34. Creative IP can possess option value because one hit can be monetized through many future channels.
35. Good growth makes the whole worth more than the sum of the parts.
36. Growth should improve multiple dimensions of the firm's economics rather than merely increase size.
37. Horizontal scale, vertical integration, and diversification can each create or destroy value.
38. The managerial kiss is not magic: organizational capabilities do not automatically transfer into new businesses.
39. Falling interaction costs can change efficient firm boundaries and favor specialization/unbundling.
40. Strategy should be allocentric, not egocentric: analyze the reactions and outside options of every important participant.
41. Sustainable power must be used carefully enough to preserve valuable relationships and the firm's license to operate.
42. The strongest strategic narrative links qualitative advantage to quantitative financial evidence.
