# Disruptive Innovation — Useful Theory, Dangerous Gospel

The course deliberately treats disruption with skepticism.

## The Useful Insight

Incumbents can rationally favor:

- current customers;
- current margins;
- current capabilities;
- sustaining improvements

over initially unattractive emerging alternatives.

This can create an opening for entrants.

## The Problem With the Label

The assigned critiques argue that many famous examples do not cleanly satisfy the theory's stated conditions or predictions.

The word "disruptive" has also expanded in ordinary business language to mean almost any significant innovation.

That weakens its analytical value.

## Better Practice

Treat disruption as a **hypothesis**.

Ask:

1. Is the entrant initially serving a low-end or new-market foothold?
2. Is the incumbent rationally ignoring it based on current customers/economics?
3. Does the entrant improve along a trajectory that eventually satisfies mainstream needs?
4. Are incumbent capabilities actually liabilities in the new architecture?
5. Are sunk-cost and replacement effects enough to explain inertia?
6. Is credible entry strong enough for the efficiency effect to dominate?

## Theory vs. Storytelling

A theory should help predict and explain.

If every successful entrant is labeled disruptive after the fact, the framework becomes unfalsifiable.

## Principle

> **Use disruption to generate questions, not to end the analysis.**
