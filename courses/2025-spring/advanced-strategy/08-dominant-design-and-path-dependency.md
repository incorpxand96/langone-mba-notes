# Innovation, Dominant Design, and Path Dependency

A dominant design is a product architecture or configuration around which an industry converges.

## Dominant Design

A design becomes dominant when it performs well on the attributes customers care about and becomes widely adopted.

The point is not necessarily who invented first.

The economically important actor may be the one that combines features into a configuration that creates much more value for a broad market.

## Weight and Variance Link

A dominant design often emerges from correctly identifying:

- high-weight attributes;
- which costly attributes can be omitted with limited WTP loss.

## Path Dependency

Once adoption begins, the installed base can attract:

- complementors;
- trained labor;
- suppliers;
- repair infrastructure;
- standards;
- customer familiarity.

Those complementary investments raise the WTP of the dominant design further.

## Positive Feedback

```text
Early adoption
    ↓
More complements / knowledge / infrastructure
    ↓
Higher WTP and lower adoption risk
    ↓
More adoption
```

## Strategic Implication

The advantage can outlive the original technical superiority because the ecosystem itself becomes valuable.

## Principle

> **Winning the design contest can create a path-dependent ecosystem that becomes harder to dislodge than the original product advantage.**
