# Cases and Exercises

The course is built around negotiation simulations rather than abstract doctrine alone.

This page records the primary lesson I want to retain from each exercise.

---

## Dosagen–Synertech

**Theme:** Distributive negotiation fundamentals.

Key lessons:

- BATNA determines real bargaining power.
- Reservation price should be derived from alternatives.
- Multiple objective reference points can support very different positions.
- First offers anchor.
- Preparation matters more than improvisational brilliance.

---

## Knight–Excalibur

**Theme:** Early distributive bargaining and information.

Key lessons:

- A transaction can contain quality, timing, branding, and risk issues beyond headline unit price.
- The apparent single issue may hide additional dimensions.
- Know which facts are shared and which are private.

---

## Texoil

**Theme:** Negative bargaining zone and interests vs. positions.

Key lessons:

- A price-only negotiation may have no ZOPA.
- A negative bargaining zone should trigger creativity, not immediate capitulation.
- Underlying interests can create non-monetary solutions.
- A negotiation can contain both competitive and cooperative elements.

---

## Leckenby Company

**Theme:** Concessions, reciprocity, fairness, and communication constraints.

Key lessons:

- Allow room for concessions.
- Prefer bilateral movement.
- Smaller concessions can signal a limit.
- Do not split the difference automatically.
- Communication is not merely transmission; messages are perceived and interpreted.
- Leverage and fairness shape process.

---

## Working Moms

**Theme:** Integrative bargaining.

Key lessons:

- Creating value and claiming value are separate tasks.
- Multiple issues allow trades.
- Different priorities create surplus.
- Different time preferences create surplus.
- Different forecasts can be handled with contingent contracts.
- Information sharing is necessary for value creation, but should be reciprocal and deliberate.

---

## Negotiation Style Exercise

**Theme:** Competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating.

Key lessons:

- A favored style is not a universally correct style.
- Style should depend on substantive stakes, relationship, and time horizon.
- Development means expanding behavioral range.

---

## Federated Science Fund

**Theme:** Coalitions.

Key lessons:

- Power depends on coalition contribution.
- A grand coalition may be unstable even when it creates the most total value.
- Coalition members compare their allocation with what they can earn elsewhere.
- Formal tools such as Shapley value illustrate marginal contribution.
- Relationship obligations affect coalition stability.

---

## Harborco

**Theme:** Multi-party negotiation.

Key lessons:

- More parties create more than more complexity.
- Decision rules and veto rights change power.
- Coalitions and counter-coalitions are central.
- Constituencies matter.
- Hidden agendas matter.
- Team roles and information discipline matter.
- Many feasible agreements can exist without a politically feasible agreement emerging.

---

## Acquiring a Company

**Theme:** Information asymmetry and adverse selection.

Key lessons:

- Conditional information matters.
- Acceptance can reveal that the asset is worse than the buyer hoped.
- Synergies do not automatically overcome adverse selection.
- Strategic reasoning requires thinking about what the other side knows.

---

## Name Your Price

**Theme:** Salary negotiation.

Key lessons:

- strengthen BATNA;
- use market criteria;
- set aspiration and reservation levels;
- trade across compensation dimensions;
- do not disclose weak alternatives unnecessarily;
- wait before countering;
- make problems collaborative rather than issuing demands;
- know when to stop.

---

# The Pattern Across Cases

The simulations become progressively more complex:

> **price → information → multiple issues → relationships → style → coalitions → organizational politics**

The core tools remain useful throughout.

What changes is the number of people, issues, incentives, and interpretations that must be managed.
