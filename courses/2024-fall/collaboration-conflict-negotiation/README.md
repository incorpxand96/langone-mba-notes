# Collaboration, Conflict, and Negotiation

This folder is my working knowledge base for **MGMT-GB 2159: Collaboration, Conflict, and Negotiation**, taught by **Zur Shapira** at NYU Stern.

The course treats negotiation as both a **strategic** and a **human** process. Good outcomes depend on analysis and preparation, but also on listening, trust, emotion, fairness, relationships, and the ability to adapt one's style to the situation.

The course arc moves from relatively simple two-party price bargaining to multi-issue value creation, conflict management, and multi-party coalition dynamics.

# The Course's Core Mental Model

## Before the Table

The most important work often happens before negotiating:

1. Define my BATNA.
2. Convert the BATNA into a reservation price.
3. Set an aspiration.
4. Estimate the other party's BATNA, reservation price, and priorities.
5. Identify useful objective reference points.
6. Decide whether the negotiation is primarily distributive, integrative, or both.
7. Consider the importance of the future relationship.
8. Plan my opening, information strategy, and concession pattern.

The course repeatedly emphasizes that bargaining skill at the table is only one part of performance; preparation and information gathering carry much of the load.

---

## At the Table

Questions to keep asking:

- What information am I learning?
- What is the other party actually trying to accomplish?
- Am I negotiating a position or an underlying interest?
- Are there multiple issues?
- Do we value those issues differently?
- Am I being anchored or framed?
- Are my concessions reciprocal?
- Is the relationship important?
- Is emotion distorting the process?
- Is there a better package than the one currently on the table?

---

## After the Table

Reflection matters.

Ask:

- What worked?
- What surprised me?
- What barriers prevented a better agreement?
- What did the other side do that I failed to anticipate?
- Which course concepts explain the outcome?
- What would I change in my preparation next time?

Negotiation skill should be developed through repeated practice and deliberate review, not treated as a fixed personality trait.

# My Current Negotiation Principles

1. **Prepare harder than I bargain.**
2. **Know my BATNA before discussing terms.**
3. **Translate the BATNA into an explicit reservation price.**
4. **Set an aspiration so the reservation price does not become my target.**
5. **Do not reveal my reservation price.**
6. **Use objective reference points selectively and be ready to challenge theirs.**
7. **Expect anchors to affect judgment even when they are arbitrary.**
8. **Re-anchor quickly when the other side sets an unfavorable reference point.**
9. **Distinguish positions from interests.**
10. **Do not assume the pie is fixed when multiple issues exist.**
11. **Trade on differences in priorities, time preferences, risk preferences, and forecasts.**
12. **Share enough information to create value without casually surrendering leverage.**
13. **Bundle issues rather than negotiating every issue one at a time.**
14. **Make reciprocal rather than unilateral concessions.**
15. **Smaller concessions near the end can communicate that I am approaching my limit.**
16. **Do not split the difference mechanically.**
17. **No conflict-management style is universally best.**
18. **Relationship value should change my strategy.**
19. **In multi-party settings, map coalitions, vetoes, constituencies, and decision rules before negotiating.**
20. **Fairness has strategic value because process affects trust and future cooperation.**
21. **Take a break when emotion starts driving the negotiation.**
22. **Never celebrate a victory in a way that creates a loser.**
