# Claiming Value

**Distributive bargaining** is the process of dividing a fixed or largely fixed surplus.

It is most visible when:

- there is a single dominant issue;
- interests are directly opposed; and
- one side's gain is approximately the other side's loss.

The objective is not simply to "win."

It is to protect the value of my alternatives while claiming an appropriate share of the available surplus.

---

# 1. The Distributive Toolkit

Before bargaining:

- know BATNA;
- set reservation price;
- set aspiration;
- estimate the other side's reservation price;
- prepare reference points;
- decide on an opening strategy.

During bargaining:

- manage anchors;
- make purposeful concessions;
- seek reciprocity;
- use objective rationale;
- avoid unnecessary disclosure;
- watch emotion;
- know when to stop.

---

# 2. Opening Offers

An opening offer should be ambitious enough to create room to bargain but credible enough to remain defensible.

The course's standard is essentially:

> Push as far as can be justified without becoming absurd.

An extreme but unsupported number can damage credibility.

An overly modest number gives away value before the other side has demanded it.

---

# 3. Concession Making

The Leckenby exercise makes concession behavior explicit.

## Leave Room

Do not begin with a "first and final" offer unless there is a deliberate strategic reason.

The other side often values the process of movement.

## Prefer Bilateral Concessions

A concession should usually produce movement in return.

Unilateral concessions can teach the other party to wait for more.

## Shrink Concessions Near the End

Smaller moves can communicate that I am approaching my limit.

## Do Not Split the Difference Automatically

"Halfway" is psychologically appealing, but it may have no connection to:

- BATNAs;
- reservation prices;
- fairness;
- market value; or
- economic contribution.

The midpoint is not inherently correct.

---

# 4. Reciprocity

Reciprocity is both a social norm and a bargaining mechanism.

If I make a meaningful concession, the other party often feels pressure to respond.

But reciprocity depends on the concession being **visible**.

That means I should:

- explain movement;
- mark what I am giving up;
- connect the concession to a request from them; and
- avoid giving concessions so quickly that they are treated as the new baseline.

---

# 5. Leverage

Leverage is not simply rank or aggression.

It can come from:

- BATNA;
- timing;
- information;
- control over a scarce resource;
- ability to delay;
- coalition support;
- credibility;
- relationships; or
- the other side's constraints.

The course repeatedly asks negotiators to identify both sides' sources of power rather than assuming leverage from visible status alone.

---

# 6. Fairness

Fairness matters for at least two reasons.

### Normative

People care about whether an outcome or process feels legitimate.

### Strategic

Perceived unfairness can:

- trigger resistance;
- reduce trust;
- slow settlement;
- damage future relationships; and
- cause people to reject otherwise economically attractive outcomes.

Fairness is therefore not merely a moral add-on to negotiation.

It can affect economic outcomes.

---

# 7. Salary Negotiation

The salary exercise applies distributive and integrative principles to compensation.

Course guidance includes:

- strengthen BATNA;
- do not lie;
- do not reveal a weak BATNA casually;
- if BATNA is strong, avoid sounding threatening;
- establish a reservation point and aspiration;
- use objective market criteria;
- explain how requests benefit the employer;
- trade across priorities rather than focusing only on salary;
- think long term.

## Timing

Avoid discussing salary too early when possible.

Once an offer exists, the employer has already invested in selecting me and the discussion becomes more concrete.

## Counteroffers

- do not react immediately under emotion;
- express concerns as problems to solve rather than demands;
- know when additional bargaining creates more risk than value.

---

# Worked Example: Leckenby

Leckenby strips away ordinary conversation and highlights bargaining mechanics.

The exercise focuses attention on:

- leverage;
- reciprocity;
- concession patterns;
- fairness; and
- communication constraints.

It also makes clear that a message passes through several stages:

> **sent → received → perceived → interpreted**

Even when the economic information is simple, meaning can be distorted.

---

# My Rules of Thumb

- Claim value deliberately; do not apologize for having interests.
- Open with room to move.
- Make movement visible.
- Ask for reciprocity.
- Use decreasing concessions as I approach my limit.
- Never split the difference by reflex.
- Support claims with external rationale.
- Treat fairness as strategically relevant.
- Know when the expected value of another round of bargaining is negative.
- Preserve the relationship when the relationship matters.
