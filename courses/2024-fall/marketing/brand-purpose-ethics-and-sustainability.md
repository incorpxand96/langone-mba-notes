# Brand Purpose, Ethics, and Sustainability

Modern brands increasingly communicate not only what they sell and how it works, but what they believe and what role they want customers to play.

The course pushes beyond endorsement toward brands acting as **change agents**.

## Brand Purpose

Potential benefits:

- deeper emotional connection;
- differentiation;
- cultural relevance;
- customer/employee identification;
- community.

Potential risks:

- alienating customers;
- inconsistency;
- superficiality;
- distraction;
- hypocrisy.

The strategic question is whether purpose is relevant, credible, operational, and durable.

## Case: Dove

Dove moved from a functional moisturizing claim to a broader mission around beauty and self-perception.

Purpose became part of brand meaning.

It also increased the burden of consistency across the broader company.

## Case: Allbirds

Allbirds embeds purpose in operations through carbon measurement, natural materials, lower-carbon inputs, shared tools, supplier knowledge, and industry partnerships.

That is different from merely communicating sustainability.

## Purpose as Customer Action

The course highlights brands that help customers take action rather than simply express support.

This moves from "we care" toward "here is what we are doing, and here is how you can participate."

## Marketing Ethics

Major issues include:

- data privacy;
- truth in advertising and labeling;
- addictive design and rabbit holes;
- consumerism;
- sustainability;
- carbon footprint;
- transparency;
- planned obsolescence;
- misleading social-position signaling.

## Privacy as Value Exchange

Customers should understand what data is shared, how to control it, how easy opting out is, and what value they receive in return.

## Color Washing

Greenwashing, pinkwashing, rainbow-washing, sports-washing, and similar behavior seek reputational credit without sufficient action.

Transparency makes hypocrisy increasingly discoverable.

## My Take

It is useful to distinguish four levels of purpose:

1. **Purpose as message**
2. **Purpose as positioning**
3. **Purpose as operating model**
4. **Purpose as ecosystem intervention**

The deeper the level, the harder the purpose is to imitate and the more credible it becomes.

## Rules of Thumb

- Purpose should connect to brand and customer.
- Operational behavior matters more than slogans.
- The more public the stance, the greater the consistency burden.
- Make data value exchange clear.
- Do not use social identity as decoration.
- Expect customers to verify claims.
- Ethics and reputation increasingly interact with performance.
