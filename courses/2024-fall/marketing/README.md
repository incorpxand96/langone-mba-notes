# Marketing

This folder is my working knowledge base for **COR1-GB 2310: Marketing**, taught by **Michelle Greenwald** at NYU Stern.

The course was deliberately managerial: understand the major concepts and tools of marketing, then make choices about **who to target, what the brand should mean, how to activate that positioning, and how to know whether the choices worked**.

Two ideas organize the course particularly well:

> **Strategy should determine tactics. Marketing activity should activate a positioning, not substitute for having one.**

> **Marketing is choosing.**

Modern marketers can target more narrowly, produce more content, use more channels, and measure more variables than ever. That increases the importance of prioritization rather than reducing it.

## My Current Marketing Mental Model

1. **Define the brand challenge.** Launch, reposition, defend, recover, expand, retain, or grow.
2. **Define the positioning.** Target, competitive frame, point of difference, end benefit.
3. **Understand the journey.** What behavior should happen next?
4. **Activate the positioning.** Choose product, price, distribution, advertising, promotion, PR, digital, retail, packaging, and experience.
5. **Measure what changed.** Awareness, consideration, conversion, retention, frequency, advocacy, sales, share, economics.
6. **Learn and reallocate.** Faster optimization should not become random activity.

The objective remains:

> **Maximize the marginal utility of marketing dollars spent.**

## My Current Marketing Principles

1. Positioning comes before activation.
2. Marketing is choosing.
3. A target is useful only if it changes the message, product, channel, or economics.
4. Segmentation should reflect how customers actually make choices.
5. The competitive frame determines which differences matter.
6. A point of difference needs an end benefit.
7. Customer journeys organize tactics around behavior rather than channels.
8. Integrated marketing requires consistency without identical execution everywhere.
9. More digital addressability increases the value of prioritization.
10. More data does not automatically create more insight.
11. Product quality matters more when customers can discover and share defects faster.
12. Brand equity can come from functional performance, symbolic meaning, community, purpose, or experience.
13. Purpose is strongest when it is operational, not merely communicative.
14. Price should reinforce positioning and perceived value.
15. Promotion changes economics; advertising primarily changes meaning and demand.
16. Customer acquisition and retention are different marketing problems.
17. CLTV can discipline acquisition spending, but only if its assumptions are credible.
18. Customer insight is essential, but customers cannot always articulate breakthrough innovation in advance.
19. The strongest brand systems make the product, channel, message, and experience reinforce one another.
20. Measurement should inform strategy rather than replace it.

## Running Example: Capital One Venture X

My integrated project analyzed Capital One's attempt to move upmarket with Venture X.

The brand challenge:

> Can Capital One extend beyond its historically downmarket/subprime associations and credibly compete in premium travel rewards?

My project framed the target as a **Jet Setter**: affluent, financially savvy, high-credit, frequent travelers who understand rewards mechanics and value premium travel experiences.

The competitive frame was premium travel rewards cards. Points of difference included rewards, lounges, travel portal, and premium perks. The end benefit was not merely "more points" but **an all-purpose premium card that helps the customer maximize both economic return and travel experience**.

The project made several course concepts concrete:

- features support a positioning; they are not the positioning;
- owned customer data can make targeting more economically efficient;
- journey stages require different tactics;
- a pre-approval tool can reduce friction and improve conversion;
- brand success metrics and business profitability are not identical.

A campaign can succeed in search share, awards, influencer discussion, and awareness without proving attractive product economics.
