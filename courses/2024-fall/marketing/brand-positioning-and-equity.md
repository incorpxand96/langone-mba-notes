# Brand Positioning and Equity

The course treats positioning as foundational.

Positioning should be established before product development, website, packaging, advertising, or pitch decks.

The core elements are:

1. **Target audience**
2. **Competitive frame**
3. **Points of difference**
4. **End benefit**

## Target Audience

The target should be specific enough to influence features, pricing, tone, media, channels, and experience.

## Competitive Frame

What alternatives does the customer compare?

The competitive frame determines which attributes matter and which differences are meaningful.

## Points of Difference

A useful POD should be important, meaningfully different, credible, and durable enough to matter.

Tools include feature charts, perceptual maps, brand imagery, and research.

## End Benefit

The course repeatedly asks:

> **Why should anybody care?**

Features matter because they create a customer outcome.

## Perceptual Maps

Perceptual maps show how brands compare on attributes customers value, identify gaps, and distinguish current from desired perception.

Competitive reality is partly what customers believe.

## Brand Identity

Identity elements include name, logo, font, color, symbols, graphics, aesthetics, personality, and voice.

These elements should activate positioning; they are not substitutes for it.

## Repositioning

Repositioning may change the target, competitive frame, point of difference, or end benefit.

## Case: Dove

Dove's original positioning was functional: cleansing without drying skin.

Becoming a Masterbrand created a need for broader meaning across multiple personal-care products. The Campaign for Real Beauty moved Dove toward a broader point of view about beauty and self-perception.

That created more emotional meaning but also a greater authenticity burden.

## Case: Liquid Death

Liquid Death has modest functional product differentiation but strong symbolic differentiation through packaging, metal/punk aesthetics, voice, content, environmental positioning, and fan community.

The brand system itself creates difference.

## Worked Example: Venture X

### Target

Affluent, financially savvy travelers.

### Frame

Premium travel rewards credit cards.

### Points of Difference

Rewards, travel portal, lounge access, premium perks.

### End Benefit

Maximize both financial value and premium travel experience through one card.

A useful lesson:

> **"2X miles" is not the positioning. It is evidence supporting the positioning.**

## My Take

Positioning is a compression device. It should make downstream decisions easier by clarifying who not to target, which competitors matter, which features deserve emphasis, what price is credible, and which experiences fit.

## Rules of Thumb

- Position before activating.
- Define competition through customer choice.
- Translate features into benefits.
- Use perceptual evidence, not internal belief.
- Keep identity consistent with positioning.
- Reposition strategically, not cosmetically.
- Distinctiveness can be symbolic as well as functional.
