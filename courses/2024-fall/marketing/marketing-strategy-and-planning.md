# Marketing Strategy and Planning

Marketing is a system of managerial choices.

A marketing plan forces the organization to decide what it is trying to achieve, which customers matter most, what the brand should mean, where money should be spent, what tactics should be used, and how success will be measured.

The course frames the challenge as allocating scarce marketing resources across **infinite options**.

## Planning Process

### 1. Pre-Plan Analysis

Understand industry, competitors, historical programs, research, trends, values, channels, platforms, and SWOT.

### 2. Develop Strategy

Define objectives, source of volume, positioning, targets, customer journey, and priorities.

### 3. Allocate Budget

Allocate spending across marketing elements, target groups, geography, seasonality, and journey stage.

### 4. Build Tactics

Specify what, where, when, how, cost, and ownership.

### 5. Activate

Execution is where the plan becomes real.

### 6. Evaluate

Use sales, share, engagement, attitudes, customer behavior, channel metrics, and campaign KPIs.

### 7. Modify

Corrective action is part of the plan. Digital allows changes to occur very quickly.

## Marketing Plan Purpose

A plan is both an **economic allocation document** and an **organizational coordination mechanism**.

It should:

- allocate resources toward the highest-value messages and vehicles;
- make roles, sequence, timing, responsibilities, and expected outcomes clear.

## Sources of Volume

Growth can come from:

- new category entrants;
- competitor switching;
- greater frequency;
- larger purchases;
- trade-up;
- cross-sell;
- improved retention;
- loyalty;
- advocacy.

These imply different targets and tactics.

## Customer Acquisition vs. Retention

Acquiring and retaining customers are different problems.

A satisfied competitor user may be harder to switch than a new category entrant. Existing customers may instead offer growth through frequency, additional products, premiumization, or advocacy.

## Customer Lifetime Value

CLTV can discipline acquisition spending. It depends on assumptions around repeat rate, frequency, spend, margin, and relationship length.

Those assumptions can be uncertain. CLTV is a framework, not a fact.

## Strategy Is Not Linear

New information about customers, competition, economics, channels, or product capabilities can require revisiting prior decisions.

## My Take

The most useful planning idea is that **marketing is capital allocation under uncertainty**.

Digital makes the decision space larger. It does not remove scarcity.

That is why "marketing is choosing" becomes more important, not less.

## Rules of Thumb

- Define the business problem before the campaign.
- Identify the source of volume.
- Tie every major tactic to a customer or journey stage.
- Treat budget allocation as strategy.
- Use metrics to update choices, not merely report activity.
- Reallocate when evidence changes.
- Do not confuse activity volume with plan quality.
