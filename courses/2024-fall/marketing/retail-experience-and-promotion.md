# Retail Experience and Promotion

Marketing does not end with advertising.

Physical and digital environments can shape conversion, trial, understanding, loyalty, and brand meaning.

## Advertising vs. Promotion

The course offers a useful distinction:

> **Advertising appeals to hearts and minds. Promotion appeals to the wallet.**

Promotion changes the immediate economic proposition.

Examples include coupons, sampling, contests, rebates, and discounts.

## Experiential Retail

Retail can become a brand experience rather than just a distribution point.

L.L. Bean's outdoor experiences illustrate how the environment can let customers experience the brand's world.

## Digital Replication of In-Person Experience

Digital tools increasingly reproduce physical-retail functions:

- virtual try-on;
- chatbots;
- consultations;
- body scans;
- room visualization.

These can increase conversion and reduce returns.

## Allbirds

Allbirds began DTC, providing direct consumer relationships, behavioral data, narrative control, and margin advantages.

Physical stores later added product interaction, awareness, revenue, and convenient returns.

## My Take

Channel strategy should begin with:

> **What customer problem does this channel solve?**

Stores can solve trial, confidence, service, and experience. Digital can solve discovery, convenience, personalization, and comparison.

The strongest omnichannel systems use each for what it does best.

## Rules of Thumb

- Promotion should have a behavioral objective.
- Do not train customers to wait for discounts accidentally.
- Let experience reinforce positioning.
- Use physical and digital channels complementarily.
- Treat the store as media when it meaningfully communicates the brand.
- Reduce friction at the journey stage where it matters.
