# Marketing Organization and Agencies

Marketing responsibility is increasingly fragmented.

Organizations may include brand, media, creative, consumer insights, digital, data, AI, customer experience, growth, innovation, sustainability, and purpose roles.

Agency ecosystems are similarly specialized.

## Traditional Agency Capabilities

Examples include account management, creative, media planning, media buying, production, consumer insights, traffic, finance, and legal.

Newer capabilities include data visualization, dashboards, ROI, conversion, programmatic buying, dynamic creative optimization, and GenAI.

## Specialized Agencies

Examples include digital, influencer, SEO, PR, event, promotions, shopper, multicultural, sustainability, and mobile.

The challenge becomes integration.

## Organizational Risk

Fragmentation can create inconsistent messaging, duplicated work, unclear ownership, weak data integration, and platform-by-platform optimization without brand coherence.

## Dove: Brand Development vs. Brand Building

The Dove case illustrates a global/local split.

### Brand Development

More centralized and focused on long-term brand health, innovation, strategy, and global ideas.

### Brand Building

More local and focused on growth, profit, market execution, PR, spend, and local activation.

The structure balances consistency with local effectiveness.

## My Take

Specialization is useful because marketing has become technically broad.

But fragmentation creates an integration tax.

The more specialists a brand uses, the more valuable clear positioning and operating cadence become.

## Rules of Thumb

- Organize specialists around one strategy.
- Clarify decision rights.
- Define the owner of the customer journey.
- Protect brand consistency.
- Centralize where scale matters.
- Localize where customer or channel differences matter.
- Do not let agency specialization become strategy fragmentation.
