# Integrated Marketing Communications

Integrated marketing communications translates brand strategy into a coherent system of customer-facing activity.

Potential elements include advertising, digital, direct marketing, PR, events, experiential, consumer promotion, trade promotion, packaging, retail, naming, and pricing.

## The Big Idea

A brand should have a central strategic idea that can travel across channels.

Integration does **not** mean identical execution everywhere. Different platforms have different formats, norms, audiences, attention patterns, and strengths.

The challenge is strategic consistency with medium-specific execution.

## Paid, Owned, and Earned

### Paid

The brand pays for distribution.

### Owned

The brand controls the asset or channel.

### Earned

Others distribute or discuss the brand because the content or experience earns attention.

## Customer-Generated Communication

Reviews, fan art, user-generated ads, communities, referrals, and social sharing can create authenticity and reach while reducing direct brand control.

## Case: Dove

Dove combined billboards, Super Bowl advertising, YouTube, PR, influencer outreach, partnerships, and consumer-generated advertising around a shared brand point of view.

## Case: Liquid Death

Packaging, name, entertainment content, events, and fan behavior reinforce one another. The can itself becomes media.

## Worked Example: Venture X

### Awareness

Celebrity-led video, travel imagery, search, social, digital targeting.

### Evaluation

Website detail, reward economics, portal/perk explanation, third-party reviews.

### Purchase

Pre-approval, application flow, sign-up incentives.

### Usage / Retention

Annual benefits, lounges, travel experiences, rewards.

## My Take

Integrated marketing is less about "being everywhere" than about **making each element do a specific job in one coherent system**.

Integration is partly about exclusion.

## Rules of Thumb

- Start with positioning.
- Define the behavioral job of each element.
- Adapt to platform strengths.
- Keep strategic meaning consistent.
- Treat packaging and experience as communications.
- Use customer participation where authenticity matters.
- Do not confuse omnipresence with integration.
