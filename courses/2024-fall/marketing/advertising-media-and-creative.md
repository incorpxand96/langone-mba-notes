# Advertising, Media, and Creative

Advertising translates positioning into a message designed to influence customer perception or behavior.

## Creative Genres

Examples include:

- emotional;
- humor and satire;
- factual evidence / straight sell;
- fantasy;
- overcoming brand misperceptions;
- demonstration;
- comparison;
- testimonial;
- slice of life / business;
- empowerment or encouragement;
- promotion support;
- visual mnemonics;
- consumer generated.

The right choice depends on target, category, positioning, problem, and media context.

## Functional vs. Emotional

A brand can sell:

- what the product does;
- how the product makes the customer feel;
- what the brand says about the customer.

Emotional meaning is strongest when the product can still support the promise.

## Media Choice

Media planning asks:

- who needs to see the message;
- where they can be reached;
- what format works there;
- how much frequency is needed;
- what role the channel plays in the journey.

## The Platform Is Part of the Creative

A message for television, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, search, retail, or email should not simply be copied across formats.

## My Take

The important distinction is between **creative novelty** and **strategic creativity**.

A surprising ad is useful only when it makes the positioning more noticeable, memorable, believable, or emotionally resonant.

## Rules of Thumb

- Choose creative genre based on the problem.
- Do not use emotional creative to hide a weak product.
- Use demonstrations when proof matters.
- Use humor only when it reinforces rather than eclipses the brand.
- Design for the platform.
- Make the brand easy to remember.
- Measure the objective, not just creative awards.
