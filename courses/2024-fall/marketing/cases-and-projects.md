# Cases and Projects

The cases and integrated project turn the course frameworks into managerial choices.

## Dove — Evolution of a Brand

**Core problem:** How can a functional cleansing brand become a Masterbrand capable of spanning a broader personal-care portfolio?

**Lessons:**

- functional positioning can become strategically limiting;
- brand meaning can support portfolio expansion;
- cultural relevance can create earned media;
- purpose creates authenticity requirements;
- global brand architecture and local activation can be separated organizationally.

**Durable question:** How far can a brand stretch beyond its original functional promise before credibility weakens?

## Liquid Death — Water Made Metal

**Core problem:** How can a commodity-like product create a differentiated brand and expand beyond an initial subculture without destroying what made it distinctive?

**Lessons:**

- brand identity can create real differentiation;
- packaging can be part of product and media;
- subculture targeting can create an initial community;
- a strong brand can broaden beyond its original target;
- low-cost market testing can validate a concept before scale;
- sustainability can reinforce rather than define the brand.

**Durable question:** How can a brand scale beyond an initial tribe without becoming generic?

## Allbirds — Decarbonizing Fashion

**Core problem:** How can a small footwear company build differentiation around comfort, natural materials, design, and climate impact while influencing the wider industry?

**Lessons:**

- sustainability can be embedded in product innovation;
- DTC can improve data and narrative control;
- stores can complement digital;
- purpose can be operational;
- sequencing of communication matters;
- customers may first want the product, then learn the purpose;
- sharing capabilities with competitors can support ecosystem change.

**Durable question:** Should purpose lead the proposition, or should product desire earn the right to tell the purpose story?

## elBulli — The Taste of Innovation

**Core problem:** How can an organization systematize radical creativity without simply responding to explicit customer demand?

**Lessons:**

- breakthrough innovation may be vision-led;
- creativity can be organized;
- experimentation can be systematic;
- operational feasibility constrains innovation;
- experience design can create extraordinary demand;
- scarcity can be part of the experience.

**Durable question:** When should an innovator follow customer insight, and when should it create something customers could not have requested?

## Capital One Venture X — Integrated Marketing Project

This was my individual course project and is therefore the most useful source for my own applied commentary.

### Brand Challenge

Can Capital One move beyond its historically downmarket associations and credibly compete in premium travel cards?

### Positioning

**Target:** "Jet Setter" — affluent, frequent traveler, high credit, financially literate, interested in premium experiences.

**Competitive frame:** Premium travel rewards cards.

**Points of difference:** Rewards, travel portal, lounges, premium perks.

**End benefit:** Strong economic return plus elevated travel experience.

### Journey Activation

**Awareness:** search, social, video, celebrity/travel creative.

**Evaluation:** detailed website, comparisons, perks, reward economics.

**Purchase:** pre-approval and application.

**Retention / Growth:** annual bonuses, travel experiences, rewards, cross-sell.

### Measurement

Useful brand-level indicators included search share, reviews, influencer commentary, awards, and visibility.

But the project raised an important economic question:

> Does strong marketing performance translate into sufficient incremental profit to justify the premium product?

That connects marketing metrics back to business economics.

### My Recommendation

Move from launch-stage acquisition intensity toward more sustainable growth by reducing expensive acquisition where appropriate, cross-selling existing high-value Capital One customers, using proprietary data to identify high-propensity prospects, and maintaining premium travel experiences that reinforce the positioning.
