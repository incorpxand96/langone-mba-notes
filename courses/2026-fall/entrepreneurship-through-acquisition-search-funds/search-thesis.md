# Developing a Search Thesis: Analytics & Measurement Services

## Thesis in One Sentence

My goal is to acquire a **tech-enabled analytics services company** that provides recurring, mission-critical measurement, data integration, and decision-support services to mid-market businesses.

The ideal target combines the durability and cash-flow profile of a strong ETA business with a domain in which I have a genuine operating and analytical edge.

## Why This Thesis Fits Me

My background makes analytics and measurement services a natural place to search.

- I have extensive experience applying analytics in a digital and highly regulated environment, including experimentation, model building, valuations, portfolio monitoring, cross-functional problem solving, and quantitative decision-making.
- My experience spans the full funnel: ideation, product development, growth, and portfolio refinement.
- I have used Python, R, Stata, SQL, and spreadsheets, but I view analytics as a means to an operating decision rather than an end in itself. My preference is to move from modeling and diagnosis to recommendation and execution.
- Before Capital One, I gained exposure to industries including agriculture, healthcare, biotechnology, real estate, energy, insurance, and intellectual-property litigation.
- I also have direct ETA exposure from diligence on a seven-figure marina acquisition, including financial modeling, operating analysis, valuation and cap-rate work, and interaction with the broker and owner-operator.
- Most importantly, I am interested in **acquiring and growing a business**, rather than making a short-term financial trade.

That last distinction matters. The search thesis should identify businesses I would actually want to operate for years, not merely assets that screen well on paper.

## The Search Box

### Revenue Quality

I will prioritize **recurring or highly repeatable revenue**, with a preference for businesses where at least **50% of revenue** comes from contractual, retainer, or managed-services relationships.

Strong targets should also demonstrate:

- high historical customer retention;
- repeat usage that is embedded in customer workflows; and
- limited dependence on continually winning one-off consulting projects.

The core question is not simply whether customers have paid repeatedly in the past, but whether there is a structural reason they are likely to continue doing so.

### Mission-Critical Analytics

I am more interested in analytics that supports an ongoing business process than in commodity reporting or discretionary project work.

Potential areas include:

- customer and marketing measurement;
- experimentation and attribution;
- data integration;
- business-intelligence infrastructure;
- pricing and revenue analytics; and
- outsourced analytics operations.

The most attractive businesses should sit close enough to a customer's operating decisions that replacing the provider is costly, disruptive, or unattractive.

### Defensibility

I will favor companies serving mid-market customers where the provider develops **difficult-to-replicate knowledge of the customer's data architecture, business processes, or domain**.

The desired moat does not necessarily need to be proprietary software. It may instead come from a combination of:

- embedded workflows;
- integrations;
- institutional knowledge;
- domain expertise;
- customer relationships; and
- a track record of reliable execution.

This is especially important in a services business, where a generic capability can otherwise become commoditized.

### Financial Profile

My initial search parameters are approximately:

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | $3M–$15M |
| EBITDA | $750K–$3M |
| Revenue mix | Ideally 50%+ recurring / contractual / managed services |
| Customer base | Diversified |
| Capital intensity | Modest |
| Founder dependence | Limited or reducible |
| Geography | Tri-state area or distributed/remote |

I am willing to look slightly below the conventional ETA EBITDA range when the underlying business quality, recurring revenue, and growth opportunity are compelling.

### Geography

I will prioritize businesses in **New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut**, as well as companies with distributed workforces and nationally distributed customers.

This matters both personally and strategically. A distributed analytics-services model can widen the search universe without requiring relocation and can make geographic proximity to any single customer less important.

## Why Analytics Services Can Fit ETA

The ETA framework favors businesses with durable cash flow, recurring or repeat revenue, limited customer concentration, attractive margins, manageable capital requirements, and a defensible position.

Analytics services can meet those criteria, but only when the business is structured correctly.

A recurring managed-service analytics provider is meaningfully different from a project-based consultancy. The former may have embedded customer relationships, predictable revenue, and operating leverage. The latter may require the owner to continually sell the next engagement.

For that reason, I would rather buy an analytics business with moderate growth and durable customer relationships than a faster-growing firm whose revenue must be rebuilt every year.

## The Alternative Thesis: Marinas

I initially considered marinas as another search thesis.

The attraction is intuitive: a well-located marina can possess elements of a **natural local monopoly** because waterfront supply is physically constrained and new development can be difficult. My prior diligence experience also gives me some familiarity with marina economics and acquisition underwriting.

However, analytics is currently the stronger fit for me.

The primary reasons are:

1. **Operator-market fit.** My professional experience gives me a much deeper base of knowledge in analytics than in marina operations.
2. **Geographic flexibility.** An analytics business can potentially be operated from the tri-state area or through a distributed model, while a marina generally requires close physical involvement with a specific asset.
3. **Value-creation fit.** I have clearer ideas about how I could improve sales, measurement, productization, analytics delivery, and operating processes in an analytics business.
4. **Long-term interest.** Analytics is closer to the work I have already chosen to build expertise in and is therefore more likely to sustain my interest as an owner-operator.

Marinas remain an interesting ETA category, but a good search thesis should reflect not only the attractiveness of an industry but also the searcher's comparative advantage.

## What Would Make Me Reject a Target Quickly?

A thesis is useful partly because it defines what **not** to pursue.

I would be cautious about businesses with:

- predominantly one-off project revenue;
- extreme dependence on the founder for selling or delivery;
- significant customer concentration;
- analytics work that is easily commoditized;
- weak evidence of customer retention;
- low switching costs with no compensating domain expertise;
- a business model likely to require substantial ongoing capital investment; or
- economics that depend on aggressive add-backs rather than demonstrated cash generation.

The purpose of the search box is not to eliminate judgment. It is to preserve time for opportunities that deserve deeper judgment.

## Questions I Still Need to Answer

Several questions remain central to refining the thesis:

- How much recurring revenue is enough if customer retention is exceptionally strong?
- Which analytics niches are most resistant to commoditization and automation?
- How should I distinguish a genuinely embedded provider from a consulting firm that simply has repeat clients?
- How much founder dependence is acceptable if the founder is willing to support a transition?
- Which verticals have enough fragmentation to support a proprietary search?
- Where is specialized domain knowledge most valuable to customers?
- Can a target expand margins through standardization or productization without losing the service quality that makes it valuable?
- What customer-concentration threshold should become a hard screen rather than a diligence question?

These are not reasons to avoid the thesis. They are the issues the search process should help resolve.

## Current Thesis

My current preference is to search for a **small, profitable, tech-enabled analytics and measurement services company with meaningful recurring revenue, embedded customer relationships, modest capital requirements, and limited founder dependence**.

The ideal company would be large enough to support an ETA acquisition but small enough that professionalizing operations, sharpening go-to-market strategy, improving analytics delivery, and selectively productizing services could create meaningful value.

The thesis will evolve as I see real businesses. The goal is not to prove the initial thesis correct; it is to use the thesis as a disciplined starting point and update it as evidence accumulates.
