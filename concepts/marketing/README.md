# Marketing

This section is my cross-course knowledge base for **how organizations choose customers, create differentiated value, communicate that value, and influence behavior profitably**.

My working definition:

> **Marketing is the discipline of choosing which customers and behaviors matter, defining a differentiated value proposition for them, and allocating resources to move those customers through a relationship with the brand.**

## Cross-Cutting Ideas

### Strategy Before Activity

Channels and creative are downstream choices.

The upstream questions are:

- who;
- relative to what alternatives;
- why this brand;
- why it matters.

### Marketing Is Choosing

More platforms and more data create more possible actions. Attention, money, and organizational capacity remain finite.

### Positioning Is a System Constraint

A strong positioning should influence product, price, communications, channel, and experience.

### Customers Move Through Relationships

Marketing includes acquisition, evaluation, conversion, retention, growth, and advocacy.

### Measurement Is Not Strategy

Metrics are evidence. They do not decide which business problem matters.

### Brand Meaning Can Be an Asset

Differentiation can come from functional performance, symbolic meaning, identity, community, purpose, and experience.

### Customer Insight Has Limits

Customers can explain current problems more easily than they can specify radically new possibilities.

Marketing should understand customers without assuming innovation must be consumer-authored.
