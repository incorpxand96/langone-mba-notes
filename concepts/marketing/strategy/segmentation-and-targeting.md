# Segmentation and Targeting

## Definition

Segmentation divides a market into meaningfully different customer groups.

Targeting decides which of those groups deserve distinct investment.

## Why It Matters

Not every observable difference between customers deserves a marketing segment.

A segment is useful when it changes product, value proposition, message, channel, price, service, or budget.

## Framework

Potential segmentation dimensions:

- demographics;
- psychographics;
- behavior;
- life stage;
- journey stage;
- interests;
- frequency;
- category experience.

Then ask:

1. Is the segment identifiable?
2. Is it economically meaningful?
3. Does it respond differently?
4. Can I reach it?
5. Can I serve it differently?

## How I Think About It

Microtargeting creates a temptation to confuse **addressability** with **strategy**.

Just because I can target a tiny group does not mean it deserves its own strategy.

The useful number of segments is the number that improves decisions without fragmenting the brand and operating model.

## Rules of Thumb

- Segment for action.
- Avoid decorative personas.
- Use behavior where it predicts value.
- Balance current and future customers.
- Do not let personalization erase a coherent brand.
- Allocate budget, not just labels.
