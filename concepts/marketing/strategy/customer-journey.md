# Customer Journey

## Definition

The customer journey is the full experience across touchpoints from initial awareness through acquisition, use, retention, repurchase, advocacy, and potentially disposal.

## Why It Matters

The journey converts marketing from a channel list into a behavior system.

The core question becomes:

> **What behavior should happen next?**

## Framework

Possible stages:

- awareness;
- familiarity;
- consideration;
- evaluation;
- trial;
- purchase;
- onboarding;
- use;
- retention;
- loyalty;
- repurchase;
- advocacy.

Each stage needs:

- customer barrier;
- desired behavior;
- message;
- channel;
- metric.

## How I Think About It

The journey changes the sequence from:

> "Which channels should we use?"

to:

> **"Which customer needs to do what next?"**

Only then choose the channel.

## Example: Venture X

Awareness: travel-focused media.

Evaluation: reward and perk information.

Purchase: pre-approval and application.

Retention: ongoing rewards and premium experiences.

Cross-sell: existing Capital One data can identify high-propensity customers.

## Rules of Thumb

- Define the next behavior.
- Match metric to stage.
- Identify friction.
- Treat retention as marketing.
- Design owned channels deliberately.
- Use advocacy when customers can credibly carry the message.
