# Positioning

## Definition

Positioning defines the place a brand should occupy in a target customer's mind relative to alternatives.

A reusable structure:

1. **Target**
2. **Competitive frame**
3. **Point of difference**
4. **End benefit**

## Why It Matters

Positioning disciplines downstream decisions.

It should affect product, price, messaging, media, design, channel, and experience.

Without positioning, tactics become disconnected activity.

## Framework

### Target

Who is the priority customer?

### Competitive Frame

What alternatives does that customer consider?

### Point of Difference

What meaningful advantage does the brand offer?

### End Benefit

Why does that advantage matter in the customer's life?

## How I Think About It

A feature is not a positioning.

A slogan is not a positioning.

A persona is not a positioning.

Positioning is the **strategic relationship among customer, competition, difference, and benefit**.

The strongest test is:

> **Would this positioning cause me to make different decisions?**

If not, it is probably too generic.

## Rules of Thumb

- Define competition from the customer's perspective.
- Translate features into outcomes.
- Make trade-offs visible.
- Use perceptual evidence.
- Let price and experience reinforce the position.
- Reposition strategically, not cosmetically.

## Example: Venture X

Target: affluent, financially savvy travelers.

Frame: premium travel cards.

Difference: strong rewards plus premium travel ecosystem.

Benefit: maximize the economic and experiential value of frequent travel.

The 2X reward rate supports the positioning. It is not the positioning itself.
