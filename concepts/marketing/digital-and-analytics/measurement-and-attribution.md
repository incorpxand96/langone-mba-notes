# Measurement and Attribution

## Definition

Marketing measurement asks whether an activity changed the customer or business outcome it was intended to change.

Attribution asks how much credit should be assigned to each marketing element.

## Why It Matters

Modern journeys contain search, social, influencers, websites, stores, email, word of mouth, and paid media.

More observable touchpoints do not automatically mean more causal certainty.

## Metric Hierarchy

### Activity

Impressions, posts, content.

### Engagement

Clicks, likes, comments, shares.

### Journey Behavior

Awareness, consideration, application, conversion, repeat.

### Business

Revenue, margin, CLTV, share, profit.

The closer the metric is to the business outcome, the more economically useful it becomes, but causal isolation may become harder.

## How I Think About It

The useful question is not:

> "Did the metric improve?"

It is:

> **"What changed because of the marketing?"**

This is why experiments and causal designs are valuable where feasible.

## Example: Venture X

Search interest and industry discussion demonstrated strong market presence.

But those metrics do not prove the product created enough profit to justify acquisition cost, bonuses, rewards, and premium experiences.

Marketing measurement should eventually connect to product economics.

## Rules of Thumb

- Match metric to objective.
- Separate activity from outcomes.
- Use experiments where possible.
- Treat attribution models as models.
- Measure incrementality.
- Connect brand metrics to economics over time.
- Beware vanity metrics.
