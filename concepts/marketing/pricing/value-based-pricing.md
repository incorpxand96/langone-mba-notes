# Value-Based Pricing

## Definition

Value-based pricing sets price primarily around the value customers perceive relative to alternatives rather than starting only from seller cost.

## Why It Matters

Cost determines whether a price is economically sustainable.

Customer value determines whether the price is commercially sustainable.

Competitors help define the relevant comparison.

## Framework

Think of price between two bounds.

### Floor

Cost and required economics.

### Ceiling

Customer willingness to pay based on perceived value.

### Strategic Position

Within that range, consider brand positioning, competition, objectives, product line, elasticity, and reference prices.

## Whole Product Value

In technology, value may include core functionality, support, integration, ecosystem, customer success, and complementary products.

Customers price the solution they receive, not the engineering boundary of the core product.

## How I Think About It

Price is not only extraction.

It is also communication.

A premium price can support quality, exclusivity, and confidence.

A low price can support accessibility, penetration, or value.

The wrong price can undermine the intended positioning.

## Rules of Thumb

- Start from customer alternatives.
- Quantify value where possible.
- Know the cost floor.
- Use competitor prices as context, not destiny.
- Align price with positioning.
- Consider discount reference effects.
- Price the whole solution.
