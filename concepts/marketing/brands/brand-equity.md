# Brand Equity

## Definition

Brand equity is the incremental value created because customers know, recognize, trust, identify with, or prefer a brand.

That value can arise from awareness, associations, perceived quality, trust, identity, community, and loyalty.

## Why It Matters

Brand equity can influence willingness to pay, consideration, retention, expansion, word of mouth, distribution, and resilience.

## How Brand Equity Is Built

### Functional

Product performs better.

### Symbolic

Brand communicates identity or meaning.

### Experiential

Interactions are distinctive.

### Social

Community and other customers reinforce the brand.

### Purpose

Brand stands for something customers value.

## Example: Liquid Death

Water is difficult to differentiate functionally.

Liquid Death creates equity through name, packaging, voice, aesthetics, irreverent content, environmental story, and fandom.

That is a useful reminder that brand equity can be a **system of reinforcing signals**.

## Example: Dove

Dove expanded brand meaning beyond moisturizing into a broader point of view about beauty.

That creates emotional reach but increases the credibility burden.

## How I Think About It

Brand equity is strongest when the brand makes choice easier.

The customer knows what to expect, why it is different, and what it says about them.

## Rules of Thumb

- Distinctiveness matters.
- Product performance remains foundational.
- Consistency compounds.
- Community can deepen equity.
- Brand meaning should support commercial strategy.
- The broader the promise, the greater the credibility burden.
