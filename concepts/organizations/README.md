# Negotiation

Negotiation is how parties with partially conflicting interests attempt to reach a voluntary agreement.

The durable concepts include:

- alternatives;
- reservation points;
- aspirations;
- anchors;
- information;
- value creation;
- value claiming;
- concessions;
- coalitions; and
- process design.

See [Negotiation](negotiation/).

# Collaboration

Collaboration is not simply being agreeable.

It is the process of coordinating across differences to produce an outcome that individuals could not produce as effectively alone.

The durable concepts include:

- interests vs. positions;
- trust;
- information sharing;
- trade-offs;
- relationship value;
- conflict styles; and
- joint problem solving.

See [Collaboration](collaboration/).

# Cross-Cutting Ideas

## Coordination Requires Information

People cannot trade intelligently across priorities they do not understand.

But complete transparency is not always optimal.

Effective coordination depends on knowing which information must be shared and which information should remain private.

## Conflict Is Not Automatically Dysfunctional

Conflict can reveal:

- incompatible goals;
- hidden constraints;
- alternative ideas;
- differences in forecasts;
- distributional issues.

The managerial problem is not to eliminate disagreement.

It is to channel disagreement into better decisions without destroying relationships or execution.

## Relationships Are Economic Assets

Trust can reduce:

- verification costs;
- bargaining friction;
- defensive behavior;
- need for formal control.

But relationship orientation can also create over-accommodation and weak value claiming.

## Process Shapes Outcomes

The rules of interaction matter:

- who speaks;
- who votes;
- who can veto;
- whether votes are public;
- whether parties can caucus;
- how information flows;
- how concessions are marked.

Process is not separate from substance.

## Fairness Is Both Social and Strategic

People respond to perceived fairness in:

- allocation;
- procedure;
- treatment.

A deal that looks economically rational can fail when the process feels illegitimate.

# My Current View

Organizations function through a constant tension between:

- **individual interests** and **collective value**;
- **competition** and **cooperation**;
- **formal authority** and **informal influence**;
- **economic logic** and **human emotion**.

Good management does not eliminate those tensions.

It designs structures and behaviors that make them productive.
