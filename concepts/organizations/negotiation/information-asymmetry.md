# Information Asymmetry in Negotiation

## Definition

Information asymmetry exists when the parties possess different relevant information.

The asymmetry may concern:

- value;
- quality;
- alternatives;
- urgency;
- forecasts;
- constraints;
- preferences.

---

## Why It Matters

The party with better information may be able to:

- claim more value;
- screen offers;
- selectively accept transactions favorable to itself.

That means the other party's behavior can reveal information.

---

## Framework

Ask:

1. What do I know?
2. What do they know?
3. What do I think they know?
4. What does their willingness to transact reveal?
5. Can uncertainty be verified later?
6. Can I design a contingent term around it?

---

## Adverse Selection

A buyer should be cautious when the seller knows quality better and can decide whether to accept.

Acceptance may be bad news.

The key is to evaluate the asset **conditional on acceptance**, not using an unconditional average.

---

## How I Think About It

Information is not simply an advantage to hoard.

Sometimes information must be shared to create value.

The discipline is to distinguish:

- information about **priorities**, which can enable trades;
- information about **reservation points**, which can surrender leverage;
- information about **quality**, which may need verification or contingent pricing.

---

## Rules of Thumb

- Treat acceptance as data.
- Ask how the counterparty was selected.
- Verify private quality claims.
- Use contingent terms when future outcomes are observable.
- Reveal priorities selectively.
- Protect bottom lines.
- Think about second-order beliefs.

---

## Failure Modes

- Using unconditional expected value.
- Assuming synergies eliminate adverse selection.
- Over-sharing to build trust.
- Under-sharing and making integrative bargaining impossible.
