# BATNA, Reservation Price, and ZOPA

## Definition

These three concepts form the economic foundation of a negotiation.

### BATNA

**Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement**.

What I will do if this negotiation fails.

### Reservation Price

The worst outcome I am willing to accept before choosing the BATNA.

### ZOPA

**Zone of Possible Agreement**.

The range in which both parties prefer agreement to their alternatives.

---

## Why It Matters

Without these concepts, negotiators can mistake:

- agreement for success;
- movement for progress;
- pressure for economic necessity.

A deal below my reservation point is worse than no deal.

---

## Framework

### Step 1 — Identify the Actual Alternative

Do not use vague language such as:

> "I have other options."

Specify the best one.

### Step 2 — Value It

Translate the alternative into the same terms as the current negotiation where possible.

### Step 3 — Adjust for Deal-Specific Factors

Consider:

- switching cost;
- timing;
- risk;
- non-financial preferences;
- relationship value.

### Step 4 — Set Reservation Point

Write it down before negotiating.

### Step 5 — Set Aspiration Separately

The reservation point is the limit.

The aspiration is the goal.

### Step 6 — Estimate the Other Side

The likely overlap between reservation points determines whether a positive ZOPA exists.

---

## How I Think About It

BATNA is the most important protection against negotiating with myself.

It gives an external answer to:

> **Should I keep moving?**

The stronger my alternatives, the easier it is to reject a bad agreement.

The weaker my alternatives, the more important it is not to let urgency become visible desperation.

---

## Rules of Thumb

- Strengthen BATNA before negotiating.
- Never fabricate a BATNA.
- Do not reveal a weak BATNA casually.
- Do not reveal the reservation point.
- Set aspiration independently.
- Update a reservation point only when new facts change the economics.
- Take a break before crossing it.

---

## Failure Modes

### No Explicit BATNA

Treating "I need this deal" as analysis.

### Reservation Point Drift

Changing the bottom line simply because negotiations have lasted a long time.

### Aspiration Collapse

Turning the reservation point into the target.

### False ZOPA

Assuming overlap exists because both parties are still talking.
