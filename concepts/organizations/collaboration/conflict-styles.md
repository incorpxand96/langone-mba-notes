# Conflict Styles

## Definition

Conflict-management style is the behavioral approach a person uses when substantive interests and relationships are in tension.

Five recurring approaches are:

- competing;
- collaborating;
- compromising;
- avoiding;
- accommodating.

---

## Why It Matters

The main risk is not having a style.

Everyone has defaults.

The risk is using the same default regardless of:

- stakes;
- relationship;
- urgency;
- repetition;
- power;
- issue structure.

---

## Framework

Evaluate three categories of goals.

### Substantive

How important is the issue?

### Relational

How important is the relationship?

### Temporal

Is the interaction:

- one-shot or repeated?
- urgent or patient?
- short-term or long-term?

Then choose behavior.

---

## The Five Styles

### Competing

High assertion.

Useful for protecting important interests.

Risk: trust and creativity.

### Collaborating

High problem solving and relationship investment.

Useful when both stakes and future relationship matter.

Risk: time and exploitation.

### Compromising

Middle ground.

Useful for speed.

Risk: leaving value uncreated.

### Avoiding

Delay or disengagement.

Useful for timing, cooling emotion, or trivial issues.

Risk: unresolved problems.

### Accommodating

Yielding on substance to preserve relationship or achieve a larger strategic goal.

Risk: loss of influence and repeated exploitation.

---

## How I Think About It

A good manager should have **range**.

The goal is not to become maximally collaborative.

It is to become capable of collaborating, competing, accommodating, compromising, or avoiding **on purpose**.

---

## Rules of Thumb

- High stakes do not always mean compete.
- High relationship value does not always mean accommodate.
- Collaboration is expensive; use it where the value justifies the effort.
- Avoidance can be a strategy.
- Compromise is fast, not necessarily optimal.
- Practice the style that feels least natural.
