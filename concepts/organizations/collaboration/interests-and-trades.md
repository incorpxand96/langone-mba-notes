# Interests and Trades

## Definition

A **position** is a proposed outcome.

An **interest** is the underlying reason that outcome matters.

Collaboration improves when people can move from:

> "I need X"

to:

> "Here is the problem X solves for me."

---

## Why It Matters

Two positions can appear incompatible while the underlying interests are not.

Understanding interests reveals:

- alternative solutions;
- differently valued issues;
- constraints;
- opportunities for trade.

---

## Framework

When conflict appears stuck:

1. State the positions.
2. Ask why each position matters.
3. Identify constraints.
4. Identify issues that differ in importance.
5. Add possible side issues.
6. Build packages.
7. Test them with hypothetical questions.

---

## Trading on Differences

Collaboration creates value when people differ in:

- priorities;
- time horizon;
- risk tolerance;
- forecasts;
- costs;
- capabilities.

Difference is not merely a source of conflict.

It is often the source of the trade.

---

## How I Think About It

The managerial instinct to "split the difference" is often too fast.

Compromise can be useful, but it assumes the problem is distributive.

The better first question is:

> **Is there a trade that makes both sides better off than the compromise?**

---

## Rules of Thumb

- Ask why.
- Share priorities, not necessarily bottom lines.
- Use package proposals.
- Trade across issues.
- Do not assume equal valuation.
- Use "what if?" questions.
- Continue searching after tentative agreement.

---

## Failure Modes

- Treating stated demands as true interests.
- Negotiating one issue at a time.
- Sharing nothing.
- Sharing everything.
- Compromising before exploring alternatives.
