# Collaboration

Collaboration is the coordinated pursuit of an outcome that depends on **information, resources, or capabilities distributed across people**.

It is not the same as harmony.

Healthy collaboration can include substantial disagreement.

The important question is whether disagreement produces:

- better information;
- better options;
- better decisions; and
- stronger execution

rather than defensiveness, delay, or relationship damage.

# A Collaboration Sequence

## 1. Understand Interests

What is each person actually trying to accomplish?

## 2. Improve Information

What do people know, and what are they assuming?

## 3. Diagnose Conflict

Is the disagreement:

- substantive;
- informational;
- relational;
- structural; or
- political?

## 4. Choose a Process

Should we:

- collaborate;
- compete;
- compromise;
- pause;
- accommodate;
- escalate to a decision-maker?

## 5. Create Trades

Where do priorities differ enough to produce a better joint outcome?

## 6. Communicate for Interpretation

Do not stop at:

> "What did I say?"

Ask:

> "What did they reasonably hear?"

## 7. Decide and Execute

Healthy collaboration does not require permanent consensus.

At some point, the organization needs a decision, clear ownership, and commitment to execution.

# My Current View

Effective collaboration requires balancing two risks.

### Too Little Cooperation

People:

- hide information;
- defend positions;
- duplicate work;
- compete internally;
- miss synergies.

### Too Much Accommodation

People:

- avoid necessary conflict;
- compromise prematurely;
- fail to advocate;
- create superficial consensus.

Good collaboration is therefore **high-information and high-accountability**, not merely agreeable.

Conflict is not the opposite of collaboration.

Handled well, conflict is one of the mechanisms through which collaboration becomes useful.
