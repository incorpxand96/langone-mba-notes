# Plain English

## Definition

Plain English is writing designed to be understood on the first reading.

Useful characteristics include:

- everyday words;
- short sentences;
- active voice;
- personal pronouns;
- logical organization;
- readable design.

---

## Why It Matters

Business writing competes with:

- time pressure;
- attention scarcity;
- email volume;
- specialized jargon.

Clarity saves the reader time.

---

## Techniques

### Prefer Simpler Words

Use:

- use, not utilize;
- because, not due to the fact that;
- today, not presently.

### Convert Nouns Back Into Verbs

- perform an analysis -> analyze;
- make a recommendation -> recommend;
- reach a conclusion -> conclude.

### Prefer Active Voice

Active voice can clarify:

- who acted;
- who owns the decision;
- what happened.

### Use Short Sentences and Paragraphs

Reduce cognitive burden.

### Use Lists and Headings

Make structure visible.

---

## Passive Voice

Passive voice is not forbidden.

It can be useful when:

- actor is unknown;
- actor is unimportant;
- action matters more than actor.

The issue is intentional use, not a blanket ban.

---

## How I Think About It

Plain language does not require shallow thinking.

In many cases, sophisticated thinking increases the obligation to explain clearly.

---

## Rules of Thumb

- Prefer concrete words.
- Cut unnecessary phrases.
- Use verbs.
- Keep sentences short.
- Use active voice by default.
- Use jargon only when it saves the audience effort rather than creating it.
- Design for the first reading.
