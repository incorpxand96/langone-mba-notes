# Audience, Intent, Message

## Definition

A reusable communication framework:

- **Audience** - who must engage?
- **Intent** - what result should occur?
- **Message** - what idea should remain?

This is the strategic core of a communication before format, wording, or delivery.

---

## Why It Matters

A communicator can be:

- accurate;
- polished;
- articulate;

and still fail if the audience does not:

- understand relevance;
- know what matters;
- know what to do.

---

## Framework

### Audience

Ask:

- Who are they?
- What do they know?
- What do they expect?
- What do they care about?
- What resistance might exist?
- How do they benefit?

### Intent

Write the desired result as an action or observable outcome.

Examples:

- approve the recommendation;
- remember three risks;
- send feedback by Friday;
- change a behavior.

### Message

State the Big Idea in one sentence.

The message should answer:

> Why should this audience care?

---

## How I Think About It

The framework changes the drafting question from:

> "What do I want to say?"

to:

> **"What does this audience need to understand in order to produce the result I want?"**

That is one of the most transferable lessons in the course.

---

## Rules of Thumb

- Audience before content.
- Intent should be measurable where possible.
- Message should fit in one sentence.
- Remove material that does not serve the intent.
- Adapt examples and evidence to audience knowledge.
- End where the intent points.
