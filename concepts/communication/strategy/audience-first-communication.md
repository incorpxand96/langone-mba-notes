# Audience-First Communication

## Definition

Audience-first communication organizes information around the listener or reader's needs rather than the communicator's internal thought process.

---

## Why It Matters

Experts often communicate in the order they learned or analyzed a problem.

Audiences usually need a different order:

1. Why this matters.
2. What the conclusion is.
3. What supports it.
4. What happens next.

---

## Framework

Ask:

- What does the audience already know?
- What do they need first?
- What questions will arise?
- Which evidence will be credible?
- What terminology will create friction?
- What should happen after the communication?

---

## Reader's / Listener's Logic

A useful sequence:

- main point;
- preview;
- supporting evidence;
- implications;
- next step.

This often differs from the chronology of the analysis.

---

## How I Think About It

Audience-first communication is a form of empathy with operational consequences.

It does not mean saying whatever the audience wants to hear.

It means making the actual idea **usable** by the audience.

---

## Rules of Thumb

- Lead with relevance.
- Anticipate questions.
- Use familiar language where possible.
- Explain unfamiliar concepts only when needed.
- Show the path through the message.
- Make the desired action explicit.
