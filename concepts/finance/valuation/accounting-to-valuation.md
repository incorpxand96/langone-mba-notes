# Accounting to Valuation

Accounting and finance are complementary.

## Accounting Provides

- operating revenue and expense;
- balance-sheet investment;
- working-capital timing;
- capital expenditure;
- debt/equity structure;
- cash-flow reconciliation.

## Finance Adds

- forward expectations;
- timing;
- risk;
- opportunity cost of capital;
- present value.

## The Bridge

```text
Revenue
   ↓
Operating profit
   ↓
Taxes
   ↓
Working-capital investment
   ↓
Capital expenditure
   ↓
Free cash flow
   ↓
Discount for timing and risk
   ↓
Enterprise value
```

## ROIC vs. Required Return

Accounting measures:

`ROIC`

Finance supplies:

`Opportunity Cost of Capital`

The value-creation question is:

> **Does the enterprise earn returns on incremental invested capital above the return investors require for comparable risk?**

## Growth

Growth is not automatically valuable.

Growth tends to create value when:

`Return on Incremental Capital > Required Return`

Growth can destroy value when additional investment earns below the opportunity cost of capital.

## Multiples

Accounting produces the denominator:

- earnings;
- book value;
- EBITDA;
- sales.

Finance explains the multiple through:

- growth;
- risk;
- reinvestment;
- required return.

## Principle

> **Accounting describes the economic engine; valuation prices the future output of that engine.**
